# Cubic primitive-cost jumps at a forced local quotient

Choosing the next ideal quotient cannot ensure a quadratic increment of
primitive wall cost. Over every field of characteristic different from
two, a local Gorenstein family below has a **unique** ideal quotient of
one smaller length, and that step has cubic primitive-cost jumps of both
signs along infinite parameter sequences. The complete fans and actual
edge lengths are also explicit. Their actual edge-length difference is
only quadratic.

This strengthens the [earlier arbitrary-step obstruction](ideal_step_incidence.md):
there is no alternative first quotient in these examples. It does not
refute a cubic total budget for a suitably chosen whole chain, nor the
unrestricted final-fan budget. Both fans have only quadratic size. The
construction and proof use elementary local algebra, the established
planar inverse-curve fan, and one-dimensional ideal deletion. Exact
priority is unassessed; this is supporting evidence, not completion of
the extremal goal or a separate paper claim.

## An explicit local algebra with two branches

Fix \(N\ge2\) and any field \(K\). Put
\[
 H_N=K[x,y,z]/(x^N,yz,(1+x)y^2+xy,(1+x)z^2+xz).
                                                               \tag{FS1}
\]
In \(R=K[x]/x^N\), write \(u=(1+x)^{-1}\). Monic reduction gives
\[
 H_N=R\oplus Ry\oplus Rz,\qquad
 y^2=-xuy,\quad z^2=-xuz,\quad yz=0.                    \tag{FS2}
\]
These formulas define associative commuting multiplication on the
displayed free \(R\)-module, proving independence as well as spanning.
Thus \(\dim H_N=3N\). All three variables are nilpotent, so this
algebra is local, with residue field \(K\). Its socle is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{soc}H_N=Kv_y\oplus Kv_z,
 \qquad v_y=x^{N-1}y,\quad v_z=x^{N-1}z.               \tag{FS3}
\]
Indeed multiplication by \(x\) first restricts each of the three
components in (FS2) to its top coefficient. Multiplication by \(y,z\)
then kills the remaining possible plain \(x^{N-1}\) component.

Compare the actual ideal quotients
\[
 A_N=H_N/(v_y-v_z),\qquad C_N=H_N/(v_y,v_z),
 \quad \dim A_N=3N-1,\quad\dim C_N=3N-2.               \tag{FS4}
\]
The common image \(s_N\) of \(v_y,v_z\) spans the kernel of
\(A_N\to C_N\).

If \(\operatorname{char}K\ne2\), then
\[
                         \operatorname{soc}A_N=Ks_N.  \tag{FS5}
\]
Here is a direct check, which also locates the characteristic exception.
Lift a socle class to \(a(x)+yb(x)+zc(x)\) in (FS2).
Its products by \(y,z\) must be multiples of \(v_y-v_z\).
Since those products occupy separate branch summands, both vanish
already in \(H_N\). Hence
\(a=xub=xuc\pmod{x^N}\).
Its product by \(x\) lies in \(K(v_y-v_z)\), so
\[
 xa=0,\quad xb=\lambda x^{N-1},\quad
 xc=-\lambda x^{N-1}.
\]
Write \(a=\alpha x^{N-1}\). The two preceding equations with
\(u^{-1}=1+x\) imply \(\lambda=\alpha=-\lambda\).
In characteristic different from two, \(a=\lambda=0\);
then \(b,c\) are top coefficients and their classes span \(Ks_N\).
This proves (FS5). In characteristic two the extra solution is
nonzero modulo \(K(v_y-v_z)\); the socle has dimension two.
Thus the uniqueness conclusion is not asserted there.

The classical Artinian local criterion identifies (FS5) as the
Gorenstein property; it is the dimension-zero case of
[Lindo--Pande, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06491v2),
where \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(K,A)=\operatorname{soc}A\).
More directly, any one-dimensional ideal in a
local algebra generated by nilpotent coordinates is killed by those
coordinates: each acts by a nilpotent scalar. Thus every such ideal
is a socle line. Equation (FS5) proves that \(A_N\to C_N\) is
the unique possible one-dimensional ideal quotient, even after
extending the field.

## The entire positive fan

The planar branch module \(Ry\), with cyclic generator \(y\), is
the quotient
\[
 T_N=K[x,y]/(x^N,xy+x+y).                               \tag{FS6}
\]
For positive weights, the relation has head \(xy\). The standard
set consists of the cheapest \(N\) monomials in the two rays
\(1,x,x^2,\ldots,y,y^2,\ldots\). Every candidate is independent:
translating \(X=1+x,Y=1+y\) gives \(XY=1\) and \((X-1)^N=0\).
A two-arm prefix changes triangularly, with diagonal ones, to \(N\)
consecutive Laurent powers of the unit \(X\). Multiplying by a
power of \(X\) identifies these with \(1,X,\ldots,X^{N-1}\).
This proves independence over every field. The resulting inverse-curve
fan is an established input in the
[planar wall-cost calculation](wall_cost_products.md).

Normalize \(w_x=1\), and put \(u=w_y/w_x\), \(v=w_z/w_x\).
Let \(D_j(u)\) be that \(j\)-element two-ray prefix, and let
\(c_j(u)\) be the cost of its last monomial. In particular
\(D_{j-1}(u)\subset D_j(u)\). Its \(j-1\) walls are
\[
               u=\frac{a}{j-a},\quad 1\le a<j.         \tag{FS7}
\]
The function \(c_j(u)\) is nondecreasing: it is the \(j\)-th
smallest member of a family of nondecreasing functions, including
the constant cost zero. Consequently \(u+c_N(u)\) is strictly
increasing.

The plain \(x\) ray is compulsory in \(H_N\). Its whole standard
set is that ray together with \(yD_N(u)\) and \(zD_N(v)\),
where the variable names in the second prefix are \(x,z\).
Quotienting by \(v_y\) deletes exactly the monomial
\(y(D_N(u)\setminus D_{N-1}(u))\), of cost \(u+c_N(u)\).
This follows from the actual planar quotient \(T_N\to T_{N-1}\)
and the elementary initial-ideal deletion rule.
The two branch supports are disjoint, so quotienting by \(v_y-v_z\)
deletes the more expensive of their two leading monomials. Strict
monotonicity makes their equality exactly \(u=v\). Therefore the
complete standard sets of \(A_N\) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
u<v & \{1,x,\ldots,x^{N-1}\}\sqcup yD_N(u)\sqcup zD_{N-1}(v)\\
u>v & \{1,x,\ldots,x^{N-1}\}\sqcup yD_{N-1}(u)\sqcup zD_N(v).
\end{array}                                                   \tag{FS8}
\]
Those of \(C_N\) use \(D_{N-1}\) in both branches.
Equations (FS7)--(FS8), with their open inequalities, specify every
full positive cone, rather than selected weights. Refinements at ties
choose neighboring cones and add no global monomial initial ideals.

The substitution \(p=u/(1+u)\) orders the walls at \(a/N\)
and \(b/(N-1)\). These two lists strictly interlace:
\[
 \frac aN<\frac a{N-1}<\frac{a+1}N\quad(1\le a\le N-2).
\]
Counting the grid rectangles intersecting either side of the diagonal
in (FS8) gives the exact counts
\[
 g(A_N)=(N-1)(N+2),\qquad g(C_N)=(N-1)^2,
 \qquad g(A_N)-g(C_N)=3N-3.                              \tag{FS9}
\]
For example, the \(u<v\) half contributes
\((N-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(N-i)=(N-1)(N+2)/2\) cells.
The other half is symmetric.

## Exact primitive and actual edge lengths

Let \(W\) sum the primitive one-norms over actual undirected wall
pieces, and let \(L\) sum the one-norms of their **actual** profile
jumps. Put
\[
 a_j=\sum_{b=1}^{j-1}\frac{j}{\gcd(j,b)}
     =\sum_{d\mid j}d\varphi(d)-1,\qquad a_1=0.         \tag{FS10}
\]
This is the previously established planar primitive wall cost.
Then, over every field,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W(A_N)&=N a_N+(N+1)a_{N-1}+4N-4,\\
 W(C_N)&=2(N-1)a_{N-1},\\
 L(A_N)&=2(N-1)(N^2+1),\\
 L(C_N)&=2(N-1)^2(N-2).                                \tag{FS11}
\end{aligned}
\]

For the primitive formulas, a vertical \(N\)-wall has \(N-a\)
pieces above the diagonal. A vertical \((N-1)\)-wall has \(b+1\)
pieces below it. The raw planar exchange vectors are
\((a,-(N-a),0)\) and \((b,-(N-1-b),0)\).
Symmetry \(a\leftrightarrow N-a\) and
\(b\leftrightarrow N-1-b\), and the horizontal counterparts,
give \(N a_N+(N+1)a_{N-1}\).
The diagonal has \(2N-2\) pieces, each with primitive direction
\((0,1,-1)\), supplying \(4N-4\).

For \(L\), keep the raw lengths \(N,N-1\) on those walls.
Their total is \((N-1)(2N^2-N-2)\). On successive diagonal
intervals, the slope of \(u+c_N(u)\) is
\[
                       N,1,N-1,1,\ldots,2,1.
\]
The diagonal actual lengths therefore sum to
\(2(\sum_{j=2}^N j+N-1)=N^2+3N-4\). Adding proves (FS11).
In particular
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W(A_N)-W(C_N)&=N(a_N-a_{N-1})+3a_{N-1}+4N-4,\\
 L(A_N)-L(C_N)&=6N^2-8N+2.                             \tag{FS12}
\end{aligned}
\]

The familiar elementary totient estimate
\(a_m\le3m^2/4-1\) for even \(m\) makes the first difference
cubic on infinite sequences. To verify that estimate, write
\(m=2^e q\) with \(q\) odd. The multiplicative function
\(a_m+1=\sum_{d\mid m}d\varphi(d)\) has
\(2\)-part \((2\cdot4^e+1)/3\le3\cdot4^e/4\), while its
odd part is at most \(q^2\).
For every odd prime \(p\), this gives
\[
                W(A_p)-W(C_p)\ge p^3/4.                \tag{FS13}
\]
For \(p\ge47\), the opposite sign follows at \(N=p+1\):
\[
 W(A_{p+1})-W(C_{p+1})
 \le-\frac{p^3}4+\frac{21p^2}4+\frac{13p}4-\frac14
 \le-\frac{p^3}8.                                     \tag{FS14}
\]
The upper bound \(|W(A_N)-W(C_N)|=O(N^3)\) follows at once
from \(a_j\le j(j-1)\). Thus both cubic orders are sharp in
this family. There are infinitely many eligible primes, and
\(n=3N-1\), so these are cubic jumps in the actual algebra length.

In characteristic different from two, (FS5) makes these jumps forced:
no rule selecting a one-dimensional ideal quotient can guarantee
\(W(A)-W(A')=O(n^2\log^C(n+1))\) for every input and any fixed
\(C\). The same holds for an absolute-jump bound. The positive
deletion direction is also cubic by (FS14).
This does **not** disprove a cubic-polylogarithmic total variation bound
for a specially chosen complete chain: a single cubic step is compatible
with that total. Nor does it refute an induction using \(L\), whose
forced jump here is the quadratic expression in (FS12).

## A coordinate change from six cones to quadratic growth

There is also an explicit presentation consequence, without changing
the abstract algebra. Put
\[
 B_N=K[x,Y,Z]/(x^N,YZ,Y^2+xY,Z^2+xZ,
                              x^{N-1}(Y-Z)).             \tag{FS15}
\]
This ideal is ordinary homogeneous. The substitutions
\[
       x\longmapsto x,\qquad
       Y\longmapsto(1+x)y,\qquad Z\longmapsto(1+x)z       \tag{FS16}
\]
give an isomorphism \(B_N\simeq A_N\). Its inverse uses the
polynomial \(u=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(-x)^i\) modulo \(x^N\).
For example \(Y^2+xY\) maps to
\((1+x)((1+x)y^2+xy)\); the other displayed relations follow
likewise. The \(R\)-module reductions prove equality of lengths
and the inverse, with no power-series completion required.

For every \(N\ge3\), the full positive fan of this graded
presentation has exactly six cones, the six strict orders of
\(w_x,w_Y,w_Z\). In degrees \(1\le d<N\), the plain
\(x^d\) is compulsory. All \(Y\)-branch monomials of degree
\(d\) are parallel, and the cheapest is \(x^{d-1}Y\) if
\(w_x<w_Y\), otherwise \(Y^d\). The \(Z\) branch is
analogous. In degree \(N\), only the identified branch socle
survives. Its two costs are
\[
 w_Y+(N-1)\min(w_x,w_Y),\qquad
 w_Z+(N-1)\min(w_x,w_Z).
\]
The cheaper is determined by \(w_Y<w_Z\), since the displayed
function of the branch price is strictly increasing. These choices
give six distinct standard sets when \(N\ge3\); when \(N=2\)
two boundaries disappear and there are four.
In particular, over every field,
\[
 g(B_N)=6,\quad g(A_N)=(N-1)(N+2)\quad(N\ge3),
 \qquad B_N\simeq A_N.                                 \tag{FS17}
\]
The degree-two branch choices already distinguish the different
comparisons with \(w_x\), and the top socle distinguishes the
two possible branch orders, proving distinctness.

Keeping the actual moment jumps also gives
\[
       W(B_N)=12,\qquad L(B_N)=4N^2-6N+6\quad(N\ge3).
                                                               \tag{FS18}
\]
On each of \(w_x=w_Y\) and \(w_x=w_Z\), the two edge
multiplicities are \((N-1)(N-2)/2\) and \(N(N-1)/2\).
On \(w_Y=w_Z\) they are \(1,N\). Multiplying their sum
by the primitive one-norm two proves (FS18).

Thus a unit rescaling of two nilpotent generators changes a constant
fan into a quadratic one, and changes actual edge length from
quadratic to cubic, within the same local Gorenstein algebra in
characteristic different from two. This is a consequence about the
specified coordinate presentation, not a new algebra classification.
Abstract isomorphism or the existence of a grading after a nonlinear
coordinate change cannot transfer a small fan to the original
generators. The earlier positive-grading theorem concerns grading
of the given polynomial presentation. Exact priority of this explicit
quantitative separation is unassessed.

## Reproducibility and the remaining problem

Run from the repository root:

```bash
python3 -B scripts/run_forced_socle_fan_checks.py
./build.sh
python3 tests/test_smoke.py
```

The checker constructs actual commuting multiplication columns directly
from (FS2) and the two quotient relations. Independently traversed
border cones are compared with every cone of (FS8). It computes socles
by a stacked multiplication kernel, records the characteristic-two
exception, checks ordinary reduced-basis S-pairs, and recovers primitive
and actual edge costs from the complete fans. An independent arithmetic
sieve checks the totient sums and the stated prime inequalities.
The graded presentation is checked independently, including all its
cones, actual edge lengths and the explicit unit-triangular isomorphism.
Exact witnesses and source hashes are saved in
[forced_socle_fan_checks.json](../results/research/forced_socle_fan_checks.json).

This proof uses the existing
[one-dimensional ideal deletion rule](adjacent_rank_fans.md),
[common-coordinate fan-product framework](wall_cost_products.md),
and the classical state-polyhedron dictionary of
[Babson--Onn--Thomas, Theorem 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0207135).
The elementary socle criterion, Möbius translations and totient
estimates are not claimed as new principles. Exact priority of the
assembled forced-step theorem remains unassessed.

The goal still requires a collective bound on the final fan, a suitable
whole-chain estimate, or a genuinely supergeneric realizable family.
The fixed-count sections still require a new coupling argument: one
cannot import the planar matroid token proof merely from their
two-exchange edge description. No unrestricted exponent, lower
separation, or required general complexity consequence is established
by these results.
