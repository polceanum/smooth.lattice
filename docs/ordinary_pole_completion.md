# Weighted basis completion for rational-function curve quotients

Let \(K\) be any field and \(P,Q\in K[t]\), with \(Q\) monic of
degree \(q\ge1\), \(Q(0)\ne0\), \(\deg P\le q\), and
\(\gcd(P,Q)=1\). Let \(F\) be monic of degree \(n>0\), with
\(F(0)\ne0\) and \(\gcd(F,Q)=1\). For
\[
 I_{F,P,Q}=(xy-1,Q(x)z-P(x),F(x)),
\]
there are uniform estimates with absolute implied constants:
\[
 \begin{split}
 g(I_{F,P,Q})&=O(q^{10}n^2(1+\log(n+1))),\\
 V(I_{F,P,Q})&=O(q^{10}n^3(1+\log(n+1))),\\
 |\mathcal U(I_{F,P,Q})|&=O(q^{11}n^2(1+\log(n+1))).
 \end{split}                                                \tag{1}
\]
The modulus and denominator can have repeated or non-rational factors.
No genericity, splitting, perfectness or characteristic restriction is
required; the numerator may be a nonunit or zero modulo \(F\).
Here \(g\) counts distinct monomial initial ideals over global orders,
equivalently full positive weight cones; \(V\) is the sum of the
primitive threshold costs \(\nu(u)\) over actual undirected walls,
as defined in the [primitive-wall note](primitive_wall_spectrum.md);
and \(\mathcal U\) is the union of the reduced Gröbner bases.
These are all finite quotients of the fixed curve with coordinates
\(x=t,y=t^{-1},z=P(t)/Q(t)\). Its ring is
\(K[t,t^{-1},Q^{-1}]\): Bézout for \(P,Q\) expresses \(Q^{-1}\)
as a polynomial in \(t,z\). Its finite ideals are generated by the
polynomials \(F\) satisfying the hypotheses above. For \(n\ge100q\),
every such quotient also satisfies \(g(I_{F,P,Q})=\Omega(n^2/q)\).
Thus for each fixed pole order the fan power is quadratic for every
quotient, with at most one logarithmic gap.

A family in this entire rational-function class with \(g\ge c n^{2+\epsilon}\)
requires \(q=\Omega_{c,\epsilon}((n^\epsilon/\log(n+1))^{1/10})\).
In particular, subpolynomial denominator degree cannot produce a
polynomial separation above the generic corner-cut scale.
The exponents of \(q\) are deliberately loose upper bounds.

The [Frobenius estimate](frobenius_pole_transport.md) remains stronger,
with \(q^9\) in its matching characteristic and sharp quadratic/cubic
orders for the binary quartic. The present argument fills the previously
uncontrolled ordinary-order region and covers every numerator and
denominator in the regular-at-zero-and-infinity class. It preserves the
explicit rank failure outside Frobenius; it does not assert a new exact
shift where that shift is false.

The unrestricted \(G_3\) endpoint, a polynomial lower separation and
quantitative priority remain open. These class results and classical
determinant/convexity tools are not a completion of the original goal.

## Retained notation and established bounds

Use the carrier and allocation notation of the Frobenius note. Normalize
\(w_z=1\), put \(\alpha=w_x/w_z,\beta=w_y/w_z,T=\min(\alpha,\beta)\),
and fix a strict chart
\[
 m<q\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)<m+1,\quad a=q-m,\quad
                         -m\le j<a.
\]
A standard set is \(D(A,B,c)\), with pure axis lengths \(A,B\), ray
lengths \(c_j\), and \(n=1+A+B+\sum c_j\). The tag costs
\(\tau_j=j\alpha\) for \(j\ge0\), \(-j\beta\) for \(j<0\), obey
\(0\le\tau_j\le qT\).

The carrier proof for general \(P/Q\) is already in the
[pole-order note](pole_order_fans.md#rational-functions-regular-at-zero-and-infinity-have-the-same-generic-fan).
Its kernel calculation does not require generic evaluation. Let
\(\mathfrak D=(Q)_0\) be the pole divisor of \(z\), of degree \(q\).
Three elementary statements hold uniformly here:

* Equal ray lengths and all base tags give the full principal-part
  space \(L(A\infty+B0+C\mathfrak D)\). At size \(n\), it is a basis modulo
  every \(F\), after clearing a unit denominator.
* A universal complete-pole basis gives \(A,B\le2q+n/(qT)\).
* Missing required base tags lie collectively in a profile box with
  sides \(4qn,4qn,n^2\) when \(n\ge4q\), giving \(O(qn^2)\) states.
  At \(n<4q\), the established \(O(n^3)\) bound is already \(O(qn^2)\).

For the complete-pole assertion, the highest layer modulo the previous
layer is represented by \(t^jP^r\bmod Q\), for \(q\) consecutive
Laurent exponents. These form a basis of \(K[t]/Q\), since \(t,P\)
are units there. Induction supplies all principal parts of
\(C\mathfrak D\), even for repeated or non-rational poles. Clearing
\(t^BQ^C\) gives all polynomials of degree less than \(n\).
The cheap-prefix argument for missing tags uses divisor
\(q\infty+q0+\lfloor qT\rfloor\mathfrak D\), of the same total
degree as before. Only allocations with all base tags remain.

## A weighted basis-completion lemma

Let \(D(A,B,c)\) be any monomial vector basis of this quotient, coherent
or otherwise, with \(A\ge a-1,B\ge m+q\). Set
\[
 \ell=\min_jc_j,\qquad d_j=c_j-\ell,\qquad
                         d=\sum_jd_j.
\]
Let \(D' = D(A,B-q,c+\mathbf1)\) denote the literal shifted allocation.
It may be dependent. Nevertheless there is a genuine monomial vector
basis \(E\) of the same quotient with
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \operatorname{cost}(E)\le
             \operatorname{cost}(D')+qT d.\quad}           \tag{2}
\]
It can be found by ordinary greedy completion while forcing the
independent complete-pole core described below. The completion need not
be a downset. It is used as a vector basis for a cost comparison, never
declared the standard set of another quotient.

**Proof.** Multiplication by \(h=t^q/Q(t)\) is a unit modulo \(F\), in every
characteristic, even if \(P\) is not a unit there.
Take the part \(D_0\subset D\) consisting of both axes and all full
ray layers through \(\ell\). Its rational span is
\(L(A\infty+B0+\ell\mathfrak D)\). Multiplication by \(h\) maps it exactly onto
\[
 L(A\infty+(B-q)0+(\ell+1)\mathfrak D).
\]
The corresponding monomial frame
\(W=D(A,B-q,(\ell+1)\mathbf1)\) is therefore a basis of \(hV(D_0)\).
Since \(D\) was independent modulo \(F\), this frame is independent
there too. The \(d\) remaining columns of \(hD\) extend it to a basis.

For an excess ray column \(t^jz^r\), \(r>\ell\), its multiplied
function is \(t^{j+q}P^r/Q^{r+1}\). Its pole at infinity has order
at most \(a-1\), it has no pole at zero, and its other poles are bounded
by \((r+1)\mathfrak D\). Thus it is a
linear combination of the minimal axis tags and all carrier rays
through level \(r+1\). Every monomial in this expansion costs at most
\[
                              r+1+qT.                    \tag{3}
\]
Those minimal axis tags are already in \(W\).

Expand the nonzero determinant of the frame consisting of \(W\) and
these multiplied excess columns. At least one choice of one monomial
from each expansion still gives a nonzero determinant. It is a basis,
with total cost at most
\[
 \operatorname{cost}(W)+
             \sum_j\sum_{r=\ell+1}^{c_j}(r+1+qT).
\]
The literal \(D'\) consists of \(W\) and the corresponding columns
with costs \(r+1+\tau_j\). Their difference is at most
\(\sum_j(qT-\tau_j)d_j\le qT d\), proving (2). This proof uses
ordinary determinant multilinearity, not a generic noncancellation
assumption. \(\square\)

For an effective completion, greedily extend \(W\) using the two axes
and all \(q\) ray prefixes through \(1+\max c_j\). This common pool has
\(O(qn+n)\) columns. A nonzero determinant above proves it has rank
\(n\). Minimum-cost completion within it is at least as good as the
basis selected by the expansion. Given the residue columns, dense
elimination costs \(O(qn^3)\) field operations, with column preparation,
sorting and coefficient bits separate. No new greedy algorithm is claimed.

There is a symmetric forward-axis version: if \(A\ge a-1+q,B\ge m\),
use unit \(1/Q\), decrease \(A\) by \(q\), and increase every ray
length by one. The same proof applies. Multiplied excess functions
\(t^jP^r/Q^{r+1}\) have zero-pole order at most \(m\), no pole at
infinity, and remaining poles bounded by \((r+1)\mathfrak D\).
This is a costed completion in both directions. Exact forward transport
was a stronger feature of the special numerator \(P=1\).

That distinction is necessary. In characteristic two, take
\(P=t+1,Q=t^2+t+1,F=(t+1)^7\). In chart \(m=0\), the
actual standard set at weights \((3,10,13)\) is
\(D=\{1,x,x^2,x^3,x^4,y,z\}\), of rank seven. Its literal
forward shift is \(\{1,x,x^2,y,z,z^2,xz\}\), of rank six.
Forcing the complete core and completing gives
\(E=\{1,x,x^2,y,z,xz,xz^2\}\), of rank seven. The literal,
completed and upper costs in (2) are respectively \(74,77,80\).
Here \(z\) is nilpotent and \(E\) is not a downset; both features
are retained in the report.

## The variance estimate survives the extra cost

Take an actual coherent \(D\), let \(R\) be the cheaper axis length,
and fix the other axis. Set
\[
 S=\sum c_j=M-R,\quad v_j=c_j-S/q,\quad
 x=\|v\|_2,\quad J=\sum\tau_j.
\]
The same separated cost formula is
\[
 g(R)+E(v),\quad
 g(R)=\frac{qT+1}{2q}R^2+
   \left(\frac T2-\frac Mq-\frac12-\frac Jq\right)R,\quad
 E(v)=\tfrac12x^2+\sum\tau_jv_j.                            \tag{4}
\]
If \(R\) is at least its required base length plus \(q\), the corresponding
forward or inverse version of (2) gives
\[
 g'(R)\le(qT+1)/2+T d.                                    \tag{5}
\]
For an exact transport, when available, the \(Td\) term is unnecessary.
If \(R\) is smaller, \(R<2q\) and \(g'(R)\le2qT\).

Raise \(R\) by \(\delta=S\bmod q\) and balance the ray lengths.
The resulting universal complete-pole basis gives
\[
 E(v)\le4q^2T+q+qT d.
\]
Since \(d=q(S/q-\ell)=-q\min_jv_j\le qx\), and
\(\|\tau\|_2\le q^{3/2}T\), it follows that
\[
 \tfrac12x^2\le(q^2+q^{3/2})Tx+4q^2T+q,\qquad
                         x\le8q^2\max(1,T).              \tag{6}
\]
For example the positive root is at most
\(4q^2T+\sqrt{8q^2T+2q}<8q^2\max(1,T)\).

At fixed \(A,B\), retain just
\(X=\sum_{j>0}jc_j,\ Y=\sum_{j<0}(-j)c_j\). Each integer differs
from its equal-ray value by at most
\[
                         8q^{7/2}\max(1,T).               \tag{7}
\]
For fixed \(A,B,X,Y\), the smallest feasible \(z\)-moment is independent
of the prices. A tie gives the same whole profile, and cannot yield two
distinct strictly optimal matroid bases. Thus at most one coherent state
has a given tuple, retaining all crossings.

For \(L\le T<2L,\ 1\le L<n\), the count per chart is at most
\[
 \left(2q+1+\frac n{qL}\right)^2(33q^{7/2}L)^2
 \le17424q^9n^2.                                         \tag{8}
\]
Sum \(q\) charts and at most \(\lceil\log_2n\rceil\) scales.
The \(T<1\), \(T\ge n\), missing-tag and small-\(n\) portions are handled
as follows. If \(T<1\), the axes have at most \(n^2\) choices and
(7) allows \(O(q^7)\) moment pairs per choice and chart.
If \(T\ge n\), the bottleneck bound gives \(O(q^2)\) axis
pairs, and \(0\le X,Y\le qn\) gives \(O(q^2n^2)\)
moment pairs. Sum the \(q\) charts. The missing-tag part was already
bounded collectively, and at \(n<4q\) use \(O(n^3)\).
This proves (1). The individual reduced-basis size bound \(q+5\) and
\(V\le3ng\) give its remaining consequences.

## Every quotient has quadratically many forced states at fixed order

For \(n\ge100q\), choose
\[
 2n/5\le A,B\le9n/20,\quad A+B\equiv n-1\pmod q,\quad
                         C=(n-1-A-B)/q.
\]
There are \(\Omega(n^2/q)\) such pairs, with an absolute constant.
Put \(s=C+3/4\), \(\alpha=s/(A+1/2),\beta=s/(B+1/2)\).
Their minimum price satisfies \(qT<3/4\).
In the corresponding strict carrier chart, each of the \(q\) ray
prefixes of length \(C\) costs less than \(s\), and its next element
costs more. The axis prefixes have exactly lengths \(A,B\).
There are precisely \(n\) chosen carrier monomials; they form the
universal complete-pole basis. If a price lies on a carrier wall, perturb it
slightly, preserving these strict threshold inequalities. Every pair
therefore exposes a standard set over every \(F,K\). Different axis
lengths distinguish the states. This proves the lower assertion without
a generic point assumption.

## Relation to existing tools and the next broad question

The carrier and complete-pole span arguments are already proved in the
earlier notes. Determinant expansion, forced-set matroid contraction and
minimum-cost augmentation are classical linear algebra and greedy tools.
The [Babson--Onn--Thomas framework](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0207135)
supplies the initial/profile correspondence, and the missing-tag bound
uses the classical lattice-vertex theorem. No new general determinant,
matroid or lattice theorem is claimed. The exact priority of this
ordinary-pole bound and its costed completion application remains unassessed.

The closest inspected computational comparison is
[Jeannerod--Neiger--Schost--Villard, arXiv:1602.00651v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1602.00651v1),
Problem 1, Definitions 1.1--1.2 and Theorem 1.3. It computes an interpolation
module basis in shifted Popov form for one supplied integer shift;
Section 1.3 also explains weighted multivariate interpolation.
Its theorem assumes a supplied Jordan representation and gives a fast
field-operation bound for that problem. It does not state the number of
distinct initial ideals as all positive weights vary. Conversely, (1)
is a counting statement, not an improvement to that single-shift
algorithm. This specific comparison does not establish priority over
other interpolation, state-polytope or curve-dependent fan literature.

The report is results/research/ordinary_pole_checks.json. Cleared
rational numerators independently check the complete-core and expansion
spans. Remainder-column quotients check the constrained completions,
including the preserved characteristic-zero rank failure, and verify the
budget by direct costs. Complete border fans, exact chart-area covers,
larger ordinary-order scans and forced common-basis witnesses check the
variance and conditional profile count. The current report covers 64
complete fans and 1,459 cones, including 24 general rational-function
quotients, 22 with nonunit numerator coordinates. Seventeen of those
models have \(F(1)=0\), and the preserved rank-failure example has
literal rank four and completed rank five. Both unit directions are
checked before quotienting, including repeated irreducible denominators.
Finite checks are supporting
evidence, not proof of novelty.

This closes the fixed/subpolynomial-degree regular rational-function branch
as a polynomial-separation route. It does not cover rational functions
with additional poles at zero or infinity, general fixed curves or
unrestricted \(G_3\); polynomially growing denominator degrees remain
possible under (1). The useful general mechanism is to
price the incomplete part of a unit-multiplied frame while keeping its
complete geometric core. Extending that mechanism to a general
coexistence budget, or constructing a realizable family that defeats it,
remains part of the original active goal.
