# Minimum length for a prescribed branch intersection

Let \(A=K[x,y,z]/I\) have finite length \(n\), over any field, and
assume \(yz\in I\). Put \(B=K[x]\) and \(W=yA\cap zA\). This
note determines the minimum possible \(n\) for every prescribed nonzero
finite torsion \(B\)-module \(W\), classifies all coordinate quotients
attaining that minimum, and bounds their complete positive initial fans.
There is no locality, grading, splitting-field or fixed-generator assumption.

Write the monic invariant factors in **descending** divisibility order:
\[
 W\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^r B/(d_i),\qquad d_{i+1}\mid d_i,
 \qquad \deg d_i>0.
\]
Define
\[
 h=\sum_i\deg d_i,\quad
 \Phi(W)=\sum_i i\deg d_i,\quad
 g_j=\prod_{i=j+1}^r d_i\ (0\le j\le r),\quad D=\deg d_1.
 \tag{M1}
\]
In particular \(g_r=1\), \(\deg g_0=h\), and
\(\sum_{j<r}\deg g_j=\Phi(W)\).

**Theorem.** The possible lengths for this prescribed intersection are
exactly
\[
                         n\ge2\Phi(W).                    \tag{M2}
\]
At equality both actual planar branch modules are uniquely determined:
\[
 (I:y)\cap K[x,y]=J_W(x,y),\qquad
 J_W=(g_0,g_1y,\ldots,g_{r-1}y^{r-1},y^r),               \tag{M3}
\]
and likewise for the \(z\)-branch. All minimum-length coordinate ideals
are parametrized by an isomorphism between the two canonical copies of
\(W\) and an element of \(W\). For every such ideal,
\[
       g(I)=O(h\Phi(W))=O(hn)\le O(n^2).                 \tag{M4}
\]
Here \(g(I)\) counts distinct monomial initial ideals for global orders,
or equivalently maximal cells of the positive weight fan. The quadratic
power is sharp in this entire minimum-length class, in characteristic zero.
This does not bound all quotients with \(yz=0\).

The invariant-factor structure theorem, module dominance, diagonal gluing,
matroid contraction and the lattice-polytope vertex bound are established
ingredients. The exact priority of the minimum, rigidity and combined fan
statements is **unassessed**. They do not settle unrestricted \(G_3(n)\),
establish a supergeneric lower bound, or complete the research goal.

The subsequent [Fitting-core deduction](fitting_branch_core.md) identifies
the planar auxiliary minimum and rigidity below as a classical
Hilbert--Burch/Smith-form corollary, using a stronger coefficientwise ideal
containment. It also extends the fan bound to nonminimal branches in
terms of their excess length. The planar auxiliary lemma is therefore
not an independently new theorem; the assembled classification and
quantitative statements still require their exact priority comparison.

## A planar minimum and its equality case

We prove the stronger auxiliary statement: if \(C=K[x,y]/J\) is finite
and a \(B\)-submodule \(W\subset C\) is killed by \(y\), then
\[
                         \dim_K C\ge\Phi(W),              \tag{M5}
\]
and equality for nonzero \(W\) forces \(W=\ker(y|C)\) and
\(J=J_W\) in the actual coordinates.

Decompose by the commuting operators into the \(y\)-nilpotent summand
and its complement, and then into \(f(x)\)-primary summands for monic
irreducible \(f\). These are cyclic quotient algebras. Only the first
summand can contain \(W\); all other summands add nonnegative length.
It suffices to work in one primary component, normalizing lengths by
\(\deg f\). Let the elementary exponents of \(W\) be
\(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_s>0\).

The power filtration has cyclic factors
\[
 C_j=y^jC/y^{j+1}C\simeq B/(f^{a_j}),\qquad
 a_0\ge a_1\ge\cdots\ge0.
\]
Multiplication by \(y\) is a surjection \(C_j\to C_{j+1}\).
For \(W_j=W\cap y^jC\), its factor embeds in the kernel of this
map, so it is cyclic of length
\(\gamma_j\le\delta_j:=a_j-a_{j+1}\).

The partition \(\lambda\) dominates the decreasing rearrangement of
\((\gamma_j)\). For completeness, lift generators of any \(k\)
chosen cyclic factors. Their generated submodule has length at least
the sum of those factor lengths, by the induced filtration. A submodule
generated by \(k\) elements of \(\bigoplus B/(f^{\lambda_i})\)
has length at most \(\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_k\): the PID embedding
criterion bounds its invariant exponents componentwise by the ambient
ones, and it has at most \(k\) nonzero exponents. Choosing the \(k\)
largest factors proves the assertion. Partial summation and rearrangement
therefore give
\[
 \sum_i i\lambda_i
 \le\sum_i i\gamma_i^\downarrow
 \le\sum_j(j+1)\gamma_j
 \le\sum_j(j+1)\delta_j
 =\sum_j a_j.                                             \tag{M6}
\]
Summing after multiplying by \(\deg f\) proves (M5), including
irreducible and inseparable primary factors over the original field.

For equality, every inequality in (M6) is an equality. Thus
\[
 \delta_j=\gamma_j=\lambda_{j+1},\qquad
 a_j=\sum_{i>j}\lambda_i,\qquad W=\ker y.                 \tag{M7}
\]
The last assertion also follows from rank-nullity:
\(\dim\ker y=a_0\deg f=\dim W\).
We now prove rigidity by induction on \(s\), without assuming that a
deformation with these associated factors is already split.

Put \(L=\ker y\) and \(L_1=L\cap yC\).
The quotient \(L/L_1\) is cyclic of exponent \(\lambda_1\).
It splits as a \(B\)-module: any lift of its generator is killed by
\(f^{\lambda_1}\), since this polynomial kills all of \(L\).
Hence \(L_1\) has exponents \(\lambda_2,\ldots,\lambda_s\).
The cyclic planar module \(yC\), with cyclic generator \(y\),
attains (M5) for this tail partition. Induction gives its exact
staircase annihilator, in particular \(f^{a_1}y=0\).

The kernel of \(C/yC\to yC/y^2C\) is generated by
\(f^{a_1}\). Equality supplies a lift
\(w_0=f^{a_1}+yv\in L\). Since \(yw_0=0\) and
\(f^{a_1}y=0\), one has \(y^2v=0\), so \(yv\in L_1\).
Both \(w_0\) and \(yv\) are killed by \(f^{\lambda_1}\).
Consequently \(f^{a_0}=0\). Together with the inductive relations
this yields
\((f^{a_0},f^{a_1}y,\ldots,y^s)\subset J\), with matching
colength, hence equality. For \(s=1\), (M7) gives \(yC=0\)
and starts the induction. Intersect the primary staircase ideals;
their row coefficients multiply to \(g_j\), proving (M3) globally.

## Exact minimum and joint resource feasibility

Let \(Y=yA\), \(Z=zA\) and \(d=\dim_K A/(Y+Z)\).
Both branches are cyclic planar modules, and their common submodule
\(W\) is killed by \(y,z\). Multiplication by \(y\) gives a
surjection \(A/(Y+Z)\to Y/yY\). Rank-nullity gives
\(h\le\dim(Y/yY)\le d\). Therefore
\[
 \dim Y+\dim Z=n+h-d\le n.
\]
Apply (M5) to each branch to obtain (M2).

In \(C_W=K[x,y]/J_W\), the kernel of \(y\) is the direct sum
of the cyclic modules generated by
\(g_{j+1}y^j\), \(0\le j<r\), with annihilators \(d_{j+1}\).
Start with
\[
 H_0=K[x,y,z]/(g_0,yz,yJ_W(x,y),zJ_W(x,z)).
\]
Its vector-space summands are \(B/g_0\) and two copies of \(C_W\),
tagged by \(y,z\). Its length is \(h+2\Phi(W)\).
Identify the two kernel copies by the graph of any \(B\)-isomorphism.
That graph is killed by \(y,z\), is an ideal of dimension \(h\),
and meets either branch trivially. The quotient has length \(2\Phi(W)\)
and intersection exactly \(W\).

Every larger length is possible. Add a disjoint factor
\(K[x]/(x^t)\) with \(y=1,z=0\), where \(t=n-2\Phi(W)>0\).
The original factor has nilpotent \(y\), so these coordinate ideals
are comaximal over every field. The new factor contributes no overlap.
This padding is a length construction, not a fan-preservation assertion.

In particular, for prescribed dimension \(h>0\) and minimal generator
number \(r\), the exact feasible triples are
\[
                1\le r\le h,\qquad n\ge2h+r(r-1).         \tag{M8}
\]
Indeed \(\Phi-h=\sum_i(i-1)\deg d_i\ge r(r-1)/2\).
Equality is attained by \(d_1=x^{h-r+1}\),
\(d_2=\cdots=d_r=x\), followed by the padding just described.
The full invariant-factor data can force a larger minimum than (M8).
For \(W=0\), every positive length is possible and its minimum is one.

## All minimum-length quotients, in their original coordinates

At \(n=2\Phi(W)\), the two branch lengths equal \(\Phi(W)\),
\(d=h\), and \(W=\ker(y|Y)=\ker(z|Z)\). Rigidity gives (M3).
The surjection \(A/(Y+Z)\to Y/yY\) is an isomorphism, so the
axis quotient is \(B/g_0\). In particular \(g_0(x)\in Y+Z\).
Write it as \(v_Y+v_Z\). Since \(g_0Y=g_0Z=0\), multiplication
by \(y,z\) shows \(v_Y,v_Z\in W\). Thus
\[
                     g_0(x)\in W,\qquad d_1g_0\in I.      \tag{M9}
\]

Use the fixed envelope
\[
 H_{\max}=K[x,y,z]/(g_0d_1,yz,yJ_W(x,y),zJ_W(x,z)).
 \tag{M10}
\]
Call its two branch kernels \(W_Y,W_Z\), with canonical generators
\(g_{j+1}y^{j+1}\), \(g_{j+1}z^{j+1}\). Choose
\(\theta:W_Y\to W_Z\) a \(B\)-isomorphism and \(u\in W_Y\).
Quotient by the graph \(\{w-\theta(w):w\in W_Y\}\) and by
the \(B\)-submodule generated by \(g_0-u\).

The graph has dimension \(h\). The second submodule has dimension
\(D\), projects isomorphically onto \(g_0(B/g_0d_1)\), and is
disjoint from the graph: \(d_1\) kills \(u\), so no extra relation
appears when that projection is zero. Both submodules are killed by
\(y,z\), so their sum is an ideal; neither branch acquires a relation.
The quotient length is
\(h+D+2\Phi-h-D=2\Phi\), with exactly the required intersection.

Conversely, every minimal quotient contains the relations (M10).
Its kernel on the two branches is exactly the graph of their induced
identification. Equation (M9) gives a unique element \(u\) after
using the \(Y\)-branch as reference. These relations already have
the necessary dimension, hence generate the whole kernel.
Thus the parametrization is bijective for labelled coordinate ideals.
It is not a classification modulo coordinate changes or an assertion
that a linear change preserves a Gröbner fan. Over a finite field it
immediately gives \(|W|\,|\operatorname{Aut}_B(W)|\) such ideals.

## One fixed residual matroid for the entire positive fan

The quotient \(A/W\) has one initial ideal for every global order:
its actual ideal is
\[
 (g_0,yz,g_1y,g_2y^2,\ldots,y^r,
                 g_1z,g_2z^2,\ldots,z^r).                \tag{M11}
\]
Each displayed leading monomial dominates all its tails in every
global order, since the tails are proper divisors in \(x\).
Row division gives colength \(2\Phi-h\), proving equality with the
quotient ideal, rather than just an inclusion. Its fixed standard set is
\[
 C=\{x^i:0\le i<h\}
 \ \cup\!\bigcup_{0\le j<r-1}
 \{x^iy^{j+1},x^iz^{j+1}:0\le i<\deg g_{j+1}\}.          \tag{M12}
\]
Because \(I\subset I+W\), every standard set of \(A\) contains \(C\).

The relations (M3),(M9) also confine every standard set to the fixed
ground consisting of \(x^i\), \(0\le i<h+D\), and the two branch
rows \(x^iy^{j+1},x^iz^{j+1}\), \(i<\deg g_j\), \(j<r\).
After removing \(C\), the residual labelled ground \(E\) consists of
\[
 \begin{split}
 &x^i,\quad h\le i<h+D;\\
 &x^iy^{j+1},\ x^iz^{j+1},\quad
       \deg g_{j+1}\le i<\deg g_j,\quad0\le j<r.
 \end{split}                                               \tag{M13}
\]
There are \(2h+D\le3h\) labels. Their residue vectors modulo
\(\operatorname{span}_K C\) form a single rank-\(h\) represented
matroid. Each branch part is a basis. This is a vector contraction;
\(\operatorname{span}C\) need not be an algebra ideal.

Take the convex hull \(Q\) of the exponent sums of all bases of this
residual matroid. Every parent standard set is the unique minimum of
its residue-column basis weights at an interior generic weight. All such
sets contain \(C\); subtraction of its fixed moment makes their residual
profiles distinct exposed vertices of \(Q\). By (M13),
\[
 Q\subset[0,h(h+D-1)]\times[0,\Phi]\times[0,\Phi].         \tag{M14}
\]
For the second coordinate, for example, the sum over **all** residual
\(Y\)-labels is
\(\sum_{j<r}(j+1)(\deg g_j-\deg g_{j+1})=\Phi\).

Apply the classical Andrews vertex bound
\(f_0(P)=O_d(\operatorname{vol}(P)^{(d-1)/(d+1)})\) in
dimension three to \(P=Q+[0,1]^3\). This full-dimensional lattice
polytope has at least as many vertices as \(Q\): its normal fan
refines that of \(Q\). Its volume is \(O(h^2\Phi^2)\) by (M14).
This proves (M4), uniformly over all fields and all invariant factors.
The vertex estimate is due to
[Andrews, 1963](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1963-0143105-7);
[Dillon, Theorem 1](https://travisadillon.com/documents/volume_vs_vertex_in_lattice_polytopes.pdf)
gives an author-hosted modern proof. The application does not constitute
a new lattice-polytope vertex theorem.

Every standard set uses only the axis and at most \(r\) rows on each
branch. Its monomial ideal has at most \(2r+4\) minimal generators,
including the pure-axis head and \(yz\). Hence the union of all reduced
bases has at most \((2r+4)g(I)\) polynomials. The existing profile-edge
estimate also gives primitive wall cost \(O(hn^2)\), since each edge
has cost at most \(3n\). No optimal enumeration or bit-complexity
claim follows.

## Sharpness and the remaining problem

The existing [Pascal branch family](univariate_branch_gluing.md),
\[
 A=K[t]/(t^{2m}),\qquad x=t,\quad
 y=t^m(1+t)^m,\quad z=t^m(1+t)^{2m},
\]
has \(W\simeq B/(x^m)\), \(h=\Phi=m\), and
\(g(I)=\binom{m+2}{2}\) in characteristic zero. It belongs to
the minimum class, so the quadratic power in (M4) is sharp. Its
primitive wall cost has cubic order. In characteristic \(p\), the
same displayed family can instead collapse to three cones when
\(m\) is a power of \(p\); sharpness here is not an all-field lower
bound for this particular family.

For nonminimal quotients the branches need not have rigid staircases,
and \(A/W\) can have a changing planar fan. That additional freedom
is precisely what this proof does not control. The next general question
is whether the excess length \(n-2\Phi(W)\) bounds those changes well
enough for a joint estimate. A product of independent fan counts is not
a sharp collective argument.

The [previous gluing note](univariate_branch_gluing.md) compares the

[Ananthnarayan--Avramov--Moore, Sections 1--2](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~frankmoore/Papers/connected.pdf)

Those works already use fiber products and diagonal module quotients;
their additional Gorenstein or graded hypotheses must be retained.
No new gluing operation is asserted here.

[Khatami's survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08550), abstract and
Sections 1--4, studies the maximal commuting nilpotent Jordan type for
a fixed operator. The present optimization prescribes the module on
\(\ker y\) and minimizes the length of a cyclic two-operator algebra;
these are different formulations. This distinction alone does not establish
novelty: a theorem-level comparison with commuting-matrix and punctual
Hilbert-scheme results remains necessary. An unsuccessful keyword search
is not evidence of priority, significance or publishability.
