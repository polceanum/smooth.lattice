# Identical planar rank oracles can hide a linear fan-count gap on real points

For every integer \(q\ge2\), there are two radical ideals of the same
length \(n=2q\), over one real algebraic number field, with the following
properties. Both contain \(x^2-x\) and \(z-y\). Their underlying
algebras are the same split algebra, their \(y,z\) multiplication
operators agree, and their \(x\) operators have the same eigenvalue
multiplicities. The four labelled planar rank functions in the
[quadratic-coordinate reduction](quadratic_intersection_fans.md) agree
on **every** finite monomial set. Nevertheless their full fan counts are
\[
                      g(I_q)=4,\qquad g(J_q)=2q+2=n+2.       \tag{1}
\]
All point coordinates are real algebraic, and the \(y,z\) coordinates
are strictly positive. In the two nonzero rank-\(q\) planar modules,
any \(q\) distinct power columns are independent.

Consequently the four rank oracles, the length and the \(x\) Jordan
type alone cannot determine the fan count. A symmetric multiplicative
approximation based only on those data must allow factor at least
\[
                          \sqrt{(n+2)/4}.                  \tag{2}
\]
This is an information limit for those specified inputs, not a lower
bound for algorithms given the full quotient or its coefficients.
For the particular oracle data (7), this factor is sharp: every
compatible quotient with the stated idempotent coordinate has between
\(4\) and \(n+2\) cones, and both endpoints occur in (1).
It gives a reduced positive-real counterpart to the earlier nilpotent
equal-marginal control, with a larger count-ratio exponent. The older
control preserves certain coefficient ideals as well; that is not
asserted here. It does not improve unrestricted
\(G_3\), produce a supergeneric family, or establish exact novelty.

## The common positive point coordinates

Let \(T_d\) be the Chebyshev polynomial defined by
\(T_d(\cos\theta)=\cos(d\theta)\), and put
\[
 t_j=\cos\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{4q},\qquad
 y_j=z_j=2+t_j,\qquad 0\le j<2q.                         \tag{3}
\]
These are the distinct roots of \(T_{2q}\), translated by two.
They lie in \((1,3)\). Work over the real splitting field of
\(T_{2q}\), enlarged by \(\sqrt2\) if necessary. All coordinates
below belong to that one number field.

For the first configuration take
\[
 x_j=\frac{1+\sqrt2 T_q(t_j)}2\in\{0,1\}.                 \tag{4}
\]
Each value occurs \(q\) times. For the second take
\[
                  x'_j=\mathbf1_{t_j<0}.                  \tag{5}
\]
It also has \(q\) values of each kind. Let \(I_q,J_q\) be the
vanishing ideals of \((x_j,y_j,z_j)\) and \((x'_j,y_j,z_j)\).
Distinct \(y_j\)'s show that both algebras are the same product of
\(2q\) copies of the coefficient field. Both are reduced and are
generated by the \(y\) coordinate alone. Each is free of rank \(q\)
over \(K[x]/(x^2-x)\), since both idempotent eigenspaces have dimension \(q\).

The common polynomial \(T_{2q}(y-2)\) has simple roots. The identity
\(T_{2q}=2T_q^2-1\) shows directly that the first ideal is
\[
 I_q=(z-y,\ x^2-x,\ T_q(y-2)-(2x-1)/\sqrt2).              \tag{6}
\]
The two assignments of \(x\) are different couplings on the same
labelled \(y,z\) points; no change of the \(y,z\) operators is used.

## Why every one of the four rank oracles agrees

Write \(L=K[y,z]1\), \(U=K[y,z]x\), \(W=L\cap U\).
For both configurations \(L=A\), \(U=W\) has dimension \(q\),
and \(L/W\) has dimension \(q\). The fourth module \(U/W\) is zero.

A planar monomial \(y^a z^b\) has value \(y_j^{a+b}\).
On any subset of distinct positive nodes, power columns with distinct
nonnegative exponents form a generalized Vandermonde matrix of full
possible rank. Thus for every finite monomial set \(E\), putting
\(d(E)=|\{a+b:y^az^b\in E\}|\), the four ranks are exactly
\[
       \min(2q,d(E)),\quad \min(q,d(E)),\quad
                         \min(q,d(E)),\quad 0.             \tag{7}
\]
The same rule holds whichever \(q\) nodes carry the tag.
This proves equality of all the labelled rank data, and therefore of
their planar fans. It does not assert equality of the coefficient
ideals for the two selected node subsets.

Here is the elementary interpolation input in (7). A nonzero polynomial
with \(r\) nonzero terms has at most \(r-1\) distinct positive roots.
Divide out its lowest power and differentiate; Rolle's theorem gives
induction on \(r\). Hence an \(r\)-by-\(r\) power-evaluation matrix
at positive nodes is nonsingular. Rectangular ranks follow by choosing
square submatrices. This is classical interpolation, not a new
full-spark theorem.

## Two cones for the composed tag

First eliminate \(z\). Let \(s=w_x/w_y>0\). In (6), the leading
comparison in the last relation is \(y^q\) against \(x\).
If \(s<q\), its head and \(x^2\) leave exactly the \(2q\) monomials
\[
              \{y^j,xy^j:0\le j<q\}.                     \tag{8}
\]
If \(s>q\), eliminate \(x\); the pure \(y\) polynomial has degree
\(2q\), leaving \(\{1,y,\ldots,y^{2q-1}\}\).
In each case these head ideals are contained in the actual initial
ideal and have its exact colength, so they are equal. All lower terms
of \(T_q(y-2)\) have strictly smaller \(y\) cost. This proves the
whole planar fan, including that no other open cones occur.


## Every allocation occurs for the sign tag

Translation \(y\mapsto y+2\) preserves every monomial initial ideal:
the substitution changes a monomial only by proper divisors. We can
therefore work at the unshifted symmetric nodes \(\pm a_i\), with
\(a_i>0\) distinct, \(1\le i\le q\). Replacing \(x'\) by
\(u=1-2x'\) is likewise a triangular scalar translation and rescaling.
At these nodes \(u\) has values \(+1\) and \(-1\), matching the
sign of the node.

Every planar standard set must have the form
\[
 B_b=\{1,y,\ldots,y^{2q-b-1}\}
                 \sqcup\{x',x'y,\ldots,x'y^{b-1}\},
                    \qquad 0\le b\le q.                  \tag{9}
\]
Indeed \(x'^2-x'\) bounds the first exponent by one, and divisor
closure gives two nested rows. All these sets are independent.
To prove it, replace the second row by \(uy^j\); its difference
from the old one lies in the already present lower row. Add and
subtract the evaluation rows at \(a_i,-a_i\). Ordinary even powers
and tagged odd powers occupy one block; ordinary odd powers and
tagged even powers occupy the other. Both blocks have exactly \(q\)
columns, for either parity of \(b\). Within a block their exponents
are distinct: one source supplies even exponents, the other odd ones.
Each block is a generalized Vandermonde matrix at the positive
\(a_i\)'s, so both are invertible.

The basis moment cost of (9), up to a fixed constant, is
\[
                           b s+b^2-2qb.                  \tag{10}
\]
At \(s=2q-2b\) it differs from every other allocation by the
positive square \((j-b)^2\). For \(b=q\), take any sufficiently
small positive \(s\), and for \(b=0\) take \(s>2q-1\).
Thus every \(B_b\) is positively coherent, and (9) lists the whole
planar fan. It has exactly \(q+1\) cones.

There is also an exact rank contrast at every two-row candidate (9).
For the composed tag its rank is
\[
                         2q-\min(b,q-b),                 \tag{11}
\]
whereas for the sign tag it is \(2q\). To see (11), the composed
tag is a polynomial of degree \(q\) in \(y\). The lower row spans
all degrees below \(2q-b\); the remaining tagged powers have distinct
leading degrees \(q,\ldots,q+b-1\), all below \(2q\).
Exactly \(\max(0,2b-q)\) of them add new degrees. For even \(q\)
and \(b=q/2\), the rank gap is \(q/2=n/4\). For $1\le b\le q/2$ these candidates are strict ordinary geometric prefixes:
take $(w_x,w_y,w_z)=(4q-4b+1,2,8q^2)$. The last selected score is
$4q-2b-1$ and the next is $4q-2b$. Higher $x$ powers and every
$z$ monomial have larger scores. Thus the missing coupling can affect
greedy ranks linearly in length.

## Recover the full three-variable fans and the approximation limit

Because \(z=y\), at a generic positive weight all nonconstant
monomials use the cheaper of \(y,z\). Every planar cone occurs on
both sides of \(w_y=w_z\), with different standard monomial labels.
Every planar standard set includes a nonconstant power of that cheaper variable.
There are no further initial sets at the boundary, since a global
order chooses one of \(y,z\) first. Consequently each planar count
doubles, proving (1).

An estimator given only (7), the length and the common Jordan type
receives identical inputs on the two ideals. If it gives a number
\(v\) within a factor \(\alpha\) of both counts, then
\((n+2)/\alpha\le v\le4\alpha\), proving (2).
A randomized estimator with success probability greater than one half
on each input has the same obstruction: below that factor the two
allowed output intervals are disjoint, while its output distribution
is identical. Access to the missing coupling coefficients changes the
information model and is not covered by this conclusion.

The matching upper bound for this oracle equivalence class is elementary.
The first rule in (7) forces \(z\) to be a nonzero scalar multiple
of \(y\), and makes \(1,y,\ldots,y^{2q-1}\) a basis. The other
rules make both \(B_0\) and \(B_q\) independent, the latter by
projecting its lower row to \(L/W\). Idempotence and divisor closure
allow only the \(q+1\) candidates (9); (10) makes every independent
candidate coherent. Thus \(4\le g\le2q+2\). The estimate
\(2\sqrt{n+2}\) attains worst symmetric factor
\(\sqrt{(n+2)/4}\) on this entire equivalence class. No such upper
approximation guarantee is proved for arbitrary four-oracle inputs.

## Verification and priority boundary

~~~bash
python3 -B scripts/run_real_marginal_rank_checks.py
./build.sh
python3 tests/test_smoke.py
~~~

The checker uses exact cyclotomic number fields for small common
Chebyshev configurations, separately reconstructs their entire border
fans, tests their monomial rank oracles, and checks every original
S-pair. It also verifies the parity-block determinant argument and
Chebyshev polynomial identities independently. The uniform theorem
uses the proof; finite examples do not establish exact novelty.

Triangular translation is the established linear-shift invariance of
[Dimitrova--He--Robbiano--Stigler, Proposition 2.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.03680v2).
Chebyshev identities and roots are classical; see
[DLMF, Section 18.5](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5) and
[Section 18.16](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.16). Triangular translation,
the residue-matroid dictionary and the earlier equal-marginal
construction are existing inputs. The distinction from
[that nonflat family](planar_layer_rank_obstruction.md) is the reduced
positive-real setting, equality of all four rank oracles, and the
linear count ratio. No exact priority or standalone publication
significance is established. The examples stay far below the generic
\(n^2\log^2 n\) fan scale; the original extremal goal remains open.
