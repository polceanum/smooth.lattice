# Sharp quadratic fans from three univariate cross annihilators

Let \(A=K[x_1,x_2,x_3]/I\) have length \(n>0\), over any field.
Suppose monic univariate polynomials \(f_i(x_i)\), of positive degrees
\(d_i\), satisfy
\[
                 f_i(x_i)f_j(x_j)=0\quad\hbox{in }A
                         \qquad(i\ne j).                    \tag{U1}
\]
The individual \(f_i\)'s need not vanish. Put
\[
       S=d_1d_2d_3,\qquad R=d_1d_2+d_1d_3+d_2d_3.
\]
Then, uniformly in the coefficients, field and additional relations,
\[
 \begin{split}
 g(I)&=O\!\left((1+nR+S)^2(1+S)^4\right),\\
 V(I)&\le3n\,g(I),\\
 |\mathcal U(I)|&\le(3+S+R)\,g(I).
 \end{split}                                                \tag{U2}
\]
In particular, at fixed degrees the fan and full primitive-cost orders
are at most quadratic and cubic. Both powers are attained over
\(\mathbb Q\), already for the fixed choice
\[
                         (f_1,f_2,f_3)=(x^2,y,z).            \tag{U3}
\]
Sharp examples have no quadratic univariate relation in any coordinate:
their three minimal-polynomial degrees all tend to infinity.
The theorem therefore extends the earlier quadratic-coordinate class.
Sharpness of the reduced-basis union bound is not asserted.

Geometrically (U1) allows nonreduced quotients on the fixed union of
coordinate-parallel lines defined by the pairwise products. It includes
arbitrary quotients of every fixed one-dimensional monomial carrier.
It does not cover every fixed affine curve, arbitrary single-defect
surfaces, or unrestricted \(G_3\). Exact numerical priority is unassessed.

## The actual branch spaces have bounded total overlap

Write \(Y_i=f_iA\), and let \(C\) be the span of the \(S\) monomials
with exponents \(0\le a_i<d_i\). Successive monic division gives
\[
                           A=C+Y_1+Y_2+Y_3.               \tag{U4}
\]
As a \(K[x_i]\)-module, \(Y_i\) has at most \(d_jd_k\) generators:
use \(f_i x_j^a x_k^b\), with \(a<d_j,b<d_k\).
Terms divisible by either other \(f\) vanish after multiplication
by \(f_i\).

For the endomorphism \(f_i:Y_i\to Y_i\), rank-nullity gives
\[
 \dim\ker(f_i|Y_i)=\dim(Y_i/f_iY_i)\le d_i d_jd_k=S.        \tag{U5}
\]
The last quotient is generated by those \(d_jd_k\) vectors over
\(K[x_i]/(f_i)\). Neither splitting nor separability is needed.
Every element of \(Y_i\cap(Y_j+Y_k)\) is killed by \(f_i\), by (U1).
Consequently
\[
 \dim(Y_1+Y_2+Y_3)\ge\sum_i\dim Y_i-2S.                  \tag{U6}
\]
These are intersections in the same algebra. None of their vector
spans is being declared an algebra quotient.

## Four lists with bounded corank

The leading monomial of \(f_if_j\) is \(x_i^{d_i}x_j^{d_j}\) for
every global order. All initial standard sets therefore lie in
\[
 E=\{a:\prod_i(a_i+1)\le n,\quad
                  \#\{i:a_i\ge d_i\}\le1\}.              \tag{U7}
\]
Partition this ground into the core group \(E_0\), with all
\(a_i<d_i\), and three tails \(E_i\), with \(a_i\ge d_i\).
The residue matroid on their union has rank \(n\).

Let \(m_i\) be the column rank of group \(E_i\), including \(i=0\).
Monic division in \(x_i\) puts every tail column in \(Y_i+C\), so
\[
  m_0\le S,\qquad m_i\le\dim Y_i+S,\qquad
       0\le\delta:=\sum_{i=0}^3m_i-n\le6S.              \tag{U8}
\]
Here the last inequality uses (U6). It does not require the finite
tail ground to span the entire branch ideal. In the monomial case
\(f_i=x_i^{d_i}\), the sharper \(\delta\le3S\) follows directly.

First fix the internal cost orders of the four groups. Greedily compress
each group to its own ordered independent list. A rejected column is a
combination of cheaper columns of that same group and cannot be selected
by any global merged greedy scan with these internal orders.
The compressed union has \(M=n+\delta\) columns and rank \(n\).

Pass to its dual matroid, of rank \(\delta\). A minimum original basis
is the complement of a maximum-cost dual basis. Compress each of the
four lists again, this time by descending cost and dual independence.
Each resulting list has at most \(\delta\) elements. All global maximum
dual bases are now represented on a common ground of at most
\[
                               4\delta\le24S             \tag{U9}
\]
columns. The same cheaper-span argument, with the order reversed,
justifies this second compression.

This is greedy compression and matroid duality, not a new general
matching or matroid-optimization algorithm. It retains the joint rank
data which separate planar summaries can lose. In particular the bound
does not follow from the number of branch generators alone.

## Counting the internal orders and the remaining comparisons

Within tail \(i\), a difference of exponent labels has arbitrary
\(i\)-coordinate between \(-n\) and \(n\), and the other two coordinates
have absolute values less than \(d_j,d_k\). Thus all its internal
comparison planes number \(O(nd_jd_k)\). Core differences add \(O(S)\).
There is one common inventory of
\[
                              H=O(nR+S)                  \tag{U10}
\]
planes. Only mixed normals meeting the positive weight triangle matter;
counting the others as well only enlarges the bound.

Their arrangement has \(O((1+H)^2)\) open chambers, with all four
internal orders fixed on each. In one chamber, (U9) leaves
\(O((1+\delta)^2)\) pair comparisons. Their planar arrangement has
\(O((1+\delta)^4)\) cells. On each cell the maximum dual greedy
basis, hence the original standard set, is fixed.
Every open initial cone meets an open chamber of this finite inventory.
Multiplying these two bounds and using (U8) proves the first line of (U2).
Simultaneous or coincident equalities cannot increase the count.
If all three \(f_i\)'s are linear, the core has one column and every
tail is a single coordinate ray. All internal orders are fixed, so
the same argument gives \(g(I)=O(1)\), independently of \(n\).

The positive three-variable adjacency graph is planar and simple,
and every actual primitive wall cost is at most \(n\). This gives
the second line of (U2), retaining every undirected parallel piece.
The carrier in (U7) is a fixed finite box plus finitely many coordinate
rays. A standard downset cuts each ray at most once, so every initial
ideal has at most \(3+S+R\) minimal generators: three carrier heads,
at most \(S\) core points, and at most \(R\) ray frontiers. The same
fixed-carrier argument as in the
[curve-shadow theorem](curve_carrier_shadows.md) gives the last line.

At fixed degrees there is a finite exact enumeration in
\(O_{\boldsymbol d}(n^5)\) field-and-geometric-arithmetic operations:
enumerate the \(O_{\boldsymbol d}(n^2)\) internal-order chambers,
perform dense column elimination and dual compression in each, and
recover and merge the constant-size dual arrangements and their
original border cones. Preparing the \(O_{\boldsymbol d}(n)\) carrier
columns costs \(O_{\boldsymbol d}(n^3)\). This excludes coefficient
bits, an optimized implementation and any optimal-time assertion.

## A sharp family with growing coordinate degrees

Use the two length-\(2L\) planar branches in the
[zero-product sharp family](quadric_cross_fans.md#a-family-attaining-both-powers).
For clarity the formula below is independent of that section's core
length. Let \(J_L(x,t)\) be its planar ideal containing \(x^2\).
For \(s\ge3\), set
\[
 I_{L,s}=(x^s,\ x^2y,\ x^2z,\ yz,\ yJ_L(x,y),\ zJ_L(x,z)).
                                                               \tag{U11}
\]
Its vector space is the direct sum of the \(s\) core monomials
\(1,x,\ldots,x^{s-1}\), a tagged \(y\)-branch, and a tagged
\(z\)-branch, each of dimension \(2L\). The core columns are
coloops, and both branches are the previous planar quotient columns.
Multiplication by \(x\) on a branch squares to zero; multiplication
by \(y,z\) kills all core powers \(x^j\) with \(j\ge2\).
These rules commute and give the quotient in (U11).

Thus the entire fan is unchanged when the core is extended from two
to \(s\) columns. The exact product count and cost are
\[
 n=s+4L,\qquad g(I_{L,s})=(L+1)^2,\qquad
                      V(I_{L,s})=2L(L+1)^2.              \tag{U12}
\]
All pair products in (U3) vanish, although \(x^2\) itself does not.
The coordinate minimal polynomials have degrees
\[
                              s,\quad2L+1,\quad2L+1.     \tag{U13}
\]
The branch parameter has valuation one in its length-\(2L\) algebra,
which proves the last two degrees. Taking \(s=L+3\) makes all three
degrees grow with \(n\), while attaining both powers in (U2).
The other two four-monomial pair boxes have full rank four; this is
not a two-bilinear-defect example.
The lower family uses two-coordinate walls. No full-support-only
sharpness or sharp universal-basis lower order is asserted.

## A quantitative necessary condition for a local lower family

If \((x_ix_j)^D=0\) for all pairs, apply the theorem with
\(f_i=x_i^D\). For \(1\le D\le n\), it yields
\[
                             g(I)=O(n^2(1+D)^{16}).       \tag{U14}
\]
Consequently \(g(I_n)\ge c n^{2+\epsilon}\) in such a family
requires \(D=\Omega(n^{\epsilon/16})\), up to constants depending
on \(c,\epsilon\). For origin-supported quotients a suitable \(D\le n\)
always exists, but it need not be small. This is a necessary condition,
not a sufficient mechanism for a large fan.

In particular, bounded pair-product annihilation depth cannot give
the requested power separation, even when every coordinate's own
nilpotence index grows linearly. The unrestricted middle-cost problem,
general square-zero surfaces without these extra cross annihilators,
and arbitrary fixed curves remain open.

The overlap hypothesis cannot be inferred just from a short ray cover.
For \(I_n=((x-1)^n,xy-1,z)\), the core \(1\) and the two lists
\(x,\ldots,x^{n-1}\), \(y,\ldots,y^{n-1}\) span a rank-\(n\)
matroid on \(2n-1\) columns. Each list is independently of rank \(n-1\),
so their compressed corank is \(n-1\). This is a quotient of one fixed
curve with a two-ray carrier; its fan has exactly \(n\) Laurent hooks.
Thus (U8) uses actual annihilating operators, not merely leading
monomials or a fixed one-dimensional carrier.

## Inputs, verification and limits

Greedy basis selection, duality and the complement of a maximum dual
basis are classical; see the greedy setup in
[Eppstein, Section 1.2 and Section 4](https://ics.uci.edu/~eppstein/pubs/Epp-DCG-98.pdf).
The [Babson--Onn--Thomas state framework](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0207135)
is the existing link to monomial initials. Rank-nullity, monic division,
planar arrangements and the earlier product construction are the other
inputs. Exact priority of the bounded-overlap specialization and its
quantitative class bound remains unassessed.

Run

~~~bash
python3 -B scripts/run_univariate_cross_checks.py
~~~

The report is results/research/univariate_cross_checks.json. It checks
the branch-kernel and joint-rank bounds in actual algebras; compiles every
positive internal-order chamber by two independent compressions; checks
whole dual cone patches against original border normal forms; and compares
the merged complete fan with an independent direct traversal. Fixtures
include nonreduced coupling, nonlocal grid-line points, irreducible
univariates and the sharp family with extended core. Infinite bounds use
the proof, not fitted finite counts.

The sharp unrestricted \(G_3\) or actual polynomial-separation objective,
strongest-current priority comparison and honest-paper requirements are
unchanged. No restricted class theorem completes that objective.
