# A sharp quadratic fan bound from two interacting branch ideals

Let \(I\subset K[x,y,z]\) have positive colength \(n\), over any field.
Assume
\[
                  yz\in I,\qquad f(x)\in I,
 \quad f\text{ monic of degree two}.                       \tag{Q1}
\]
No reducedness, locality, flatness or generic-rank hypothesis is imposed.
In particular \(f=x^2\) is allowed.

**Theorem.** The number of monomial initial ideals satisfies
\[
                  g(I)\le 5+\frac5{16}(n+2)^2.             \tag{Q2}
\]
In fact the same bound holds for all order-ideal monomial vector
bases, whether coherent or not. Moreover
\[
             V(I)=O(n^3),\qquad |\mathcal U(I)|=O(n^2),    \tag{Q3}
\]
where \(V\) retains every undirected adjacency and its primitive
cost, and \(\mathcal U\) is the union of reduced Gröbner bases.
There are actual characteristic-zero \(x^2=0\) families of
lengths \(N=4L+2\) with
\[
             g(I_L)=(L+1)^2,\qquad
                         V(I_L)=2L(L+1)^2.                \tag{Q4}
\]
Thus both powers for \(g\) and \(V\) are sharp in this class.
Sharpness of the universal-basis upper bound is not asserted.

The proof is a joint-rank argument, not a reconstruction from
separate planar flags. The coupling can change the fan, but its
intersection rank bounds the possible branch cardinalities in
every monomial basis. This closes (Q1) as a polynomial-separation
route. It does not close arbitrary quotients with a single
bilinear defect, arbitrary square-zero quotients, or unrestricted
\(G_3\). Exact priority is unassessed.

## A small intersection from the shared multiplication

In \(A=K[x,y,z]/I\), put
\[
 Y=yA,\qquad Z=zA,\qquad C=\operatorname{span}_K\{1,x\}.
\]
Every monomial has a representative in one of these spaces, so
\[
                         A=C+Y+Z,\qquad \dim C\le2.       \tag{Q5}
\]
Because \(yz=0\), \(Y\) is generated by \(y,xy\) as a
\(K[y]\)-module. Its quotient \(Y/yY\) consequently has
dimension at most two. Rank-nullity for multiplication by \(y\)
on \(Y\) gives
\[
              \dim\ker(y:Y\longrightarrow Y)
                    =\dim(Y/yY)\le2.
\]
Every member of \(Y\cap Z\) is killed by \(y\), since it lies
in \(Z\). Hence
\[
                              \dim(Y\cap Z)\le2.          \tag{Q6}
\]
This uses the actual multiplication and intersection in one
quotient. The same conclusion follows symmetrically from \(Z\).

The map
\(Y\cap(Z+C)\longrightarrow(Z+C)/Z\) has kernel \(Y\cap Z\).
Its target has dimension at most two. Therefore
\[
                  d_Y:=\dim(Y\cap(Z+C))\le4.              \tag{Q7}
\]
Write \(m_Y=\dim Y\). In any monomial vector basis let \(t_Y\)
count the monomials with positive \(y\)-exponent. The other
columns lie in \(Z+C\). They vanish in the quotient by that
space, so the selected \(Y\) columns must span a quotient of
dimension \(m_Y-d_Y\). They are also independent in \(Y\).
Thus
\[
                        m_Y-4\le t_Y\le m_Y.             \tag{Q8}
\]
Only five integer branch sizes are possible, independently of
the coefficients, field and quotient length. One may use the
smaller actual \(d_Y\), but it is not needed for (Q2).

The same argument works with a degree-\(h\) relation in \(x\):
\(Y\) has \(h\) module generators, \(\dim(Y\cap Z)\le h\),
and the interval in (Q8) has length at most \(2h\).
The quadratic count below specifically uses \(h=2\).

## Counting the possible order-ideal bases

Since \(x^2\) and \(yz\) are leading monomials of elements of
\(I\), every initial standard set is contained in
\[
 \{1,x\}\ \cup\
 \{y^j,xy^j:j\ge1\}\ \cup\
 \{z^j,xz^j:j\ge1\}.                                    \tag{Q9}
\]
The same carrier is used when counting the stated order-ideal
vector bases. Here an order-ideal basis has the ordinary divisor
closure in the original monomials; arbitrary vector bases are
not being counted.

Its pure \(x\)-part is either \(\{1\}\) or \(\{1,x\}\).
If \(x\) is absent, no monomial involving \(x\) can occur.
Each choice of \(t_Y\) then determines the entire set, since
the \(z\)-part has length \(n-1-t_Y\). There are at most five
such bases by (Q8).

If \(x\) is present, the \(Y\)-part consists of
\[
                    y,\ldots,y^a,\quad xy,\ldots,xy^b,
              \qquad a\ge b\ge0,\quad a+b=t_Y.
\]
It has \(\lfloor t_Y/2\rfloor+1\) possible shapes.
The \(Z\)-part has
\(\lfloor t_Z/2\rfloor+1\) shapes, where
\(t_Z=n-2-t_Y\). Their product is at most
\[
 \left(\frac{t_Y}{2}+1\right)
 \left(\frac{t_Z}{2}+1\right)\le\frac{(n+2)^2}{16}.
\]
There are at most five choices of \(t_Y\). Adding the
case without \(x\) proves (Q2).

For clarity, every order-ideal vector basis does lie in (Q9),
not just every initial one. If it contained \(x^2\), it would
also contain \(1,x\), which are dependent together with \(x^2\)
by \(f\). If it contained \(yz\), its zero column would violate
independence. Divisor closure then excludes all their multiples.

## Adjacency cost and universal bases

The positive cone-adjacency graph is simple and planar in
three variables. It has at most \(3g(I)\) edges, including
lower-dimensional or one-cell cases. Every primitive wall
cost is at most \(n\) by the
[exact divisor threshold](primitive_wall_spectrum.md).
Consequently
\[
                             V(I)\le3n\,g(I),             \tag{Q10}
\]
which proves the first part of (Q3).

If \(x\) is standard, the complement of a set in (Q9) is
generated by a subset of
\[
        x^2,\ yz,\ y^{a+1},\ z^{c+1},\
                         xy^{b+1},\ xz^{d+1}.             \tag{Q11}
\]
If \(x\) is absent, use \(x,yz,y^{a+1},z^{c+1}\).
Thus each reduced basis has at most six polynomials. Taking
the union over all initials proves
\[
                           |\mathcal U(I)|\le6g(I).      \tag{Q12}
\]
This counts polynomials, not coefficient bits or arithmetic time.

The proof also gives a finite exact enumeration from commuting
coordinate matrices and a cyclic vector. Compute \(m_Y\), enumerate
the five possible \(t_Y\)'s and the shapes above, test their
column ranks, and recover each positive cone from border normal
forms. There are \(O(n^2)\) candidates, each with at most six
minimal border heads. Straight Gaussian elimination gives an
\(O(n^5)\) field-and-geometric-arithmetic bound, excluding bit
complexity. A refined implementation or matching running-time
lower bound is not asserted.

## A family attaining both powers

Use the established two-variable slab realization
\(J_L\subset\mathbb Q[x,t]\) of length \(2L\), containing \(x^2\).
Its positive standard sets are
\[
 E_b=\{1,t,\ldots,t^{2L-b-1}\}
                    \ \sqcup\ \{x,xt,\ldots,xt^{b-1}\},
                          \quad 0\le b\le L.             \tag{Q13}
\]
The second set is empty when \(b=0\).
One explicit realization in \(\mathbb Q[u]/u^{2L}\) is
\[
 x=u^L e^{(CL+1)u},\qquad
 t=u e^{(C+2L)u},\qquad C=2L(1+2L),                     \tag{Q14}
\]
with truncated exponentials. The
[ordered Hermite argument](nested_jet_matroids.md) shows every
size-\(2L\) downset in the two-layer slab is a basis.
The node encoding in (Q14) is its two-variable version.
The element \(t\) generates the whole algebra, so its length
is exactly \(2L\).

The successive walls of (Q13) have
\[
 q_b=(1,-(2L-2b-1)),\quad
              \nu(q_b)=2L-2b,\quad 0\le b<L.
\]
Thus \(g(J_L)=L+1\), and its planar weighted cost is
\(W(J_L)=L(L+1)\).
Apply the [common-coordinate product](wall_cost_products.md)
to two copies:
\[
 H_L=(x^2,\ yz,\ yJ_L(x,y),\ zJ_L(x,z)).                  \tag{Q15}
\]
Its length is \(N=2+4L\). Its fan is the exact Cartesian
product in the independent positive ratios \(w_x/w_y,w_x/w_z\),
so the count and cost identities give (Q4).
These quotients also have full-rank four-monomial boxes
\(\{1,x,y,xy\}\) and \(\{1,x,z,xz\}\): the core and
the two indicated branch columns are independent. Hence
the class is not reduced to the earlier theorem requiring
two deficient coordinate-pair boxes.

The sharp family uses two-coordinate walls. It proves sharp
total cost for the class, not a sharp lower order restricted
to full-support directions. The upper theorem itself includes
all remaining full-support directions and arbitrary coupling.

This class is also not explained by linear-box saturation.
In the sharp family the box with side counts \(2,L,2L+1\)
has \(2L(2L+1)=\Theta(N^2)\) monomials but rank \(N-2\).
It contains both core columns and a full \(z\)-branch basis.
Its \(y\)-branch contains the \(2L-2\) independent columns
represented by \(t^j,xt^j\), \(0\le j<L-1\).
Thus the largest nonsaturating box can be quadratic in the
length even though the whole fan has sharp quadratic order.

## Verification, established inputs and scope

Run

~~~bash
python3 -B scripts/run_quadric_cross_checks.py
~~~

The report is results/research/quadric_cross_checks.json.
It checks the branch intersection and rank window in actual
algebras, compares the constrained candidate list with independent
small downset enumeration, and recovers entire positive fans from
quotient columns. Examples include nonreduced cross-layer gluing,
nonlocal point quotients, an irreducible quadratic over \(\mathbb Q\),
and the exact sharp family.

The inputs are rank-nullity, cyclic module generation, divisor
closure, the state-polytope correspondence and planar adjacency
bound, the exact primitive threshold, and the previously established
slab and fan-product constructions. The new argument here uses
the intersection of the actual branch ideals to restrict all
monomial bases. Its precise priority remains unassessed; no
new general module or gluing theory is claimed.

This is a class theorem with a further universal-basis and
enumeration consequence. General quotients with \(x^2=0\)
need not satisfy \(yz=0\); a single bilinear defect with no
quadratic bound on the remaining coordinate is also outside
(Q1). The unrestricted middle-cost budget, sharp \(G_3\),
current-priority audit and original publication gates remain open.
