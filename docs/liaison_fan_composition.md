# Liaison addition and the cost of composing quotient fans

The single-higher-interaction family is now
[closed linearly](higher_seed_transport.md). A possible next lower-bound
mechanism is to combine two genuinely three-variable quotient fans.
There is an exact algebraic construction, but its operation and length
formula belong to classical liaison addition. Its fan is a **common
refinement**, not an unrestricted product of branch choices.

Two quantitative checks below reject immediate versions of this route:
arbitrarily many ordinary box-prefix fans still have only the generic
power scale after refinement, and the balanced local-rectangle gluing
costs too much length. No unrestricted $G_3$ improvement or publication
claim follows. The construction remains available if branches with a
different quantitative mechanism can be supplied.

## An exact finite affine construction

Work in $R=K[x,y,z]$ over any field. Let $f(x),g(y),h(z)$ be monic
polynomials of positive degrees $a,b,c$. Let $J_1,J_2$ have positive
finite colengths $m_1,m_2$, with
$$
(g,h)\subseteq J_1,\qquad (f,h)\subseteq J_2.
$$
Put
$$
H=(h,fg)+fJ_1+gJ_2. \tag{L1}
$$

**Proposition 1.** Its length is
$$
N=abc+m_1+m_2. \tag{L2}
$$
For every global monomial order,
$$
\operatorname{in}H=(z^c,x^ay^b)
 +x^a\operatorname{in}J_1+y^b\operatorname{in}J_2. \tag{L3}
$$
Thus, if $D_i$ are the branch standard sets, the full standard set is
$$
\{x^iy^jz^k:i<a,j<b,k<c\}
\ \sqcup\ x^aD_1\ \sqcup\ y^bD_2. \tag{L4}
$$

**Proof.** In $S=R/(h)$, the monic polynomials $f,g$ form a regular
sequence and $(f)\cap(g)=(fg)$. The ideal $(f,g)/(fg)$ is the
direct sum of the submodules generated by $f$ and $g$.
Quotienting these by $fJ_1$ and $gJ_2$ gives an exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow R/J_1\oplus R/J_2
 \xrightarrow{(u,v)\mapsto fu+gv}R/H
 \longrightarrow R/(f,g,h)\longrightarrow0. \tag{L5}
$$
For instance, if $fu+gv$ belongs to $fJ_1+gJ_2$ modulo $h$,
subtract those representatives. The resulting equality between an
$f$-multiple and a $g$-multiple forces the first coefficient into
$(g)$ and the second into $(f)$. These ideals are already contained
in $J_1,J_2$, respectively, proving injectivity. The last quotient
has the monic box basis of size $abc$, which proves (L2).

All the generators on the right of (L3) are leading monomials of
elements of $H$. Its proposed standard set is (L4): $D_1$ has
$y$-exponent below $b$ and $z$-exponent below $c$, and $D_2$ has
$x$-exponent below $a$ and $z$-exponent below $c$. The displayed
regions are disjoint and have total size (L2). Inclusion of finite
colength monomial ideals and equality of lengths prove (L3).
$\square$

For positive weights the fan is therefore exactly the common
refinement of the two branch fans. The two branch standard sets
can be recovered separately from (L4), so no distinct realizable
pair is lost. Pairs whose open cones do not intersect are absent.
In particular $g(H)$ need not equal $g(J_1)g(J_2)$.

For example take both branches equal to
$$
J=(x^2,y^2,z-x-y-xy),\quad a=b=2,\quad c=3.
$$
Here $z^3\in J$, each branch has length four, and $H$ has length
twenty. Its entire fan is the branch fan, since refining a fan by
itself changes nothing. In characteristic zero it has six initials,
rather than thirty-six; in characteristic two the branch and glued
fans both have four. This is a finite illustration of (L3), not a
growth estimate.

## The operation and length are established prior art

[Hartshorne--Migliore--Nagel (2008)](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~jmiglior/HMN.pdf),
Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2(a), recall liaison addition and its
Hilbert-function formula, using the displayed exact sequence in
the proof. Homogenize $h$ to a hypersurface in projective
three-space, homogenize the branch point schemes, and choose the
two forms to be the homogenizations of $g\in J_1$ and $f\in J_2$.
Their affine chart gives exactly (L1). The complete intersection
has degree $abc$ and no point at infinity, so the degree identity
gives (L2). The finite affine proof above also works over finite
fields and without invoking projective saturation.
Only the cited definition, formula and displayed exact-sequence argument
were inspected; the proof delegated there to reference [15] was not audited.

The published construction and additive length must not be claimed
as discoveries here. Formula (L3) uses the separate univariate
heads and a length comparison. Its exact prior-art coverage has
not been established; its elementary derivation alone is not a
paper-level novelty claim. No assertion is made about arbitrary
liaison-addition fans without these hypotheses.

A recent related classification is
[Jonsson Kling--Lundqvist--Mohammadi--Orth, arXiv:2506.24028v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.24028).
Its characteristic-zero convention in Section 2, Theorem 4.11 and
Corollary 4.14, with their proofs, were inspected. Pure variable powers
together with a power of their sum have at most $d!$ reduced bases in
$d$ variables. This is a restricted homogeneous class, not the
unrestricted three-variable counting theorem sought here. The underlying
explicit basis construction and the full paper were not independently audited.

## Ordinary prefix fans cannot multiply into a power gain

Let $B$ be a product of three integer intervals, each finite or
bounded below and infinite above. Choose the $m$ cheapest lattice
points of $B$ at a generic positive linear price, assuming enough
points exist. Coordinate translations of $B$ do not affect its
comparison directions.

**Lemma 2.** Every primitive direction $v$ of an active wall obeys
$$
\prod_{i=1}^3(|v_i|+1)\le2m. \tag{L6}
$$

**Proof.** Choose a weight in the relative interior of the wall
whose integer orthogonal relations are precisely the multiples of
$v$. The threshold-tied points lie on one lattice line. There are
two consecutive tied points $p,p+v$ in $B$: the product-box
condition includes all intermediate lattice points on the segment.
The coordinate box between these two points lies in $B$ and has
$P=\prod_i(|v_i|+1)$ lattice points.
Reflection in its center pairs prices to twice the threshold.
Exactly two of its points are tied: the two endpoints, since $v$
is primitive and all ties are on that line. Its other points split
equally above and below the threshold. The $(P-2)/2$ strictly
cheaper points belong to a set of at most $m-1$ points. Hence
$P\le2m$. $\square$

**Corollary 3.** The common refinement of any finite collection of
such prefix fans, each selecting at most $M$ points, has
$$
O(M^2\log^4(M+1)) \tag{L7}
$$
positive cells, independently of the number of fans or their box
dimensions and translations.

Indeed the signed integer product bound (L6) allows only
$O(M\log^2(M+1))$ primitive wall planes. Their lines in the positive
weight simplex form at most quadratically many regions. Every
common-refinement cell is a union of these arrangement regions.
The divisor sum and plane-arrangement count are classical.

This applies to gluing branches whose actual fans are the ordinary
box-prefix fans, including the previously proved generic torus-grid
graph class in its stated characteristic range. Merely changing
their caps or adding more such components cannot produce a fixed
power above the generic scale. It does not apply to arbitrary
dependent interpolation columns or the local nonlinear rectangles.

## Local moment budgets survive composition

Let the branches be local algebras supported at the origin and set
$$
S_i=\sum_{j\ge0}j\dim_K(\mathfrak m_i^j/\mathfrak m_i^{j+1}).
$$
The existing determinant-filtration argument bounds the total
degree of every branch monomial basis by $S_i$.

**Proposition 4.** Under the hypotheses of Proposition 1,
$$
g(H)=O((1+S_1+S_2)^{3/2}). \tag{L8}
$$

**Proof.** Let $P_i$ be the convex hull of profiles of branch
initial standard sets. Each belongs to the nonnegative simplex
of coordinate sum at most $S_i$. Formula (L4) identifies every
glued profile with a fixed translate of a sum of two such profiles.
For a strict common positive weight, its two summand profiles
are uniquely minimal. Thus the sum is a vertex of $P_1+P_2$.
Two different realized pairs cannot give the same vertex: at a
strict exposing weight, any decomposition into summand points
must use the same two minimizing points. Initial standard sets
are likewise determined by their strictly exposed profiles.
Hence $g(H)\le f_0(P_1+P_2)$.

The sum lies in the simplex of total coordinate sum at most
$S_1+S_2$. Adding the unit cube handles smaller affine dimension
without decreasing the number of vertices. The classical
three-dimensional lattice-vertex bound, in the same form used by
the existing state-polytope proof, is (L8). This is a cardinality
bound, not an arithmetic running-time bound. $\square$

The fixed box and tag profiles in (L4) are translations. They
must not be charged again as varying moments, even when their
coordinate sums are large.

In particular take arbitrary $F\in(y,z)$ and $G\in(x,z)$, and
$$
J_1=(y^q,z^q,x-F(y,z)),\qquad
J_2=(x^q,z^q,y-G(x,z)).
$$
Choose $f=x^q,g=y^q,h=z^q$. Then
$$
N=q^3+2q^2,\qquad S_1=S_2=q^2(q-1),\qquad
g(H)=O(q^{9/2})=O(N^{3/2}). \tag{L9}
$$
The maximal ideals of the branch algebras are generated by their
two rectangular coordinates, regardless of the nonlinear coefficients.
Their Hilbert functions give the displayed moments over every field.
Thus this coupling of two arbitrary balanced nonlinear rectangles
is already below the target scale; enumerating larger glued fans
cannot rescue it. No count for an individual unrestricted nonlinear
rectangle is inferred.

## Decision and verification

The construction provides an exact way to preserve commuting cyclic
multiplication while composing fans. Its algebraic operation and
length are classical, and the most immediate branch choices fail
the quantitative gate above. Retain it as a tool; do not build a
larger generic branch-search framework around those excluded choices.
A useful next lower construction needs non-prefix dependencies,
a moment and tag budget compatible with its length, and a uniform
count of actual cone intersections. The unrestricted bound, its
strongest-current literature comparison and the original impact
and publication requirements remain open.
