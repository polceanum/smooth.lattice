# A compulsory core for repeated parallel walls

The [primitive-wall threshold](primitive_wall_spectrum.md) can be used
inside one quotient, rather than only as a union over different ideals.
Let \(I\subset K[x,y,z]\) have colength \(n\), over any field. For a
primitive mixed vector \(q\), put

\[
 u=q^+,\quad v=q^-,\quad
 C_q=(\operatorname{Div}(u)\cup\operatorname{Div}(v))
                     \setminus\{u,v\},\qquad
 \nu(q)=|C_q|+1,\qquad r=n-\nu(q)+1.
 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(p_I(q)\) count distinct positive codimension-one walls normal to
\(q\), up to sign. Different pieces separated by other cones count
separately. They are dual to different profile edges parallel to \(q\).
Write \(L_r=1+\log(r+1)\).

**Theorem 1.** If \(p_I(q)>0\), then \(r\ge1\). Every standard set on
either side of every such wall contains the *same literal monomial
set* \(C_q\). The independent columns of that set can be contracted
once. All these walls survive in a single projected rank-\(r\)
matroid on at most six translates of

\[
             V_r^3=\{a\ge0:\prod_i(a_i+1)\le r\}.           \tag{2}
\]

In particular,

\[
 p_I(q)=O\!\left(
  \min\{r^{4/3}L_r^2,\ r(1+\|q\|_1^{1/3})L_r\}\right).
 \tag{3}
\]

The constants are absolute. At \(r=1\), the sharper exact conclusion
is \(p_I(q)\le1\).

The \(4/3\) exponent in the first estimate is the published general
parametric-matroid exponent, not a new geometric bound. The additional
reduction here is the fixed compulsory core and its rank-sized
translated ground. Its precise priority remains unassessed.
Neither (3) nor the partial count below improves the current
unrestricted \(G_3(n)\) theorem or completes the active goal.

## The same core is present on both sides

At a relative interior point of a \(q\)-wall, its degeneration is
\(\mathbb Z^3/\mathbb Zq\)-homogeneous. Both endpoint standard sets
have the same graded Hilbert function. As proved in the threshold
note, \(u,v\) are nonzero in this degeneration, and all their divisors
are nonzero.

Every element of \(C_q\) has a singleton grading fiber in the *entire*
nonnegative monomial lattice. A proper divisor of \(u\) cannot be
shifted by any nonzero multiple of \(q\) without acquiring a negative
coordinate; the same holds for a proper divisor of \(v\). Since each
of these degree spaces is nonzero, its unique monomial must be in
both standard sets. This gives containment of \(C_q\), not just a
lower bound on their cardinalities.

This conclusion concerns the actual standard sets of \(I\). Thus their
common core columns are independent in \(K[x,y,z]/I\), independently
of which parallel wall is being considered. No ideal structure is
claimed for their linear span, and the contraction below is a vector
matroid contraction rather than an algebra quotient.

## A small translated ground without splitting the rank

For a finite downset \(C\), define the relative divisor ground

\[
 E(C,r)=\{a\notin C:|\operatorname{Div}(a)\setminus C|\le r\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

If a downset \(B\supset C\) has \(|B\setminus C|=r\), then
\(B\setminus C\subset E(C,r)\). If \(T(C)\) is the minimal monomial
complement of \(C\), then

\[
               E(C,r)\subseteq\bigcup_{h\in T(C)}(h+V_r^3).
 \tag{5}
\]

Indeed choose \(h\le a\) minimal outside \(C\). Every monomial in
the interval \([h,a]\) is outside \(C\), and its size is
\(\prod_i(a_i-h_i+1)\le r\).

For \(C=C_q\), \(|T(C)|\le6\). With full support, permute variables
and the sign so that \(u=x^a\), \(v=y^bz^c\). The complement is
generated by a subset of

\[
                  x^a,\ xy,\ xz,\ y^{b+1},\
                         z^{c+1},\ y^bz^c.               \tag{6}
\]

If one coordinate of \(q\) is zero, a subset of
\(x^a,y^b,xy,z\) suffices. Redundant generators in these lists can
be discarded. Thus the ground in (5) has
\(O(rL_r^2)\) elements, independent of the sizes of the translations.
The ordinary divisor estimate
\(|V_r^3|\le r(1+\log r)^2\) suffices.

Take the residue-column matroid on \(C_q\cup E(C_q,r)\), and contract
the independent columns \(C_q\). The resulting matroid \(\mathcal N\)
has rank exactly \(r\): an endpoint standard set supplies a residual
basis of that size. Every basis of \(\mathcal N\), lifted together
with \(C_q\), is a vector basis of the original quotient.

Let \(P\) be the convex hull of its exponent-sum profiles. At each
original positive \(q\)-wall the two endpoint residual bases minimize
the same objective on \(P\). Any lifted residual basis is a monomial
vector basis, so it cannot beat a global initial standard set at a
positive objective. The exposed face is exactly the original profile
edge, translated by the fixed core moment: it contains both endpoints
and is contained in the original minimizing profile segment.

For that containment, take a sufficiently small neighborhood of a
relative interior point of the given wall. Its global standard sets
are precisely the two adjacent ones, both containing the core and
lifting from the contraction. Thus the restricted and global support
minima agree on this neighborhood: lifted bases cannot improve the
global minimum, and one of these two bases always attains it.
Local agreement of support functions makes their exposed faces at
the wall point agree as well, by directional derivatives. Extra labels
cannot add points outside the original minimizing segment. This
agreement is local to the wall; forcing the core can strictly increase
the minimum elsewhere in the positive orthant.

The relative ground may contain labels outside \(V_n^3\). This causes
no problem: the preceding positive-cost minimum argument works on
any finite monomial ground containing the standard basis. It does
not apply the original hyperbolic \(n\)-bound to those extra labels.

Each such edge collapses to a vertex in the projection along \(q\).
Over one projected vertex the fiber is a point or a segment, so
different parallel edges cannot share that projected vertex. Hence

\[
                         p_I(q)\le f_0(\pi_qP).            \tag{7}
\]

This uses one common contraction. It does not add polytopes of
restrictions to the six translated pieces.

## Two estimates for that one shadow

For a rank-\(r\) matroid with \(m\) arbitrary planar labels, the
published parametric bound is \(O(mr^{1/3})\). Apply it in four
objective quadrants to the projected labels. Each quadrant is a
linear one-parameter cost problem. Degeneracies do not increase the
number of exposed profiles: a small generic perturbation preserves
chosen strict profile exposures. Since \(m=O(rL_r^2)\), (7) gives
the first estimate in (3).

There is also a translation-invariant version of the repository's
[rational-shadow estimate](rational_shadow_bound.md). If labels are
in a union of \(t\) integer translates of \(V_r^3\), their rank-\(r\)
projected profile polygon has

\[
 O\!\left(t\,r(1+\Delta^{1/3})L_r\right)                  \tag{8}
\]

vertices, where \(\Delta\) is the normalized minor sum of the
rank-two integer projection. To check the extension, translate the
dyadic parallelogram cover separately at each of the \(t\) anchors.
Its areas and determinant budgets are unchanged. There are still
only \(O(tL_r)\) distinct boundary lines, shifted by integer
determinant levels. Each of the \(r\) convex token paths crosses
each such line at most twice. Internal exchanges retain the same
two coordinate-variation budgets; their weighted dyadic sum is
multiplied by at most \(t\). This is the earlier proof with translated
labels, without replacing the rank by a sum of restriction ranks.
At \(r=1\) the ground has at most \(t\) labels and is immediate.

Choose an integer projection with primitive kernel \(q\).
Its normalized minor sum is \(\Delta=\|q\|_1\). Substituting
\(t\le6\) in (8) proves the other estimate in (3).

At \(r=1\), the minimal-length rigidity theorem fixes the two
endpoint standard sets as \(D\setminus\{u\}\) and
\(D\setminus\{v\}\). Their convex initial cones have only one
common codimension-one face. Thus \(p_I(q)\le1\).

## A uniform count near saturation of the wall budget

Let \(1\le R\le n\), and count initial cells incident to a positive
wall satisfying

\[
                         n-R<\nu(q)\le n.                 \tag{9}
\]

Over every field the number of these walls, and of their incident
initial cells, is at most

\[
            O\!\left(nR^{7/3}\log(n+1)L_R^2\right).        \tag{10}
\]

There are \(O(nR\log(n+1))\) candidate directions in this band.
For full support write \(q=(a,-b,-c)\); at each fixed \(b,c\),
the interval for \(a\) has at most \(R\) integers, while
\((b+1)(c+1)\le n+1\) gives \(O(n\log(n+1))\) pairs.
The two-coordinate directions contribute \(O(nR)\).
For each candidate (9) gives \(r\le R\). Apply the first part of
(3), sum, and count at most two endpoint cells per wall. This
proves (10); counting one cell several times only increases the bound.

For \(R=\lfloor n^{3/4}\rfloor\), this is
\(O(n^{11/4}\log^3(n+1))\). At \(R=1\), rigidity improves the
reason to the elementary \(O(n\log(n+1))\) count of saturated
directions with at most one wall each.

This also controls part of the
[collective wall-cost budget](free_wall_threshold.md). Define
\(V_R(I)\) by summing \(\nu(q)\) only over the walls in (9),
once per undirected adjacency. Since every summand is at most \(n\),

\[
                V_R(I)=O(n^2R^{7/3}\log(n+1)L_R^2).
 \tag{15}
\]

In particular the cubic polylogarithmic budget is proved for the
entire part with \(r\le\lfloor n^{3/7}\rfloor\):
\(V_R(I)=O(n^3\log^3(n+1))\). This does not control the
complementary walls and is not the conjectured bound on all of \(V(I)\).

These are partial fan counts with an explicitly checked membership
condition. Many walls have \(\nu(q)\) a fixed fraction of \(n\),
and need not satisfy (9) for sublinear \(R\). The bound therefore
does not replace the unrestricted theorem or close the collective
square-root-incidence argument.

## Both ends of the cost range are controlled

The low-cost direction estimate in the
[wall-cost reduction](free_wall_threshold.md) counts
\(O(nT^{7/3}\log^2(n+1))\) walls of cost \(\nu(q)\le T\), and at
most twice that many incident cells. It follows directly by summing
the arithmetic shadow envelope over the \(O(T^2\log(T+1))\)
directions in the exact spectrum. Here \(\|q\|_1\le\nu(q)\le T\).

Combining this with (10), all cells incident to any wall with

\[
 \nu(q)\le\lfloor n^{3/7}\rfloor
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 \nu(q)>n-\lfloor n^{3/7}\rfloor
\]

number \(O(n^2\log^3(n+1))\). Thus a polynomial excess above
the generic quadratic power must occur among cells whose every
incident wall has intermediate cost. This is an exclusion for
two entire regions of any actual fan, not just a finite example.

There is a corresponding two-sided localization of the proposed
cubic budget on \(V(I)=\sum_e\nu(q_e)\). The low-cost portion is at
most \(O(nT^{10/3}\log^2(n+1))\), by multiplying its wall count
by \(T\). Take

\[
               T=\lfloor n^{3/5}\rfloor,\qquad
               R=\lfloor n^{3/7}\rfloor .
\]

Both the part with \(\nu(q)\le T\) and the part with
\(\nu(q)>n-R\) have total cost \(O(n^3\log^3(n+1))\).
Consequently only the middle range

\[
                  n^{3/5}<\nu(q)\le n-n^{3/7}
 \tag{16}
\]

up to integer rounding remains in this particular cubic-budget
proof obligation. No estimate for that middle range follows from
the contraction theorem. The statement retains the actual
undirected adjacency multiplicities; it does not replace them by
distinct directions.

## Parallel multiplicity can grow with the residual rank

The residual parameter cannot simply be discarded. For \(m\ge1\),
set \(a=m^4+1,\ b=m^4+2\), and use maximal monomials

\[
 x^a,\quad y^b,\quad
 t_i=x^iz^{\,2m-i+\mathbf1_{i<m}},\quad 0\le i<2m.
 \tag{11}
\]

Let \(D\) be the union of their divisor sets. In the monomial
quotient on \(D\), impose the disjoint socle relations

\[
              x^a-y^b,\qquad t_i-t_{2m-1-i},
                                  \quad 0\le i<m.        \tag{12}
\]

This is an actual cyclic commuting quotient over every field.
There are \(a+b+1\) points on the \(x,y\) axes, and
\(\sum_i(2m-i+\mathbf1_{i<m})=2m^2+2m\) further points with
positive \(z\). The \(m+1\) independent relations give

\[
              n=a+b+2m^2+m,\qquad
              r=n-\nu(a,-b,0)+1=2m^2+m+1.                \tag{13}
\]

The base direction is primitive since \(a,b\) are consecutive.
The other differences are \((d,0,-d-1)\) for the distinct odd
integers \(d=1,3,\ldots,2m-1\). Along the positive plane
\(a\alpha=b\beta\), their comparisons change at the \(m\) distinct
ratios \(\gamma/\alpha=d/(d+1)\). All residue classes choose
their cheaper representative independently. Thus the complete
positive fan has \(2(m+1)\) cells and

\[
                    p_I(a,-b,0)=m+1=\Theta(\sqrt r).      \tag{14}
\]

The family is already in the bounded binomial/socle class. Its small
fan supplies no supergeneric lower bound. It verifies that repeated
parallel walls survive in actual algebras even when
\(n-\nu(q)=\Theta(\sqrt n)\).

## Every planar fan occurs as repeated parallel walls

The preceding square-root example is not a linear ceiling in the
residual rank. There is a uniform embedding of an arbitrary planar
ideal that makes this obstruction larger.

Let \(J\subset K[x,z]\) have positive colength \(R\). Take a monic
\(f(x)\in J\) of degree \(s\le R\), and integers \(a\ge R+1\),
\(b\ge1\) with \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). Such \(f\) exists by the minimal
polynomial of multiplication by \(x\). Put
\[
 F(x)=x^{a-s}f(x),\qquad
 H=(xy,yz,xF(x),y^{b+1},F(x)-y^b,zJ)
                       \subset K[x,y,z].                 \tag{E1}
\]
Here \(F\) is monic of degree \(a\), belongs to \(J\), and has zero
constant term.

**Theorem 2.** Over every field, \(H\) has length
\[
 N=a+b+R,\qquad \nu(a,-b,0)=a+b,\qquad r=R+1.              \tag{E2}
\]
For every global monomial order,
\[
 \operatorname{in}H=
 (xy,yz,x^{a+1},y^{b+1},z\operatorname{in}J,
                       \operatorname{in}(x^a-y^b)).       \tag{E3}
\]
Consequently its full positive fan has exactly \(2g(J)\) cells,
and its fixed direction \(q=(a,-b,0)\) has exactly
\[
                         p_H(q)=g(J)                     \tag{E4}
\]
parallel wall pieces.

Here is the dimension argument, including the nonlocal case.
In \(S=K[x,z]/(xF,zJ)\), multiplication by \(z\) gives the exact
sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow K[x,z]/J
 \xrightarrow{\ \cdot z\ }S
 \longrightarrow K[x]/(xF)\longrightarrow0.               \tag{E5}
\]
Indeed \(xF\in J\). If \(zh=xFg+zj\), evaluation at \(z=0\)
forces \(g\) to be divisible by \(z\); cancellation then gives
\(h\in J\). Thus \(\dim S=R+a+1\).
The augmentation \(x=z=0\) exists even when \(J\) is nonlocal.
Forming its fiber product with \(K[y]/(y^{b+1})\) gives the
quotient before imposing \(F-y^b\), of dimension \(R+a+b+1\).
The two nonzero, independent classes \(F,y^b\) lie in its socle:
\(xF=zF=0\), and \(yF=0\) because \(F(0)=0\).
Their difference spans a one-dimensional ideal. This proves (E2).

All proposed heads in (E3) belong to \(\operatorname{in}H\).
For the last head, every lower term of \(F\) is cheaper than \(x^a\)
under any global order. If \(D\) is the standard set of \(J\), the
proposed standard set consists of
\[
 C_q\ \sqcup\ \{\text{the cheaper of }x^a,y^b\}
       \ \sqcup\ \{x^i z^{j+1}:(i,j)\in D\}.              \tag{E6}
\]
These regions are disjoint. Standard exponents of a length-\(R\)
planar ideal have \(i\le R-1<a\), so the pure-power heads do not
truncate the tagged region. Its total size is \(N\). Inclusion
of initial ideals and equality of colengths prove (E3).

Each planar cone gives an interval of positive ratios \(w_x/w_z\).
Choosing \(w_y\) above or below \(a w_x/b\) realizes both base
choices independently on that interval. Their standard sets recover
both the planar set and the base choice, so no pair is identified.
The plane \(a w_x=b w_y\) cuts each planar cone in one connected
piece. Planar wall directions have zero \(y\)-coordinate and
cannot equal \(q\); hence there are exactly the \(g(J)\) pieces
in (E4), with no additional ones.

Over \(\mathbb Q\), choose \(J\) as the ideal of the \(R\) points
\((j,j^R)\), \(1\le j\le R\). On the planar universal ground,
the codes \(i+Rj\) are distinct. Generalized Vandermonde
independence at positive nodes therefore gives the usual generic
corner-cut fan, with \(g(J)=\Theta(R\log(R+1))\).
This lower count is the established planar input, not a new planar
result. Taking \(a=R^2+1,b=R^2+2\) proves
\[
 p_H(q)=\Theta(r\log(r+1)),\quad
 N=2R^2+R+3,\quad N-\nu(q)=R.                             \tag{E7}
\]
The available upper bound is still (3).
Thus even a uniform \(O(r)\) bound for one direction is false.
The transferred fan itself has only \(O(R\log(R+1))\) cells and
is not a supergeneric \(G_3\) lower family.

The construction uses classical tagged-module and socle gluing
mechanisms, as in the [liaison note](liaison_fan_composition.md).
Exact priority of this all-order embedding and its quantitative
consequence remains unassessed.

In fact the superlinear lower bound itself is already implied by the
[earlier common-coordinate fan product](extremal_initial_ideals.md#an-exact-product-construction-reduces-a-lower-bound-to-the-plane).
To see this, combine \(J\) with
\(J_1=(x-y^b,y^{b+1})\), which has length \(b+1\) and two initials
separated by \(q=(1,-b,0)\). The common monic polynomial
\(x^2f(x)\) belongs to both branches. That product has length
\(N'=b+R+s+3\), exactly \(2g(J)\) initials, and \(g(J)\)
parallel \(q\)-walls. Its compulsory-core residual rank is
\(R+s+3\le2R+3\). Thus the established planar lower count already
excludes an \(O(r)\) cap. The exact residual \(R+1\) and formula
(E3) are supplied by the sharper construction above; the asymptotic
obstruction must not be represented as an independent new discovery.

This changes the next proof obligation:
the cubic total-cost route must handle genuinely growing parallel
multiplicity, or exploit constraints shared by different directions.

## Classical inputs, priority and verification

[Dey, *Improved Bounds for Planar k-Sets and Related Problems*](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/PL00009354.pdf),
Section 4.1, Theorem 4.1 and the following matroid application supply
the parametric exponent. The displayed polygon bound and its proof
were inspected. [Eppstein, Section 4, Theorem 4](https://ics.uci.edu/~eppstein/pubs/Epp-DCG-98.pdf)
supplies the convex-token reduction and perturbation argument; its
statement and proof were rechecked. No new parametric-matroid
bound is claimed. The relative divisor cover is an elementary
downset argument, and matroid contraction and profile projection are
classical. Exact priority of the compulsory-core reduction and its
coexistence consequences remains open.

Run from the repository root:

    python3 -B scripts/run_wall_coexistence_checks.py
    ./build.sh
    python3 tests/test_smoke.py

The checker tests common-core containment on all relevant downset
pairs through length eight, verifies the translated cover independently,
and contracts actual saved quotient matrices. Exact planar support
enumeration recovers each residual shadow and checks every saved
parallel edge and its projected vertex. Bounded direct enumerations
check that support procedure separately. The explicit infinite
family is replayed through sparse commuting multiplication and its
entire positive fan. The planar embedding is checked on both local and
nonlocal ideals, including a linear \(y\)-relation and longer compulsory
cores. Independent integer multiplication and generator annihilations
verify the constructed quotient. Every planar staircase is tested by
field elimination; its embedded cones are then compared in full with
border relations recovered from the three-variable quotient columns.
The complete cones have total normalized area one and the predicted
number of parallel walls. These controls support the proofs and do not
enumerate all ideals or establish priority.
Separate exact cost gaps away from the parallel walls verify that
forcing the compulsory core does not preserve the entire positive fan.
The finite rational example with planar length eight has spatial
length 27, residual rank nine and ten parallel walls.
