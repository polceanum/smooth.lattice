# Conormal trace bounds for square-zero overlap

The [quadratic-coordinate reduction](quadratic_intersection_fans.md) leaves
the actual overlap ideal
\(W=K[y,z]1\cap K[y,z]x\), with \(h=\dim_K W\), as a source of
uncontrolled coupling. For flat square-zero extensions of a planar
monomial quotient, this note identifies a uniform monomial envelope for
that ideal. A sharp infinite family also shows why the envelope cannot
be substituted for the image of one extension map.

These are supporting structural results. The trace identity is a
classical determinantal-deformation corollary, with the canonical-trace
formula supplied by published work. Neither result settles unrestricted \(G_3\),
produces a polynomial lower separation, or completes the research goal.

## The actual overlap is the image of one map

Let \(B=K[y,z]\), let \(M\subset B\) be a proper finite-colength
monomial ideal, set \(C=B/M\), and write \(m=\dim_K C\).
For \(\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_B(M,C)\), the
[square-zero construction](square_zero_extension_fans.md) is
\[
 A_\phi=B[x]/(x^2,\ f-x\widetilde\phi(f):f\in M),
 \qquad \dim_K A_\phi=2m.                              \tag{CT1}
\]
Here \(\widetilde\phi(f)\) is any polynomial representative modulo
\(M\); the displayed ideal already contains \(xM\), so the choice
does not matter. As vector spaces \(A_\phi=C\oplus xC\), using the
standard monomial representatives of \(C\). Therefore
\[
 K[y,z]1\cap K[y,z]x=x\operatorname{im}\phi,
 \qquad h=\dim_K\operatorname{im}\phi.                 \tag{CT2}
\]
Indeed a plain polynomial has zero lower component precisely when it
belongs to \(M\), and its upper component is then \(x\phi(f)\).
In particular \(\dim B1=m+h\), whereas \(\dim Bx=m\).

Every map \(M\to C\) kills \(M^2\). The common envelope of all
these images is consequently the conormal trace
\[
 T=\sum_\phi\operatorname{im}\phi
   =\operatorname{tr}_C(M/M^2).                         \tag{CT3}
\]
The trace of a module is an established construction; computing it
from the kernel of the transpose of a presentation matrix is recalled
in [Lindo--Pande, Remark 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06491v2).
The sum in (CT3) need not be the image of any single map.

## Uniform computation from the staircase

Order the minimal generators as
\[
 g_i=y^{a_i}z^{b_i},\quad 0\le i\le r,\qquad
 a_0>\cdots>a_r=0,\quad 0=b_0<\cdots<b_r.
\]
Put \(\alpha_i=a_i-a_{i+1}\), \(\beta_i=b_{i+1}-b_i\).
The Hilbert--Burch matrix \(X\) has \(r+1\) rows and \(r\)
columns, with column \(i\) equal to
\(z^{\beta_i}e_i-y^{\alpha_i}e_{i+1}\). Its adjacent
syzygies generate all syzygies of \(M\).

**Conormal trace identity, over every field.** With the convention
\(I_0(X)=C\),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}_C(M/M^2)=I_{r-1}(X)C
                         =\operatorname{tr}_C(\omega_C).\tag{CT4}
\]
The last equality is
[Ficarra, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07517v1).
His Theorem 3.2 computes coordinates of \(\ker X\); the conormal
trace instead uses \(\ker X^{\mathsf T}\). Thus that theorem alone
does not state the first equality in (CT4).

The first equality nevertheless follows from the classical
Hilbert--Burch deformation theorem: first-order embedded deformations
are induced by perturbing the presentation matrix. See
[Hartshorne's author-hosted notes, Theorem 8.5(3)](https://math.berkeley.edu/~robin/math274root.pdf)
for Schaps' theorem, and
[Oszer, Theorem 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07993v4)
for an explicit all-field matrix parametrization at every planar
monomial ideal. Differentiating the signed maximal minors shows that
the images of all maps \(M\to C\) are generated by the
submaximal cofactors. Every submaximal minor occurs as such a
derivative. Hence their coordinate ideal is exactly the trace.
This identifies the first equality as a classical corollary too;
it is not a new structural theorem. The elementary proof below
avoids relying on a deformation theorem for the all-field assertion.

The nonzero submaximal minors, up to sign, are
\[
 \gamma_{p,k,q}
   =y^{a_p-a_k+a_q}z^{b_p+b_q-b_{k+1}},
 \qquad 0\le p\le k<q\le r.                            \tag{CT5}
\]
To see this, omit column \(k\). Its path graph splits into the row
intervals \([0,k]\) and \([k+1,r]\). A nonzero square minor must
omit one row \(p\) from the first and one row \(q\) from the
second. The remaining path matching is unique. Its products of
\(y^{\alpha_i}\) and \(z^{\beta_i}\) telescope to (CT5).

For the inclusion \(I_{r-1}(X)C\subseteq T\), select \(r-1\)
rows and \(r\) columns of \(X^{\mathsf T}\). Their alternating
cofactor vector, extended by zero on the remaining column, is killed
by these selected rows. Its product with the remaining row is a
maximal minor of \(X^{\mathsf T}\), zero modulo \(M\).
It is therefore a vector in \(\ker X^{\mathsf T}\), defining a
map \(M\to C\). Every submaximal minor occurs as a coordinate of
such a vector. This is the usual cofactor identity, including \(r=1\).

For the reverse inclusion use the fine \(\mathbb Z^2\) grading.
Every map is a sum of homogeneous maps. For degree \((s,t)\), its
nonzero generator images are scalar multiples of
\(y^{a_i+s}z^{b_i+t}\) in the standard diagram \(D\).
If \(s,t\ge0\), every image is zero. Suppose first \(t<0\),
and let \(\ell\) be the first nonzero image. Then \(\ell\ge1\).
The left adjacent syzygy forces
\[
               y^{a_{\ell-1}+s}z^{b_\ell+t}\in M.
\]
Some generator \(g_j\), with \(j\le\ell-1\), divides this
monomial, since \(b_\ell+t<b_\ell\). If \(s<0\) its
\(y\) exponent would be smaller than \(a_{\ell-1}\), a
contradiction. Thus \(s\ge0\), and for every nonzero image
at \(i\ge\ell\), the monomial \(\gamma_{j,\ell-1,i}\)
divides \(y^{a_i+s}z^{b_i+t}\): the required inequalities are
\(a_j\le a_{\ell-1}+s\) and
\(b_j\le b_\ell+t\).

The remaining case is \(s<0,t\ge0\). Let \(\ell\) be the
last nonzero image, so \(\ell<r\). Its right syzygy forces
\(y^{a_\ell+s}z^{b_{\ell+1}+t}\in M\), divisible by
some \(g_j\) with \(j>\ell\). For every nonzero image at
\(i\le\ell\), the monomial \(\gamma_{i,\ell,j}\) divides
its tail. This proves the reverse inclusion and (CT4).
No field-size or characteristic hypothesis was used.

The formula gives an explicit upper bound for the overlap in (CT2).
Inserting it into the previously proved
[slice ceiling](profile_section_budget.md) gives
\[
 g(I_\phi)=O\!\left(\min\left\{
 n\log(n+1)(1+\dim T)^{10/3},\,
 (1+\dim T)(1+n^{4/3})\right\}\right),\quad n=2m.       \tag{CT6}
\]
This is a substitution into existing estimates, not a new unrestricted
bound. For example, if \(M=(y,z)^d\), then
\(T=(y,z)^{d-1}/(y,z)^d\) has dimension \(d\).
Every nonzero tail has degree \(d-1\), so (CT1) is homogeneous
in ordinary total degree. The existing
[positive-grading theorem](nonnegative_graded_fans.md) already gives
a linear fan bound for this entire first-order family. The trace
calculation does not reopen that closed route.

## A capacity bound, and a sharp failure of trace attainment

For any ideal \(\mathfrak q\subset C\), an image
\(J=\phi(M)\subseteq T\) is generated by at most \(r+1\)
elements. Its image in \(T/\mathfrak qT\) is therefore a quotient
of \((C/\mathfrak q)^{r+1}\). The natural surjection
\(T/J\to T/(J+\mathfrak qT)\) proves
\[
 \dim_K J\le \dim_K T-
 \max\{0,\dim_K(T/\mathfrak qT)
                 -(r+1)\dim_K(C/\mathfrak q)\}.        \tag{CT7}
\]
This elementary generator-capacity estimate can be stronger than trace
length alone. It also explains a uniform counterexample to replacing
one image by the whole trace.

Take
\[
 M_0=(y^9,y^7z^3,y^4z^5,z^9),\qquad \dim_K C_0=57.
\]
Formula (CT5) gives
\[
 T_0=(y^5,y^4z^2,y^3z^3,y^2z^4,z^5)C_0,
 \qquad \dim_K T_0=38.                                 \tag{CT8}
\]
All five displayed generators remain minimal modulo \(M_0\).
Since one image has at most four generators, (CT7) with
\(\mathfrak q=(y,z)C_0\) gives \(\dim J\le37\).
Equality is attained over every field by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \phi_0(y^9)&=y^5+y^2z^4,\\
 \phi_0(y^7z^3)&=y^3z^3+y^7+z^7,\\
 \phi_0(y^4z^5)&=z^5+y^4z^2+y^6,\\
 \phi_0(z^9)&=z^6+y^2z^4.                              \tag{CT9}
\end{aligned}
\]
The adjacent syzygies hold modulo \(M_0\), so this is an actual
extension map. The accompanying report saves two explicit \(37\)
by \(37\) minors of its integer image matrix, with determinants
\(-3\) and \(4\). Their sum is \(1\); at least one is nonzero
in every characteristic. This is an exact all-field rank certificate,
not an inference from sampling several primes.

For any integer \(s\ge1\), substitute \(Y=y^s,Z=z^s\) and set
\[
 M_s=(y^{9s},y^{7s}z^{3s},y^{4s}z^{5s},z^{9s}),
 \quad C_s=B/M_s,\quad \dim C_s=57s^2.
\]
The ring \(B\) is free of rank \(s^2\) over \(K[Y,Z]\),
with basis \(y^iz^j\), \(0\le i,j<s\). Formula (CT5) gives
\(T_s=T_0(Y,Z)C_s\), of dimension \(38s^2\).
With \(\mathfrak q=(Y,Z)C_s\), the module
\(T_s/\mathfrak qT_s\) is free of rank five over
\(K[y,z]/(y^s,z^s)\). Thus its dimension is \(5s^2\),
whereas \(\dim(C_s/\mathfrak q)=s^2\).
Formula (CT7) bounds every image by \(37s^2\). Faithfully flat
base change of (CT9) attains that value. Consequently
\[
 \max_{\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_B(M_s,C_s)}
   \dim\operatorname{im}\phi=37s^2<38s^2=\dim T_s       \tag{CT10}
\]
over every field. This is a linear-size unattainability gap for actual
algebras of length \(114s^2\).

This family has a bounded rectangle-to-length ratio:
\((9s)^2/(57s^2)=81/57\). The previously established
[nilpotent-lift box gate](square_zero_extension_fans.md) already gives
\(g=O(n^2)\) for every deformation of these fibers. Thus (CT10)
does not provide a lower separation for the extremal goal. Its role is
to prevent an invalid trace-attainment shortcut in the growing-overlap
problem.

Large trace, attainable overlap, and a large rectangle ratio can also
occur together without a large fan. For
\(M=(y^a,yz,z^b)\), \(a,b\ge2\), one has
\(m=a+b-1\), \(T=(y,z)C\), and the compatible map
\[
             \phi(y^a)=y,\quad\phi(yz)=0,\quad\phi(z^b)=z
\]
attains \(h=m-1\). Taking \(a=b\to\infty\) gives both
\(h\asymp n\) and \(ab/m\to\infty\). Every deformation of
these fibers nevertheless contains \(x^2,y^2z^2\), since
\(M^2\) annihilates (CT1). This is a fixed one-dimensional
monomial carrier, so the existing
[carrier theorem](univariate_cross_fans.md) gives \(g=O(n^2)\).
More generally, if \(y^uz^v\in M\) for fixed positive \(u,v\),
every flat extension contains \(x^2,y^{2u}z^{2v}\) and has the
same quadratic bound with constants depending on \(u,v\).
Thus trace length and the box ratio do not jointly give a sufficient
large-fan test. This is a direct application of the earlier carrier
theorem, not a new counting theorem.

## Evidence, prior-art boundary, and the next obligation

The reference implementation is
[run_conormal_overlap_checks.py](../scripts/run_conormal_overlap_checks.py);
its exact witnesses and source hashes are in
[conormal_overlap_checks.json](../results/research/conormal_overlap_checks.json).
Run from the repository root:

```bash
python3 -B scripts/run_conormal_overlap_checks.py
./build.sh
python3 tests/test_smoke.py
```

The checker compares an independent fine-degree adjacency construction
against the dense all-pair syzygy nullspace for every planar monomial
diagram of lengths 1 through 12, in characteristics 2, 3 and 101.
It also checks that the \(C\)-span of differentiated maximal minors
is the entire Hom space in every one of these cases.
It computes submaximal minors directly and compares them with (CT5),
checks actual commuting extension matrices, and verifies the two
all-field determinants using two exact elimination algorithms.
Scaled witnesses are checked at \(s=1,2,3\). These finite checks
support the implementation; the uniform statements rely on the proofs
above.

The inspected primary boundary is precise but incomplete:

* [Lindo--Pande, Definition 2.1 and Remark 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.06491v2)
  give the trace definition and transpose-kernel computation.
* [Ficarra, Theorems 3.1--3.2 and Lemma 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07517v1)
  give the canonical trace formula, the untransposed kernel calculation,
  and the bidiagonal presentation. The journal version is
  [Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 153 (2025), 3275--3289](https://doi.org/10.1090/PROC/17250);
  its complete text has not been compared with the inspected preprint.
* [Gasanova--Herzog--Hibi--Moradi, introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.04237v2)
  explicitly distinguish the sum of all canonical-module images from
  one image and define the Teter number to measure this distinction.
  Their domain module is \(\omega_C\); our extension domain is
  \(M/M^2\). A failure of trace attainment is therefore an established
  kind of phenomenon, and equality of the two traces does not identify
  their sets of individual image ideals.
* [Hartshorne, Theorem 8.5(3) and its reference to Schaps](https://math.berkeley.edu/~robin/math274root.pdf),
  and [Oszer, Theorem 5.4 with its proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07993v4),
  supply the determinantal deformation input described above. The
  inspected Oszer preprint works over an arbitrary field. The original
  1977 Schaps article was not separately inspected.

The trace identity is classified as a classical corollary. The exact
priority of the capacity application and (CT10) remains unassessed.
No unsuccessful search is treated as novelty evidence, and no separate
paper is justified here.

The next count still concerns the positively exposed minors of one
compatible map, collectively across the planar strips. A trace ideal
only constrains the possible output space. It does not control the
minor cancellations, two-exchange slice edges, or active threshold
contacts. The original unrestricted extremal and complexity objectives
remain unchanged and unresolved.
