# A canonical monomial deflation from one reduced Gröbner basis

Let \(I\subset R=K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]\) have positive finite colength
\(n\), over any field. Suppose \(I\) is not monomial. Take a reduced
Gröbner basis \(G\) in any global order and set
\[
 f=x^v=\gcd\{x^\alpha:\ x^\alpha\text{ occurs in a nonmonomial }g\in G\}.
                                                               \tag{D1}
\]
The gcd uses **all terms**, after cancellation in \(K\), not just heads.
Then \(f\) is independent of the chosen global order.
The actual colon quotient
\[
                 J=I:f,\qquad m=\dim_KR/J                \tag{D2}
\]
has the identical full positive initial fan. Its standard profiles
differ by one fixed translation. In particular it has the same
primitive wall directions, edge multiples and total primitive wall cost.
This gives a canonical, idempotent removal of common monomial padding.
It does not remove every kind of univariate padding.

The result supplies an actual smaller algebra when \(v\ne0\).
The algebra is \(R/(I:f)\), not the quotient of \(R/I\) by the
vector span of the removed monomials.
When \(v=0\), no reduction is claimed. The monomial case already
has one initial ideal and is handled separately.

## Why the gcd is intrinsic

Let \(M(I)\) be the ideal generated by all monomials that belong
to \(I\). It need not have finite colength. Define the essential
relation space
\[
 \mathcal R(I)=\{h\in I:\operatorname{supp}h
                         \cap M(I)=\varnothing\}.        \tag{D3}
\]
This is a vector space, and is nonzero precisely when \(I\) is
not monomial.

Every nonmonomial element of a reduced Gröbner basis belongs to
\(\mathcal R(I)\). Its tails are standard monomials and therefore
are not individually in \(I\). Its head cannot be individually
in \(I\) either: subtracting that monomial would give a nonzero
relation among its standard tails.

Let \(u\) be the gcd in (D1) from this one basis, and let \(A\)
be the monomial ideal generated by its monomial elements. Then
\[
                   I=A+x^uK_0                           \tag{D4}
\]
for the ideal \(K_0\) generated by the divided nonmonomial elements.
In any element of \(I\), a term not divisible by \(x^u\) must
come from \(A\), and hence belongs individually to \(M(I)\).
Thus every essential relation is divisible by \(x^u\).
Conversely the nonmonomial basis elements are essential relations.
Consequently
\[
        x^u=\gcd\{\text{all terms occurring in }\mathcal R(I)\},
                                                               \tag{D5}
\]
which proves independence from \(G\) and the order.
In particular \(f\notin I\): otherwise every term of the nonmonomial
basis elements would individually be in \(I\).

## The fixed halo and exact initial identities

Since \(J=I:f\), (D4) implies
\[
             I=A+fJ,\qquad A:f\subseteq J.               \tag{D6}
\]
Indeed \(K_0\subseteq J\), and multiplication of \(A:f\) by \(f\)
lands in \(A\). Let
\[
 H=\{x^\alpha:x^\alpha\notin A+(f)\}
   =\{x^\alpha:x^\alpha\notin M(I),\ f\nmid x^\alpha\}.
                                                               \tag{D7}
\]
It is a finite downset: \(R/(A+(f))=R/(I+(f))\) is a quotient
of the finite algebra. The equality of the two descriptions follows
because a monomial outside the \(f\)-cone can belong to \(I\) only
through \(A\).

Every relation modulo \(A\) is supported inside the \(f\)-cone.
The monomial columns labelled by \(H\) are coloops, and all remaining
nonzero columns are \(f\) times the columns of \(R/J\).
Thus for every global order the initial standard sets correspond by
\[
 B_I=H\ \sqcup\ (v+B_J),\qquad
          n=|H|+m,\qquad
 \mu(B_I)=\mu(H)+mv+\mu(B_J).                            \tag{D8}
\]
Translation preserves every monomial comparison inside the second
block. The same conclusion follows from leading-ideal inclusion and
the displayed colength:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{in}I&=A+f\operatorname{in}J
                   =M(I)+f\operatorname{in}J,\\
 \operatorname{in}(I:f)&=(\operatorname{in}I):f .
 \end{split}                                                \tag{D9}
\]
These identities hold simultaneously for all global orders.
The full positive cones and state polyhedra therefore agree up
to the profile translation in (D8).

If \(C\) is the common intersection of all initial standard sets,
then \(C_I=H\sqcup(v+C_J)\). The same statement holds for all
bases incident to any one exposed face.
The deficit \(n-|C_I|=m-|C_J|\) is unchanged.

Every proper divisor of \(f\) is nonzero and belongs to \(H\).
The first assertion follows from \(f\notin I\); the second from
its failure to be divisible by \(f\). Hence
\[
 |H|\ge\prod_i(v_i+1)-1\ge\sum_i v_i,\qquad
                  1\le m\le n-\prod_i(v_i+1)+1.         \tag{D10}
\]

## Idempotence and explicit extraction

Essential relations satisfy the exact identity
\[
                         \mathcal R(J)=f^{-1}\mathcal R(I).
                                                               \tag{D11}
\]
For a monomial \(x^\alpha\), membership in \(J\) is equivalent to
membership of \(fx^\alpha\) in \(I\). This proves that essential
supports correspond in both directions. Their gcd after division
is one. Thus the same operation applied to \(J\) has \(v=0\).
This is a canonical monomial core of the given coordinate
presentation, without an arbitrary choice of a compulsory subset.

It can be obtained from the one marked basis \(G\) using only
exponent operations. For a monomial \(x^a\in G\), replace it by
\(x^{a-\min(a,v)}\); for a nonmonomial \(g\in G\), replace it by
\(g/f\). The resulting list is a Gröbner basis of \(J\) for
the marked order and has at most \(|G|\) polynomials.
It may require interreduction to become reduced.

To check this directly, each divided polynomial is in \(I:f\),
and its head is the corresponding generator of
\((\operatorname{in}I):f\). The general inclusion
\(\operatorname{in}(I:f)\subseteq(\operatorname{in}I):f\)
and the reverse inclusion supplied by this list prove equality.
The leading-ideal criterion proves the claimed Gröbner basis.

If \(B\) is the marked standard set, no monomial-part computation
is needed:
\[
 H=\{b\in B:f\nmid x^b\},\qquad
                  B_J=\{b-v:b\in B,\ b\ge v\}.          \tag{D12}
\]

There is also a matrix-only extraction. Put
\[
 E=\{\alpha\ge0:\prod_i(\alpha_i+1)\le n+1,\ x^\alpha\bmod I\ne0\}.
 \]
Compute the kernel of its residue-column matrix. The gcd of the
monomials occurring in any basis of this kernel is exactly \(f\).
If the kernel is zero, \(I\) is monomial.
To prove completeness, every reduced-basis head \(a\) has all its
proper divisors in its standard set, so
\(\prod_i(a_i+1)\le n+1\); every tail lies in the ordinary
length-\(n\) universal ground. All nonmonomial reduced-basis
relations therefore occur in this finite kernel. Conversely all its
relations are essential because zero columns were removed.

For fixed \(d\), dense column preparation and elimination have
\(O_d(n^3(1+\log(n+1))^{d-1})\) field-operation envelope from
commuting multiplication matrices and a cyclic vector.
A marked standard set can be obtained by one positive greedy scan
of the same prepared ground. This is an elementary dense bound,
excluding coefficient bits and any first-basis computation from
arbitrary generators.

## The smaller algebra is certified by multiplication

Multiplication by \(f\) identifies \(R/J\), as a polynomial module,
with the invariant ideal \(f(R/I)\). Its dimension is
\[
                              m=\operatorname{rank}M_f.
                                                               \tag{D13}
\]
In a marked monomial basis \(B\), that ideal is spanned exactly
by the labels divisible by \(f\). Each coordinate multiplication
matrix therefore has zero entries outside this block in those
columns. Restricting to the block gives the coordinate matrices
of \(R/J\), with cyclic vector \(f\), corresponding to its unit.
This is a genuine quotient \( (R/I)/\operatorname{ann}(f)\).
It does not identify \(\operatorname{span}H\) with that annihilator.

For instance
\[
 I=(x^2,y^2-xy,z),\quad f=y,\quad
 J=(x-y,x^2,z)
 \]
has lengths \(4,2\), halo \(\{1,x\}\), and two identical cones.
The halo span contains \(1\) and is proper, so it is not an ideal.
The valid replacement uses the invariant image of multiplication by
\(y\), or equivalently the quotient by its annihilator.

## Padding-invariant quantitative bounds

In three variables, the [existing unrestricted theorem](rational_shadow_bound.md) applied to
the actual length-\(m\) quotient gives
\[
 g(I)=g(J)=O(m^{23/8}(1+\log(m+1))^{3/8}),\quad
                         V(I)\le3m\,g(I).               \tag{D14}
\]
Every actual wall has \(\nu(q)\le m\). Its parallel multiplicity
obeys the [existing coexistence bound](wall_coexistence.md) with residual
\[
 r=m-\nu(q)+1,\qquad
 p_I(q)=O\!\left(\min\{r^{4/3}(1+\log(r+1))^2,\
                r(1+\|q\|_1^{1/3})(1+\log(r+1))\}\right).
                                                               \tag{D15}
\]
This replaces padded length by the true quotient length in all these
estimates. It is an application of the earlier theorems, not a new
unrestricted exponent. The earlier simultaneous-core and middle-cost
requirements remain.

Coordinate translations preserve global initial ideals: they change
each monomial only by proper-divisor terms. One may first translate,
interreduce, and then perform the extraction. Let \(\kappa(I)\) be
the smallest resulting core length over centers in \(K^d\), assigning
length one to a translated presentation that is monomial.
The minimum exists because it is
a nonempty set of positive integers. No efficient global search for
the best center over an arbitrary field is claimed; the finite candidate
set is described below. Bounds (D14)--(D15) hold with \(m=\kappa\).
Thus \(g(I_n)\ge c n^{2+\epsilon}\) requires
\[
 \kappa(I_n)=\Omega_{c,\epsilon}
 \left(n^{(16+8\epsilon)/23}/\log^{3/23}(n+1)\right).
                                                               \tag{D16}
\]

## Unit coordinates and the possible centers

If any coordinate is a unit in \(R/I\), the intrinsic factor is one.
Indeed its minimal polynomial has nonzero constant coefficient:
otherwise cancel that unit to obtain an annihilator of smaller degree.
Every power of the coordinate is nonzero, so the entire minimal
polynomial is an essential relation. Its constant term forces gcd one
in (D5).
A proper finite monomial quotient has nilpotent coordinates, so a
presentation with a unit coordinate cannot enter the monomial exception.

Let \(m_i(T)\) be the minimal polynomial of coordinate multiplication,
and let \(\Lambda_i\subset K\) be its roots in \(K\). In a presentation
centered at \(c\), a coordinate is a unit whenever
\(m_i(c_i)\ne0\). Thus a center giving effective length below \(n\)
must lie in
\[
                         \Lambda_1\times\cdots\times\Lambda_d.
                                                               \tag{D17}
\]
There are at most \(\prod_i\deg m_i\le n^d\) candidates. Given these
roots, the marked-basis extraction at the candidates computes
\(\kappa\); finding roots over an arbitrary field is an additional
computational problem, not included in the field-operation envelope.
Translations preserve each leading monomial, so one can translate and
interreduce the same marked basis at each center.

In particular, for a quotient supported at one \(K\)-rational point,
every other center has a unit coordinate. Its best core is therefore
obtained at the support point. If the factor there is one, then
\(\kappa(I)=n\). Full core length consequently does not imply a large
fan, and need not even remove padding from an interacting part of a
larger relation system.

## A full-length core does not repair the reuse charge

Here is an all-field infinite guard against normalizing the failed
absolute-divisor-volume charge only by the global canonical core.
It retains every old recrossing while increasing length by just one.

Take the [parity-circuit construction](disjoint_socle_circuits.md#one-upper-pair-recrosses-at-a-polynomial-rate)
with parameter \(s\ge1\), \(B=4s^2\), and divisor downset \(D_s\).
Its two relations have disjoint socle supports and its length is
\(n_s=(32s^3+30s^2+10s+12)/3\). Pad by \(v=(0,A,0)\), where
\(A\ge1\). Write \(D_{s,A}=D_s+[0,v]\), and let \(W_{s,A}\) be
the span of the two translated relations. Adjoin the two new maximal
monomials
\[
                  a=x^2,\qquad b=z^{B+2},
\]
and set
\[
 \widehat D=D_{s,A}\cup\{a,b\},\qquad
 \widehat I=J_{\widehat D}+W_{s,A}+(x^2-z^{B+2}).         \tag{D18}
\]
This is a downset: \(D_{s,A}\) already contains every proper divisor
of \(a,b\). Its old maximal supports all have positive \(y\)-exponent,
so neither new tip dominates one. The added relation is a disjoint
socle circuit. Hence
\[
 \widehat n=n_s+(8s^2+4)A+1,\qquad
                 4s\le g(\widehat I)\le8s.              \tag{D19}
\]
The new fan is the old fan refined by the one line
\(t=2s^2+1\), with \(w=(t,u,1)\). The upper bound in (D19)
just splits each convex old cone into at most two pieces.

The old persistent junctions are
\[
                 (t_i,u_i)=(i(i+1),i+\tfrac12),
                        \qquad1\le i<2s.                \tag{D20}
\]
Their \(t_i\)'s are even and the new line has odd \(t\), so none is
lost or becomes a multiple crossing. Every junction still has exactly
four incident bases, actual common core of size \(\widehat n-2\),
and primitive wall directions
\((1,-2i,i^2)\), \((1,-2(i+1),(i+1)^2)\). The same upper pair is
\[
             e=(1,A+1,B),\qquad f=(1,A,B+1),
\]
with divisor volumes \(2(A+2)(B+1)\), \(2(A+1)(B+2)\).

Both new tips are individually nonzero, so \(x^2-z^{B+2}\) is an
essential relation with gcd one. The quotient is local at the origin.
The preceding unit argument therefore proves
\[
                         \kappa(\widehat I)=\widehat n
                                                               \tag{D21}
\]
over every field, even after allowing every translation.

For \(A=s\), the two divisor-volume ratios tend to \(3/7\), while
the same pair has \(2s-1=\Theta(\widehat n^{1/3})\) recrossings.
For \(s=k^4,A=k^5\), both ratios tend to one and recrossing is
\(\Theta(\widehat n^{4/13})\). Bulk costs
\(2i^2+2i+1\), with \(s\le i<2s\), remain strictly between
\(\widehat n^{3/5}\) and \(\widehat n-\widehat n^{3/7}\) for all
sufficiently large parameters in both families. These assertions
follow directly from (D19): respectively
\(\widehat n=\Theta(s^3)\) and \(\Theta(k^{13})\), whereas the
costs are \(\Theta(s^2)\) and \(\Theta(k^8)\).

Thus the proposed length/divisor-volume reuse bound, with any fixed
polylogarithmic allowance, remains false among ideals already having
full canonical core at every center. A disjoint relation can hide the
common factor of the interacting part. This does not refute the
collective wall budget: these examples still have only \(\Theta(s)\)
initial ideals and lie in the already bounded socle class. A repair
must retain the interacting relation data, not just the global gcd or
the minimized length \(\kappa\).

## Limits and verification

Reduction is essential in (D1). Adding a redundant polynomial whose
terms individually lie in \(I\) can lower the apparent gcd. Arbitrary
colon operations do not preserve fans: for
\((x-y^2,y^3,z)\), colon by \(x\) reduces two cones to one.
The canonical gcd in that example is one.

The operation removes all common monomial-factor halos, including the
monomial recrossing padding from the preceding note. It does not
remove every unitriangular or univariate padding. For example
\(((x-1)^3,y-2,z-3)\) has gcd one in its reduced basis, although it
is a univariate padding of a length-one quotient. Coordinate choice
therefore matters; \(\kappa\) allows translations but is not asserted
to classify every fan-preserving transformation.
