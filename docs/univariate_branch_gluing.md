# Univariate gluing for arbitrary finite quotients with yz = 0

Let \(A=K[x,y,z]/I\) have finite positive dimension \(n\), over any
field, and assume only \(yz\in I\). There is no degree bound on an
annihilating polynomial for \(x\). Put \(B=K[x]\),
\[
Y=yA,\quad Z=zA,\quad W=Y\cap Z,\quad
m_Y=\dim_KY,\quad m_Z=\dim_KZ,\quad h=\dim_KW,
\]
and write \(d=\dim_K A/(Y+Z)\). Let \(a=\dim_K B1\), and let
\(r=\mu_B(W)\) be the minimal number of \(B\)-module generators,
with \(\mu_B(0)=0\).

The branch intersection and the complete coupling satisfy
\[
 h\le d,\qquad m_Y+m_Z=n+h-d\le n,\qquad r(r+1)\le n.       \tag{G1}
\]
Every such coordinate quotient is a quotient of an explicit envelope
of dimension at most \(2n\). The envelope is a direct sum, as a vector
space, of one univariate core and two actual planar branch modules.
Its coupling kernel \(T\) is killed by \(y,z\), and
\[
 \dim_KT=a+h-d\le a,\qquad \mu_B(T)\le r+1.                \tag{G2}
\]
If \(A\) is Gorenstein at every local factor, then \(r\le1\), so
two \(B\)-module generators suffice for \(T\). If \(A\) is reduced,
then \(W=0\) and one suffices. The bounds on \(r\), the envelope
factor two, and the Gorenstein kernel bound two are sharp over every
field.

Equations (G1)--(G11) are structural reductions toward the counting
problem. The one-ray specialization below gives a counting theorem
under an additional quadratic relation. Fiber products, module quotients,
the invariant-factor theorem and the planar socle estimate used below
are classical. Exact priority of the assembled resource statements is
unassessed. No unrestricted improvement, supergeneric lower family,
new manuscript or completed research goal follows.

In the original smooth-number problem, positive prime-log weights
order independent exponent labels. Here the weights still order those
labels, but algebraic relations can identify their residue vectors.
The equation \(yz=0\) removes mixed branch labels, and \(W,T\) record
the remaining dependencies between the two exponent planes and their
shared axis. The reduction constrains those dependencies by multiplication;
it gives no prime-distribution consequence.

## Length bookkeeping from the actual multiplication

Since \(yZ=zY=0\), both \(y\) and \(z\) kill \(W\). Multiplication by
\(y\) gives a surjection
\[
 A/(Y+Z)\longrightarrow Y/yY,\qquad [u]\longmapsto yu\bmod yY.
\]
It is well defined because \(yZ=0\). Rank-nullity on \(y:Y\to Y\)
and \(W\subseteq\ker(y|Y)\) imply
\[
 h\le\dim_K\ker(y|Y)=\dim_K(Y/yY)\le d.
\]
Also \(\dim_K(Y+Z)=m_Y+m_Z-h=n-d\), proving the first two
statements in (G1). This uses ideals in one algebra, not two unrelated
planar rank tables.

## The sharp generator estimate

We first recall an elementary planar fact. If a finite quotient
\(C=L[u,v]/J\) has a \(t\)-dimensional subspace killed by both \(u,v\),
then
\[
                         \dim_L C\ge t(t+1)/2.              \tag{G3}
\]
Choose a monomial standard basis, and echelonize representatives of
the subspace by their leading monomials. Their \(t\) distinct leading
monomials are maximal elements of its downset: multiplication by
either variable belongs to \(J\). If \(t\) incomparable planar
monomials are sorted by increasing first coordinate, their first
coordinates are at least \(0,1,\ldots,t-1\) and their second
coordinates decrease by at least one each time. Their divisor closure
contains at least \(1+2+\cdots+t\) monomials. This proves (G3),
without a homogeneity or locality assumption. The bound is the
classical planar staircase estimate.

For \(W\ne0\), let its invariant factors over \(B\) be
\(f_1\mid\cdots\mid f_r\), all nonconstant. Extend scalars to an
algebraic closure and choose a root \(\lambda\) of \(f_1\). Then
\[
               \dim\ker(x-\lambda|W_{\overline K})=r.
\]
Repeated or inseparable roots do not change this assertion: each
cyclic summand contributes exactly one eigenvector at that root.
This subspace is killed by \(y,z\). The cyclic planar module
\[
 K[x,y]/((I:y)\cap K[x,y])\simeq Y,
 \qquad g\longmapsto yg
\]
therefore has at least \(r\) socle vectors at \((\lambda,0)\).
Apply (G3) after translating \(x\) by \(\lambda\), and do the same
for \(Z\). Scalar extension preserves all the dimensions in question.
Thus
\[
 m_Y\ge r(r+1)/2,\qquad m_Z\ge r(r+1)/2.
\]
Together with \(m_Y+m_Z\le n\), this proves \(r(r+1)\le n\).
Testing only eigenvalues belonging to the original field would miss
irreducible invariant factors and is not sufficient.

## The complete envelope, in the original coordinates

Let \(f\in B\) be the actual monic minimal annihilator of \(1\in A\)
under \(x\); \(\deg f=a\). Set
\[
 J_Y=(I:y)\cap K[x,y],\qquad J_Z=(I:z)\cap K[x,z].
\]
The polynomial \(f\) belongs to both ideals. Define
\[
 H=K[x,y,z]/(f,\ yz,\ yJ_Y,\ zJ_Z).                        \tag{G4}
\]
Here \(yJ_Y\) means the ideal generated by all \(yg\), \(g\in J_Y\),
and likewise for \(zJ_Z\). There is an exact vector-space decomposition
\[
 H=B/(f)\ \oplus\ y(K[x,y]/J_Y)\ \oplus\ z(K[x,z]/J_Z).    \tag{G5}
\]
For a direct proof, put the natural \(x\)-action on all three summands.
On the core, \(y\) sends \(p(x)\) to \(y[p(x)]\); on the second
summand it acts by multiplication by \(y\); on the third it is zero.
Define \(z\) symmetrically. These commuting actions satisfy (G4),
are generated by the core vector \(1\), and give the decomposition.
This also verifies that (G4) imposes no hidden branch relation.

The natural surjection \(\pi:H\to A\) is injective on each of the
three summands separately, by their definitions. Its kernel \(T\)
therefore has
\[
 yT\subseteq T\cap y(K[x,y]/J_Y)=0,\qquad zT=0.
\]
In particular \(T\subseteq\operatorname{ann}_H(y,z)\) is a
\(B\)-submodule. Equations (G1) and (G5) give
\[
 \dim_KH=a+m_Y+m_Z=a+n+h-d\le a+n\le2n,\qquad
 \dim_KT=a+h-d\le a.                                      \tag{G6}
\]

Conversely, start with \(f,J_Y,J_Z\) containing \(f\), form (G4),
and choose any \(B\)-submodule \(T\subseteq\operatorname{ann}_H(y,z)\)
that intersects each summand of (G5) trivially. It is an ideal: the
remaining two coordinate actions kill it. The quotient \(H/T\)
has precisely those actual core and branch modules, with the original
coordinate actions. Thus this construction parametrizes all compatible
couplings for those endpoints; it does not discard their extension data.

Projection of \(T\) to the core has kernel naturally isomorphic to
\(W\). Its image is
\(S=B1\cap(Y+Z)\), a \(B\)-submodule of the cyclic module \(B/(f)\).
Consequently
\[
              0\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow T
                 \longrightarrow S\longrightarrow0,
       \qquad \dim_KS=a-d.                                 \tag{G7}
\]
Every submodule of \(B/(f)\) is cyclic, since \(B\) is a principal
ideal domain. Lift one generator of \(S\) and add \(r\) generators
of \(W\), proving \(\mu_B(T)\le r+1\). If \(S=0\), the sharper
bound \(\mu_B(T)=r\) holds.

## Reduced and Gorenstein quotients

For reduced \(A\), the equality \(YZ=0\) implies \(W^2=0\), hence
\(W=0\). Equation (G7) makes \(T\) cyclic.

For Gorenstein \(A\), the whole joint annihilator has the stronger
classical duality description
\[
 M:=\operatorname{ann}_A(y,z)
       \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_K(A/(Y+Z),K)
       \simeq B/(g),\qquad \deg g=d.                       \tag{G10}
\]
Here \(A/(Y+Z)=B/(g)\), and all isomorphisms are \(B\)-linear.
Indeed choose a nondegenerate Frobenius pairing
\((u,v)\mapsto\ell(uv)\) on \(A\). The orthogonal complement of
\(Y+Z\) is its annihilator: if \(\ell(uJ)=0\) for an ideal \(J\),
then \(\ell(ujb)=0\) for every \(b\), forcing \(uj=0\).
The pairing identifies this orthogonal complement with the dual
of the quotient. For \(d>0\), the coefficient of \(x^{d-1}\)
modulo the monic \(g\) gives a nondegenerate pairing on \(B/(g)\);
for \(d=0\) this is the zero module.
Thus \(M\) is cyclic and has length \(d\). Its submodule \(W\)
is cyclic too, proving \(r\le1\) over the original field.

There are at most three univariate module generators in the entire
joint annihilator of the envelope:
\[
              \mu_B(\operatorname{ann}_H(y,z))\le3.        \tag{G11}
\]
To see this, the planar quotients \(C_Y=K[x,y]/J_Y\) and
\(C_Z=K[x,z]/J_Z\) are themselves Frobenius when nonzero.
For example the form \(\ell(yuv)\) has radical
\(\operatorname{ann}_A(y)\), and descends nondegenerately to
\(A/\operatorname{ann}_A(y)\simeq C_Y\).
The same duality argument shows that
\(\operatorname{ann}_{C_Y}(y)\) and
\(\operatorname{ann}_{C_Z}(z)\) are cyclic over \(B\).
Projection of \(\operatorname{ann}_H(y,z)\) to the core has
cyclic image; its kernel is the direct sum of these two modules.
Lift their generators and one for the image to prove (G11).
The sharper two-generator assertion for \(T\) still uses (G7).
These facts are applications of Frobenius duality, not new duality
principles or constant-dimensional vector-space reductions.

## Sharp examples and a fan-size guard

For \(r\ge1\), define
\[
 H_r=K[x,y,z]/(x^{r+1},yz,y(x,y)^r,z(x,z)^r).
\]
Its core has length \(r+1\); each branch has length \(r(r+1)/2\),
so \(\dim_KH_r=(r+1)^2\). Quotient by the \(r+1\) socle relations
\[
 x^i(y^{r-i}-z^{r-i})\quad(0\le i<r),\qquad x^r-y^r.       \tag{G8}
\]
They are independent, and their span meets each individual summand
trivially. The resulting \(A_r\) has
\[
 n=r(r+1),\quad a=r+1,\quad d=h=r,\quad
 W\simeq(B/(x))^r,\quad T\simeq(B/(x))^{r+1}.              \tag{G9}
\]
This attains both generator bounds. To realize the maximum
\(r=\lfloor(\sqrt{4n+1}-1)/2\rfloor\) at every length \(n\ge2\),
take \(A_r\) and, if needed, add the disjoint axis factor
\(K[x]/((x-1)^t)\), \(y=z=0\), where \(t=n-r(r+1)\).
The Chinese remainder construction works over every field; the new
factor contributes no \(Y,Z,W\). At \(n=1\) the maximum is zero.

These sharp large-generator examples have small fans. All degree-\(r\)
tips form the parallel class \(\{x^r,y^r,z^r\}\) and the pairs
\(\{x^iy^{r-i},x^iz^{r-i}\}\), \(1\le i<r\). All remaining
standard monomials are coloops. The first class selects the cheapest
coordinate, while every pair compares only \(w_y,w_z\). Thus
\(g(A_1)=3\) and \(g(A_r)=4\) for \(r\ge2\), over every field.
Large \(\mu_B(W)\) alone gives no large-fan construction.

For sharpness of the factor two and the Gorenstein kernel bound, take
\[
 A=K[t]/(t^{2m}),\qquad x=t,\quad y=z=t^m\quad(m\ge1).
\]
Here \(n=a=2m\), \(m_Y=m_Z=h=d=m\), and the envelope is
\[
 H=K[x,y,z]/(x^{2m},yz,x^my,x^mz,y^2,z^2).
\]
It has length \(4m=2n\). Its kernel is generated over \(B\) by
\(y-x^m,z-x^m\), and is isomorphic to \((B/(x^m))^2\).
The quotient is local Gorenstein in every characteristic.

## Prior-art boundary and the unresolved counting step

The envelope is also the fiber product of
\(K[x,y]/(f,yJ_Y)\) and \(K[x,z]/(f,zJ_Z)\) over \(B/(f)\).
Fiber products and diagonal module quotients are established; compare
[Ananthnarayan--Avramov--Moore, Sections 1--2](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~frankmoore/Papers/connected.pdf),
especially (1.0.2), (1.1.3), (2.0.1) and (2.1.3).
Their Theorem 2.8 imposes Gorenstein endpoint and canonical-module
hypotheses; those hypotheses are not imposed on (G4).
Our envelope endpoints need not be Gorenstein, and \(T\) may have a
core component. No new gluing operation or automatic application of
that Gorenstein theorem is asserted. The present inequalities have
self-contained elementary proofs; a failed keyword search would not
establish their novelty.

From commuting multiplication matrices and a cyclic vector, (G4)--(G7)
can be recovered by exact linear algebra: compute the two images,
the \(x\)-Krylov core, their restrictions, and the kernel of the map
from their direct sum. The number of field operations is polynomial
in \(n\). This is a coordinate-preserving reconstruction, not a
Gröbner-fan enumeration algorithm or a new linear-algebra algorithm.

A single \(B\)-module generator can span a space of dimension growing
with \(n\). The \(x\)-action and original monomial labels must remain
in a fan argument. In particular the earlier
[forced socle family](forced_socle_fan_steps.md) has cyclic gluing
and quadratically many cones. Replacing \(T\) by a constant-size
arbitrary vector matroid would be invalid.

The next counting question is the full fan of a compatible univariate
gluing, first for the two-generator Gorenstein case. Closing that class
would require a theorem about its labelled \(x\)-iterates. Equations
(G1)--(G9) do not supply such a theorem or settle unrestricted \(G_3\).

## A full count when the branch outside a plane is one ray

Suppose first that
\[
                            z^2=yz=0\quad\hbox{in }A.      \tag{G12}
\]
Put \(L=K[x,y]1\), \(\ell=\dim_KL\), \(m=\dim_KzA\), and
\(\eta=\dim_K(L\cap zA)=\ell+m-n\). These are different from
the two-branch intersection parameters \(h,r\) above. Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 g(I)&\le
 \sum_{k=n-\ell}^{\min(m,n-1)}
       \left[1+4(n-k)\lceil\log_2(n-k)\rceil\right]\\
     &\le(\eta+1)\left[1+4\ell\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil\right]
       =O(n^2\log(n+1)).                                  \tag{G13}
 \end{split}
\]
The proof works over every field, without locality, Gorenstein,
genericity or a bound on the degree of \(x\). It gives at most that
many candidate standard sets, rather than a count of every possibly
noncoherent order-ideal basis. The usual planar adjacency and primitive
threshold bounds also give \(W(I)=O(n^3\log(n+1))\).

Indeed every standard set has the form
\[
 B=P\sqcup C_k,\qquad
 C_k=\{z,xz,\ldots,x^{k-1}z\},\quad |P|=n-k,\quad
                         P\subset K[x,y].                 \tag{G14}
\]
The set \(P\) is a planar downset, and \(n-\ell\le k\le m\).
The first \(m\) columns of the ray are independent, because \(zA\)
is a cyclic \(K[x]\)-module generated by \(z\). For a fixed \(k\),
contract those actual \(k\) independent columns in the residue matroid.
Use the planar ground
\[
 E_{n-k}=\{(a,b): (a+1)(b+1)\le n-k\}.
\]
If its contracted rank is less than \(n-k\), no standard set with
that value of \(k\) exists. Otherwise it is a rank-\((n-k)\) matroid.
The planar part of any strict full optimum must minimize the planar
cost in this contraction. Apply the
[planar hyperbolic matroid bound](planar_dyadic_bound.md), with
rank and height both \(n-k\), and sum over \(k\). Distinct strict
standard sets with the same \(k\) cannot have the same exposed planar
profile: their fixed ray part would give tied full optimal bases at a
generic element-cost order. This proves (G13).

This is a genuine matroid contraction by a fixed column set.
The quotient vector space need not be an algebra, and its optimizers
need not be downsets. In particular it is not the generally invalid
replacement of a fixed-color-count section by a matroid. Fixing the
count works here because (G14) fixes the columns themselves.

The proof is constructive. Enumerate the positive planar profile
intervals of each contracted matroid; join each resulting basis to
\(C_k\); discard non-downsets; recover its full positive cone from
the original quotient's border relations. Every actual cone appears.
A contraction's interval must be certified by all fundamental-circuit
comparisons, not an algebra border calculation on the contraction.
The count bounds candidates. No bit-complexity or optimal running-time
claim is made.

There is a sharper companion for
\[
                              xz=yz=0.                     \tag{G15}
\]
Now the outside ray is \(z,z^2,\ldots\), so the same contraction
argument applies, and \(\eta\le1\). To verify the latter, assume
\(zA\ne0\). Evaluation at \(x=y=0\) defines an augmentation
\(\epsilon:L\to K\), since any relation in \(L\) multiplied by
\(z\) has constant coefficient zero. For \(w\in L\cap zA\),
\[
                              zw=\epsilon(w)z.
\]
If \(\epsilon\) vanishes on the intersection, it lies in the kernel
of \(z\) on the cyclic \(K[z]\)-module \(zA\), of dimension at
most one. Otherwise choose \(w=za\) with \(\epsilon(w)=1\).
Then \(z^2a=z\), so \(z:zA\to zA\) is surjective and hence
injective. Its restriction sends the intersection into \(Kz\),
again proving \(\eta\le1\). Consequently
\[
                    g(I)\le2+8n\lceil\log_2 n\rceil.        \tag{G16}
\]
The worst order is \(\Theta(n\log(n+1))\), with the lower bound
over \(\mathbb Q\) supplied by arbitrary planar point ideals with
\(z=0\) and the established planar corner-cut lower count.
Thus adding the entire transverse axis, including arbitrary nonreduced
coupling, does not change that worst order.

One consequence of (G13) rules out a natural local family completely.
If \(A=K[x]/(q(x)^s)\) for an irreducible \(q\), and \(yz=0\),
the \(q\)-adic orders of \(y,z\) add to at least \(s\). One is at
least \(s/2\), so either \(y^2=0\) or \(z^2=0\).
After interchanging \(y,z\), (G13) applies. Thus every primary
monogenic quotient with \(x\) as its parameter and \(yz=0\) has
only \(O(n^2\log(n+1))\) initials, over every field, including
inseparable \(q\). The unrestricted two-planar-branch problem is
not reduced to this class.

The contraction argument has a version that only requires the
**initial carrier**, not actual zero products. Suppose the standard
set of an initial ideal lies in a coordinate plane together with
one ray of either form in (G14)--(G15). The first \(k\) ray columns
are fixed. If they are dependent, that \(k\) is impossible.
Contract them and use \(E_{n-k}\) exactly as above. Sum now over
all \(0\le k<n\), without using the actual overlap or zero-product
estimates. There are at most
\[
             T(n)=\sum_{j=1}^n
                       [1+4j\lceil\log_2j\rceil]           \tag{G22}
\]
strict initial bases inside this carrier. The recovered full cones
need not fill the positive weight triangle for an individual carrier.

This yields a uniform theorem in arbitrary affine coordinates.
Let \(l_0,l_1,l_2\) be affine linear polynomials over \(K\),
with \(l_0\) nonconstant and the linear parts of \(l_1,l_2\)
independent, and assume
\[
                         (l_0l_1,l_0l_2)\subseteq I.
\]
Then
\[
                   g(I)\le6T(n)=O(n^2\log(n+1)).           \tag{G23}
\]
Indeed for every global order,
\[
 \operatorname{in}\bigl(l_0(l_1,l_2)\bigr)
              =\operatorname{lt}(l_0)\operatorname{in}(l_1,l_2).
\]
Multiplication by a common polynomial preserves leading products,
and the affine linear ideal has two distinct variable heads. Thus its
initial carrier is a plane plus one parallel or transverse ray.
The heads depend only on the ordering of the three variables;
six variable orderings cover them all. Every \(I\)-standard set
lies in at least one of these carriers. Apply (G22) and take the
union. This proof does not assume that a linear coordinate change
preserves the original fan. It retains the original monomial labels
and handles every carrier separately.

The quadratic family below shows that a uniform exponent below two
is impossible in (G23). Whether its logarithm is necessary is open
here. A polynomially supergeneric example must therefore avoid
every such common-linear-factor quadratic pencil, in any affine
coordinates. This is a restriction on a hypothetical example,
not a construction of one.

Combining (G23) with the earlier
[quadratic-coordinate cross theorem](quadric_cross_fans.md) and
[univariate-cross theorem](univariate_cross_fans.md) also closes
every ideal containing two distinct quadratic monomials:
\(g(I)=O(n^2\log(n+1))\). Two squares are covered by bounded
univariate cross annihilators; a square and a disjoint product by
the quadratic-coordinate theorem; all remaining pairs share a
linear factor and satisfy (G23). This concerns actual relations,
not merely two quadratic heads in one initial ideal.

## An explicit quadratic fan and its prime-power collapse

For \(m\ge1\), take the same local Gorenstein algebra \(K[t]/t^{2m}\)
with the coordinate map
\[
 x=t,\qquad y=t^m(1+t)^m,\qquad z=t^m(1+t)^{2m}.            \tag{G17}
\]
Its kernel is explicitly
\((x^{2m},y-x^m(1+x)^m,z-x^m(1+x)^{2m})\).
It satisfies \(y^2=yz=z^2=0\). In characteristic zero, and also
in characteristic \(p>3m-1\),
\[
                           g(I_m)=\binom{m+2}{2}.          \tag{G18}
\]
Every standard set contains \(1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1}\).
For every composition \(a+b+c=m\), its remaining monomials are
the prefixes of lengths \(a,b,c\) on
\[
 x^m,x^{m+1},\ldots;\qquad y,xy,\ldots;\qquad z,xz,\ldots .
\]
All these sets are independent. Modulo the compulsory lower ray,
put \(X=1+t\). Unit triangular column operations replace the three
prefix blocks by \(X^e\), for the distinct exponents
\[
 E=\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\sqcup\{m,\ldots,m+b-1\}
                      \sqcup\{2m,\ldots,2m+c-1\}.
\]
The coefficient determinant is the classical binomial Vandermonde
\[
 \det\bigl[\tbinom e j\bigr]_{0\le j<m,\ e\in E}
          =\frac{\prod_{e<f,\ e,f\in E}(f-e)}
                         {\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}j!}>0.          \tag{G19}
\]
All its numerator and denominator factors are less than \(3m\),
which also proves the stated large-characteristic conclusion.

Greedy therefore selects the cheapest \(m\) elements of these three
ray lists. Every composition occurs at positive weights: normalize
\(w_x=1\) and take
\[
 w_y=m+a-b+\tfrac14,\qquad w_z=m+a-c+\tfrac14.
\]
The common cutoff \(m+a-\tfrac14\) separates the selected and next
unselected terms. This proves (G18) for entire cones. Their exact
inequalities compare the last selected term of each nonempty list
with the first unselected term of every nonfull list.
The state profiles form the triangular lattice graph with unit transfers
between \(a,b,c\), so there are \(3m(m+1)/2\) undirected edges.
At a generic wall only two lists have tied frontier costs, and only
the cutoff pair can change the selected set; ties farther down either
list select both terms, or neither. Thus every edge is a unit transfer.
Every such transfer is positively exposed. For an \(a\)-to-\(b\)
edge use \(w_y=m+a-b-1\) and
\(w_z=m+a-c-\tfrac12\), with \(a\ge1\). For a \(b\)-to-\(c\)
edge use \(w_y=m+a-b+\tfrac12\) and
\(w_z=m+a-c-\tfrac12\), with \(b\ge1\).
All these weights are positive and keep the third list's frontier
strictly away from the cutoff. The \(a\)-to-\(c\) case is symmetric.
Their primitive and actual edge-length sums agree and equal
\[
 W(I_m)=\mathcal L(I_m)=m(m+1)(m+2)
       +\sum_{i+j\le m-1}|i-j|=\Theta(m^3).                \tag{G20}
\]
For the edge moving one slot from \(a\) to \(b\), the primitive
cost is \(m+a-b\); the \(a\)-to-\(c\) sum is the same.
For a \(b\)-to-\(c\) edge it is \(|b-c-1|+2\).
Summing gives (G20). This is sharp quadratic/cubic lower order
within (G12); the logarithm in its upper bound is unresolved.

At the prime-power lengths \(m=p^s\) in characteristic \(p\),
Frobenius instead gives \(y=z=x^m\), and
\[
                                g(I_m)=3.                 \tag{G21}
\]
Only the cheapest of the three identical upper ray bases is selected.
This illustrates why a small number of module generators cannot replace
the actual polynomial coefficients in a fan proof. Binomial coefficients
and their prime-digit degeneracies are established; this is not a new
Lucas/Frobenius theorem or a prime-distribution result.

## Priority and scope of the counting specialization

The counting proof uses the existing universal residue matroid, the
planar dyadic bound, ordinary contraction and border-cone recovery.
The identity for the initial ideal of a common polynomial multiple
and Gaussian elimination of a linear ideal are classical too.
Its application to (G12) and (G15), and the explicit example, have
unassessed exact priority. The generalized Vandermonde determinant
and prime-power collapse are classical inputs, not novelty claims.

[Iarrobino--McDaniel--Seceleanu, Theorem 4.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.06297v3)
characterizes graded connected sums through dual generators with a
common contraction and an equality of annihilator ideals. Its grading
and compatibility hypotheses must be checked; it does not supply
the present count for arbitrary additional nonhomogeneous relations.
[Ananthnarayan--Celikbas--Laxmi--Yang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.7600),
Introduction and Theorem 4.3, treat connected sums over the residue
field. That is different from the general shared-\(K[x]\) coupling
in (G4). Neither comparison establishes exact quantitative priority.

These class-wide counts exclude whole proposed lower-bound families.
They still do not settle \(G_3\), bound every two-planar-branch gluing,
or meet the original impact and honest-paper completion criteria.

commuting coordinate matrices. Independent dense elimination checks
the sparse coordinate calculations. Fixtures include all-field sharp
families, their axis padding, Gorenstein uniserial quotients, reduced
point algebras, nonhomogeneous forced-socle quotients and an irreducible
quadratic \(x\)-factor over \(\mathbb F_2\). Full fans of the small
sharp family are compared with its three/four-cone formula.
The one-ray compiler is independently compared with direct full-fan
traversal, including non-downset contraction optimizers and deficient
contracted grounds that must be discarded. The Pascal family's entire
cones, prefix determinants and adjacency costs are checked separately.
Integral linear changes and translations of transverse and embedded
examples test the common-factor theorem through all of its initial
carriers; an individual carrier need not cover the full fan.
Translations preserve leading monomials in global orders; the translated
fixtures are checked against the original entire fans as well.
Source hashes and exact witnesses
are saved with the report. Finite agreement supports the implementation;
the uniform assertions rely on the proofs.
