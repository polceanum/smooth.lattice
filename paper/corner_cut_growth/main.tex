\begin{abstract}
Let \(C_d(n)\) count the \(n\)-point subsets of the nonnegative integer
lattice that can be strictly separated from their complements by an affine
hyperplane. For every fixed \(d\geq2\), we prove
\[
 C_d(n)=\Theta_d\!\bigl(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}\bigr).
\]
The same order holds for the number of complete ordered prefixes of
length \(n\), equivalently the strictly admissible finite monomial
orderings. The set-count upper bound is due to Wagner. For the lower
bound, a finite
sieve produces primitive lattice simplices in separated axis boxes.
Their primitive normal directions are distinguished at a prescribed
cardinality by two integer patches on opposite sides of a boundary
level. Summing over disjoint aspect ranges supplies the logarithmic
factor. This gives a matching lower bound in every fixed dimension,
including the three-dimensional order \(n^2(\log n)^2\).
An active-face dyadic decomposition gives the ordered-prefix upper bound.
We also prove an optimal worst-case prime-realization bound: every
ordered prefix is realizable over distinct primes above \(B\) with
\(\log\max_i p_i=O_d(n\log\max(B,2))\), and some prefixes require
this order. The latter argument combines a linear integer-score
certificate with an established short-interval prime theorem. Qualitative
prime realization and the finite-weight representation are prior results.
\end{abstract}

\section{The counting problem and its scope}
A nonempty finite set \(D\subset\Nzero^d\) is a \emph{corner cut} if
\[
 D=\{x\in\Nzero^d:w\cdot x<t\}
 \quad\text{for some }w\in\R_{>0}^d,\ t>0,
\]
with no lattice point on the cutting hyperplane. Write \(C_d(n)\) for the
number of such sets of cardinality \(n\), and put
\[
 A_d(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_d(j).
\]
The parameter \(C_d(n)\) counts sets, without their internal orders or
representing weights. Every cut is an order ideal for the coordinate order.
In particular, every coordinate of an element of an \(n\)-point cut
is at most \(n-1\), so these counts are finite.

These objects occur as the exponent sets of initial segments of smooth
numbers. For distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_d\), comparing the products
\(\prod_i p_i^{x_i}\) is equivalent to comparing
\(\sum_i x_i\log p_i\). An exponent prefix is consequently a corner cut.
Conversely, every corner cut has such a realization, even with all
primes above any prescribed bound \cite[Proposition 6.2]{Vietri}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:height} bounds the required prime sizes.
Thus the full geometric class is exactly the class of exponent-prefix
sets as the ordered prime bases vary. This does not give a
prime-distribution estimate or a faster random-access algorithm for a
fixed prime set.

Corner cuts were introduced in connection with state polyhedra and
Gr\"obner bases by Onn and Sturmfels \cite{OS}. The planar order
\(C_2(n)=\Theta(n\log n)\) is due to Corteel, R\'emond, Schaeffer and
Thomas \cite{CRST}. For fixed \(d\), Wagner \cite{W02} proved
\[
 \Omega_d(n^{d-1}\log n)\leq C_d(n)
 \leq O_d(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}).
 \tag{1}\label{eq:baseline}
\]
His thesis \cite[Chapter 3]{W03} gives the lower and upper arguments
and suggests seeking greater completion multiplicity. Wagner also
credits R\'emond with independently obtaining the bounds in
\eqref{eq:baseline}. We prove a matching lower bound, improving the
lower logarithmic exponent by \(d-2\) when \(d\geq3\).
We count primitive normals in each of many aspect ranges and prove
that they give distinct prefixes at every sufficiently large prescribed
cardinality. The latter step, rather than a lift from a lower dimension,
is the key to retaining every logarithmic factor.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For each fixed integer \(d\geq2\),
\[
 C_d(n)=\Theta_d\!\bigl(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}\bigr).
\]
Here is one explicit lower bound. Put
\[
 K_d=4^{d+1},\qquad T_d=[64d^2(K_d+1)]^d,\qquad
 R_0(d)=4d^2(d+1),
\]
\[
 n_*(d)=T_d4^{R_0(d)},\qquad
 \rho_d=8^{-(d-1)}(100d)^{-d^2},
\]
and
\[
 \lambda_d=\frac{\rho_d}
 {2^d(4T_d)^{d-1}[4(d+1)]^{d-1}(d-1)!}.
\]
For every integer \(n\geq n_*(d)\),
\[
 C_d(n)\geq\lambda_d n^{d-1}(\log_4 n)^{d-1}.
 \tag{2}\label{eq:explicit}
\]
\end{theorem}
Summing gives \(A_d(n)=\Theta_d(n^d(\log n)^{d-1})\).
The pointwise conclusion matches \eqref{eq:baseline} and strengthens
its lower logarithmic exponent when \(d\geq3\).
For \(d=2\), it recovers the known planar order.

We also count complete ordered prefixes, denoting their number by
\(O_d(n)\). These are precisely Vasiliev--Pavlov's strictly admissible
finite monomial orderings \cite[Definition 5]{VP}; the class and its
enumeration are established background. Theorem~\ref{thm:orders} proves
\(O_d(n)=\Theta_d(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1})\).
Thus retaining the full internal order changes the total count by only a
bounded factor in fixed dimension. Theorem~\ref{thm:height} then gives a
sharp worst-case arithmetic size bound for realizing any such word over
prime bases. All constants may depend on \(d\); the dimension is fixed
throughout.

\section{Primitive simplices in axis boxes}
Fix an ambient integer \(d\geq2\) and set \(\dd=1/(100d)\).
For integer side lengths \(L_1,\ldots,L_d\), put
\[
 m=\min_i L_i,\quad M=\max_i L_i,\quad Q=\prod_{i=1}^{d}L_i.
\]
Define \(B_i\subset\Nzero^d\) by taking the \(j\)th coordinate interval to be
\[
 [L_j,L_j+\floor{\dd L_j}]\quad(j=i),\qquad
 [0,\floor{\dd L_j}]\quad(j\ne i).
\]
A \(d\)-by-\((d-1)\) integer matrix is called primitive if the greatest
common divisor of its maximal minors is one.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:primitive}
Assume \(M\leq m^2\) and \(m\geq2^{8d^2}\).
At least a fraction \(8^{-(d-1)}\) of the ordered choices
\(p_i\in B_i\) have primitive difference matrix
\[
 V=[p_2-p_1\ \cdots\ p_d-p_1].
\]
\end{lemma}
The minor criterion and its equivalence with full column rank modulo
every prime are standard \cite[Propositions 3--4]{MW}.
The point requiring proof here is uniformity for these moving,
possibly anisotropic boxes.

\begin{proof}
Keep the same \(\dd\) throughout a dimension induction.
Let \(E_r\) be the event that the first \(r\) points, projected onto the
first \(r\) coordinates, have primitive difference matrix.
The event \(E_1\) is automatic, with empty minor equal to one.
At step \(2\leq r\leq d\), fix a primitive configuration of the previous
\(r-1\) projected points. Its difference matrix \(U\) has size
\((r-1)\)-by-\((r-2)\). Its vector \(a_0\) of signed maximal minors
is primitive, and \(U\) has rank \(r-2\) modulo every prime.

Let \(u\) be the first \(r-1\) coordinates of \(p_r-p_1\), and put
\(D=\det[U\ u]\). This determinant is nonzero for every allowed choice.
Indeed, the normalized row matrix of the previous \(r-1\) projected
points differs from the identity by at most \((r-1)\dd\) in maximum
row-sum norm. The normal of their affine span, normalized to height one,
has scaled coordinates within
\((r-1)\dd/(1-(r-1)\dd)\) of one and is positive.
The new projected point has every scaled coordinate at most \(\dd\);
it is strictly on the origin side of that affine span.
Also,
\[
 |D|\leq (r-1)!\,2^{r-1}M^{r-1}
       \leq2^{d^2}m^{2(d-1)}.                    \tag{3}\label{eq:D}
\]

Every coordinate interval contains more than \(\dd m\) integers.
For a prime \(\ell\), the equation \(D=0\pmod\ell\) is a nontrivial
linear equation in the new projected point: at least one coordinate
of \(a_0\) is nonzero modulo \(\ell\).
Its probability is at most \(1/\ell+a/m\), where \(a=100d\).

Suppose \(\ell\mid D\). Since \(U\) has full column rank modulo
\(\ell\), there is a unique vector \(\lambda\) with
\(u=U\lambda\pmod\ell\). Write \(z\) for the \(r\)th row of the old
columns and \(u_r\) for the \(r\)th coordinate of the new column.
The full \(r\)-by-\((r-1)\) matrix loses rank modulo \(\ell\) precisely if
\[
 u_r=z\lambda\pmod\ell.                         \tag{4}\label{eq:residue}
\]
Conditional on everything except the last coordinate of \(p_r\),
this is one residue class, of probability at most \(1/\ell+a/m\).
Thus the probability of failure at any particular prime is at most
\((1/\ell+a/m)^2\).

Put \(s=\floor{(\log_2 m)/2}\), \(P=2^s\).
The small-prime union bound gives
\begin{align}
 \Pr(\text{failure at a prime }\ell\leq P)
 &\leq\sum_{\substack{\ell\leq P\\\ell\ {\rm prime}}}
                 (1/\ell+a/m)^2 \notag\\
 &\leq\frac34+\frac{2a(s+1)}{2^{2s}}+
                      \frac{a^2}{2^{3s}}.       \tag{5}\label{eq:small}
\end{align}
Here \(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}j^{-2}<3/4\) follows by comparison with
\(1/4+\int_2^\infty x^{-2}\,dx\).
The cross term uses \(H_P\leq1+\log P\leq1+s\), and the constant
term uses at most \(P\) primes.

For large primes, first fix the new projected coordinates.
Every bad prime \(\ell>P\) divides the same nonzero integer \(D\).
By \eqref{eq:D}, their number is at most
\[
 \frac{\log_2|D|}{s}
 \leq4(d-1)+\frac{d^2+4(d-1)}{s}
 \leq b:=4d-3
 \quad\text{if }s\geq d^2+4(d-1).
\]
For each of these finitely many primes, \eqref{eq:residue} bounds the
conditional last-coordinate failure probability by \(2^{-s}+a2^{-2s}\).
Their total contribution is therefore at most \(b(2^{-s}+a2^{-2s})\).

Define
\[
 E_d(s)=\frac{2a(s+1)}{2^{2s}}+\frac{a^2}{2^{3s}}
                  +b(2^{-s}+a2^{-2s}).          \tag{6}\label{eq:error}
\]
This decreases for \(s\geq1\).
For every integer \(d\geq2\), the choice \(S_d=4d^2\) satisfies
\(S_d\geq d^2+4(d-1)\) and \(E_d(S_d)<2^{-10}<1/8\).
For an explicit verification valid in every dimension, use
\(a\leq2^{4d}\), \(b\leq2^{2d}\) and \(4d^2+1\leq2^{3d}\).
They give
\[
 E_d(4d^2)\leq
 2^{1+7d-8d^2}+2^{8d-12d^2}
       +2^{2d-4d^2}+2^{6d-8d^2}<2^{-10}.
\]
The four exponents decrease for \(d\geq2\), and at \(d=2\)
are \(-17,-32,-12,-20\).

Consequently the conditional probability of a nonprimitive extension
is at most \(3/4+E_d(s)\leq7/8\).
To justify the conditioning across steps, the events
\(E_1,\ldots,E_{r-1}\) depend only on previous projected coordinates.
After these are fixed, the old \(r\)th coordinates and the new point
retain their product distributions. Thus
\[
 \Pr(E_r\mid E_1,\ldots,E_{r-1})\geq1/8.
\]
Multiplication proves that \(\Pr(E_1\cap\cdots\cap E_d)\geq8^{-(d-1)}\).
This intersection implies \(E_d\), as required.
All bounds on large primes were finite before any limit was taken.
\end{proof}

\section{Primitive normals at a prescribed size}
For each primitive frame from Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive}, orient its
cofactor normal \(a\) positively and put \(H_a=a\cdot p_1\).
Let \(P\) have rows \(p_i\). Its column-scaled matrix differs from the
identity by a matrix of maximum row-sum norm at most \(d\dd=1/100\).
The determinant is positive and every eigenvalue lies within \(1/100\)
of one. Consequently
\[
 H_a=\det P,\qquad 2^{-d}Q\leq H_a\leq2^dQ.
 \tag{7}\label{eq:heights}
\]
The solution of \(p_i\cdot w=1\) satisfies
\(\lvert L_iw_i-1\rvert\leq d\dd/(1-d\dd)<1/10\).
Since \(w=a/H_a\), this gives
\[
 \frac9{10}<\frac{a_iL_i}{H_a}<\frac{11}{10}.
 \tag{8}\label{eq:normalbox}
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:normals}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive}, these frames give
at least \(2^{-d}\rho_dQ^{d-1}\) distinct primitive positive normals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Primitivity gives an integer \(z\) with \(a\cdot z=1\).
The matrix \([V\ z]\) is unimodular, so the columns of \(V\) form a
basis of the entire integer kernel of \(a\).
If two points of the same \(B_i\) lie in the selected affine plane,
their difference is \(Vc\), with \(c\in\Z^{d-1}\).
The submatrix in rows \(2,\ldots,d\), after row scaling by the \(L_i\),
differs from the identity by at most \((d-1)\dd\) in maximum row-sum
norm. Thus
\[
 \norm{c}\leq\frac{\dd}{1-(d-1)\dd}<1,
\]
so the two points agree. Each plane determines its selected simplex.
Each box has at least \(\dd^dQ\) points, so the number of distinct
planes is at least \(\rho_dQ^d\).
For any fixed primitive normal, the integer height in
\eqref{eq:heights} has at most \(2^dQ\) possibilities. Division proves
the claim.
\end{proof}

When one normal occurs at several heights, fix one witnessing simplex
for it, and use that simplex to define \(H_a\).
The prefix below depends only on \(a\), not on this choice of witness.

For a positive integer normal \(a\), order lattice points by
\((a\cdot x,x_1,\ldots,x_d)\), and let \(D_n(a)\) be the first \(n\).
Let \(h\) be the least integer for which
\[
 F_a(h):=\card{\{x\in\Nzero^d:a\cdot x\leq h\}}\geq n.
\]
The set \(D_n(a)\) is a corner cut, including when the score-\(h\)
level is only partly included. Indeed
\(h\leq(n-1)\min_i a_i\), by considering a minimum-weight axis.
Every point at score at most \(h\) lies in \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^d\).
For \(n>1\), the integer vector \(u_i=n^{d-i}\) represents lexicographic
order in that box, and \(u\cdot x\leq n^d-1\) there.
Taking \(w=n^d a+u\) preserves all these levels and
strictly orders the boundary level. Positivity keeps every higher
\(a\)-level above them. A threshold between consecutive integer scores
gives a strict representation. The case \(n=1\) is immediate.
We will use the weak separation property
\[
 b\cdot x\leq b\cdot y
 \quad(x\in D_n(b),\ y\notin D_n(b)).
 \tag{9}\label{eq:weak}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-size injectivity]\label{lem:inject}
Let \(a,b\) be primitive positive normals furnished by the same aspect
box. Put \(K=4^{d+1}\). If the boundary height \(h\) of \(D_n(a)\)
satisfies
\[
 h/H_a\geq16d^2(K+1),
 \tag{10}\label{eq:deep}
\]
then \(D_n(a)=D_n(b)\) implies \(a=b\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the selected frame \(V=[v_1\ \cdots\ v_{d-1}]\) for \(a\).
Since \(V\) is a basis of its integer kernel, an integer solution
of \(a\cdot z=1\) can be reduced modulo \(V\) to give
\[
 z=p_1/H_a+Vc,\qquad |c_j|\leq1/2.
 \tag{11}\label{eq:bezout}
\]
Each entry satisfies \(\lvert(v_j)_i\rvert<2L_i\), so
\(\lvert z_i\rvert\leq(d+1)L_i\leq2dL_i\).
Let \(y_i=(h-1)/(d a_i)\). Reduce \((h-1)z\) modulo \(V\) about
\(y\), obtaining an integer \(x_0\) such that
\[
 a\cdot x_0=h-1,\qquad x_0-y=V\beta,\qquad |\beta_j|\leq1/2.
\]
Both integer patches
\[
 \{x_0+Vk:k\in\Z^{d-1},\ |k_j|\leq K\},\qquad
 \{x_0+2z+Vk:k\in\Z^{d-1},\ |k_j|\leq K\}
 \tag{12}\label{eq:patches}
\]
are nonnegative. To see this coordinatewise, the displacement from
\(y_i\) is less than \((2Kd+5d)L_i\), while
\eqref{eq:normalbox} and \eqref{eq:deep} give
\[
 y_i/L_i>\frac{h-1}{2dH_a}>2Kd+5d.
\]
Their \(a\)-scores are respectively \(h-1\) and \(h+1\).
Thus the first patch is entirely inside \(D_n(a)\), the second
entirely outside; the score-\(h\) tie is irrelevant.

Assume the two prefixes agree, and put \(S=\sum_j|b\cdot v_j|\).
By \eqref{eq:weak}, the maximum \(b\)-score on the first patch cannot
exceed the minimum on the second. Hence
\[
 KS\leq b\cdot z
 \leq b\cdot p_1/H_a+S/2.
 \tag{13}\label{eq:patchineq}
\]
For two normals in the same aspect family,
\eqref{eq:heights} gives \(H_b/H_a\leq4^d\), and
\eqref{eq:normalbox} gives
\[
 \frac{b\cdot p_1}{H_a}
 <4^d\frac{11}{10}(1+d\dd)<2\cdot4^d.
\]
It follows that \((K-1/2)S<2\cdot4^d<K-1/2\).
But \(S\) is an integer, so \(S=0\). Thus \(b\) annihilates every
column of \(V\) and is parallel to \(a\). Primitivity and positivity
force \(a=b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:extents}
If \(n/Q\geq T_d\) and \(m\geq2^{8d^2}\), then
\eqref{eq:deep} holds. With \(G=(d!n/Q)^{1/d}\), the maximum
\(i\)th axis coordinate of \(D_n(a)\) lies strictly between
\(GL_i/2\) and \(2GL_i\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The disjoint half-open unit cubes based at the points counted by
\(F_a(t)\) contain the positive simplex of height \(t\), and are
contained in the simplex of height \(t+\sum_i a_i\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{t^d}{d!\prod_i a_i}\leq F_a(t)
 \leq\frac{(t+\sum_i a_i)^d}{d!\prod_i a_i}\qquad(t\geq0).
\]
Use \(F_a(h-1)<n\leq F_a(h)\) and \eqref{eq:normalbox} to get
\[
 \frac9{10}G-\frac{11d}{10m}
 <\frac h{H_a}<\frac{11}{10}G+\frac1{H_a}.
\]
Since \(m\geq10d\) and \(G\geq64d^2(K_d+1)\), this implies
\(4G/5<h/H_a<6G/5\), and also \eqref{eq:deep}.
If \(M_i\) is the axis maximum, all integer axis points below score
\(h\) are included and none above it are included. Thus
\(h/a_i-2<M_i\leq h/a_i\).
Combining these inequalities with \eqref{eq:normalbox} gives
\(GL_i/2<M_i<2GL_i\).
\end{proof}

\section{Summing aspect ranges}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The upper bound is Wagner's theorem \cite{W02}; see also
\cite[Theorem 3.15]{W03}. For the lower bound, take
\(R\) maximal with \(Q=4^R\leq n/T_d\). For every tuple
\[
 r_1+\cdots+r_d=R,\qquad r_i\geq\ceil{R/(d+1)},
\]
put \(L_i=4^{r_i}\). When \(n\geq n_*(d)\), these tuples satisfy
\(R\geq R_0(d)\), \(\min_iL_i\geq2^{8d^2}\), and
\(\max_iL_i\leq(\min_iL_i)^2\).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:normals}--\ref{lem:extents} therefore give
at least \(2^{-d}\rho_dQ^{d-1}\) different \(n\)-point cuts per tuple.

The value \(G\) in Lemma~\ref{lem:extents} is common to all tuples.
Two distinct tuples differ by a factor of at least four in one \(L_i\).
The open observable ranges \((GL_i/2,2GL_i)\) are disjoint for that
coordinate, so no cut is counted twice across tuples.

Put \(r=R-d\ceil{R/(d+1)}\). The number of tuples is
\[
 \binom{r+d-1}{d-1}
 \geq\frac{R^{d-1}}{[2(d+1)]^{d-1}(d-1)!},
\]
since \(r\geq R/(d+1)-d\geq R/[2(d+1)]\).
Also \(Q>n/(4T_d)\) and \(R\geq(\log_4n)/2\).
For the latter explicit bound, \(d^2\leq4^{d-1}\) gives
\(T_d\leq4^{2d^2+4d}\), and
\(R_0(d)\geq2d^2+4d+2\).
Multiplying the counts yields \eqref{eq:explicit}.
\end{proof}

\section{Counting complete ordered prefixes}\label{sec:orders}
Let \(\mathcal O_d(n)\) consist of ordered tuples
\(W=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) of the first \(n\) points of \(\Nzero^d\)
in increasing \(w\cdot x\), as positive vectors \(w\) with rationally
independent coordinates vary. Put \(O_d(n)=\card{\mathcal O_d(n)}\).
Coordinates are labeled. Forgetting the internal order maps this class
onto the \(n\)-point corner cuts. Indeed, strict separation is controlled
by the finitely many minimal excluded points, so a separator may be
perturbed to have rationally independent coordinates.

We record explicitly the known connection with global monomial orders.
The statement concerns each finite initial interval, and does not assert
that one scalar vector represents an entire infinite monomial order.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-weight representation]\label{lem:finiteweight}
The words in \(\mathcal O_d(n)\) are exactly the length-\(n\) initial
intervals of global monomial orders.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A positive rationally independent vector gives a total, multiplicative
well-order on monomials, since only finitely many exponents have bounded
score. Conversely, Robbiano's representation \cite{Robbiano} expresses a
global monomial order by lexicographic comparison of finitely many real
linear forms \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\).
For an initial interval \(W\), put \(D=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\)
and let \(E\) be the coordinatewise minimal points outside \(D\).
The set \(E\) is finite: all its coordinates are at most one more
than the corresponding coordinate maximum of \(D\).
Each vector in the finite family
\[
 \{e_i:1\leq i\leq d\}\ \cup\
 \{x_{j+1}-x_j:1\leq j<n\}\ \cup\
 \{b-x_n:b\in E\}
\]
has lexicographically positive image under these forms. Consequently
\(w(\varepsilon)=u_1+\varepsilon u_2+\cdots+
\varepsilon^{s-1}u_s\) is positive on the whole family for sufficiently
small \(\varepsilon>0\). The unit vectors make its coordinates positive;
the other inequalities give the prescribed order and put the entire
excluded border after it. Every excluded point dominates a border point,
so positivity controls the infinite complement. A small generic
perturbation preserves these finite inequalities and gives rational
independence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ordered-prefix count]\label{thm:orders}
For every fixed \(d\geq2\),
\[
 C_d(n)\leq O_d(n)=O_d\!\left(n^{d-1}(\log(n+1))^{d-1}\right).
\]
In combination with Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, this gives
\(O_d(n)=\Theta_d(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The case \(n=1\) is immediate. For a witnessing weight \(w\), put
\(t=w\cdot x_n>0\), and restrict to the active coordinates
\(J=\{i:w_i\leq t\}\). Write \(r=\card J\geq1\).
All other coordinates vanish throughout the prefix. In this face put
\[
 b_i=t/w_i\geq1,\qquad
 L_i=2^{\floor{\log_2 b_i}},\qquad Q=\prod_iL_i=2^R.
\]
The simplex of height \(t\) is covered by the unit cubes based at its
\(n\) lattice points, so \(\prod_i b_i\leq r!n\).
The bounding box gives
\(n\leq\prod_i(\floor{b_i}+1)\leq2^r\prod_i b_i\).
Since \(L_i\leq b_i<2L_i\),
\[
 n<4^rQ,\qquad Q\leq r!n.                    \tag{14}\label{eq:order-shell}
\]
For fixed \(r\), the integer \(R\) occupies a bounded-width interval
and is at most \(\log_2n+\log_2(r!)\).
For each \(R\), there are \(\binom{R+r-1}{r-1}\) possible tuples
of nonnegative integer exponents. Thus the number of aspect tuples is
\[
 O_r\!\left((\log(n+1))^{r-1}\right).
\]
The lower
product bound in \eqref{eq:order-shell} is essential here.

For one such tuple, all prefix points lie in
\(G_L=\prod_i\{0,\ldots,2L_i-1\}\).
Comparisons within this box change on central hyperplanes
\(w\cdot v=0\), where \(v\ne0\) is an integer vector with
\(\lvert v_i\rvert\leq2L_i-1\). Up to sign, there are at most
\[
 \frac{\prod_i(4L_i-1)-1}{2}\leq4^rQ\leq4^rr!n
\]
such hyperplanes. An arrangement of \(m\geq1\) central hyperplanes in
\(\R^r\) has at most
\[
 2\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\binom{m-1}{j}=O_r(m^{r-1})
\]
chambers.
The bound follows by adding one hyperplane at a time and inducting on
the restricted central arrangement; degeneracies only reduce the count.

Within a chamber the order on \(G_L\) is fixed, and any global prefix
whose witness has this chart must be its first \(n\) points.
Thus there is at most one word per chart and chamber. Charts that are
not realized, and words counted in more than one chart, only overcount.
There are consequently
\(O_r(n^{r-1}(\log(n+1))^{r-1})\) words for each active set \(J\).
Summing over its \(\binom dr\) choices and over \(1\leq r\leq d\)
proves the upper bound. The surjection onto corner cuts gives the lower
bound \(C_d(n)\leq O_d(n)\).
\end{proof}

In particular, the mean number of admissible internal orders per
\(n\)-point corner cut is \(O_d(n)/C_d(n)=\Theta_d(1)\).
This is an average statement and gives no constant bound for each shape.
The upper argument also recovers Wagner's set-count upper order;
its original attribution is unchanged.

\section{An optimal prime-size bound}\label{sec:primeheight}
Qualitative realization by distinct prime bases follows from Vietri's
finite-inequality theorem \cite[Proposition 6.2]{Vietri}, including any
prescribed lower bound on the primes. Consecutive comparisons and the
minimal excluded border specialize that result to ordered prefixes.
We now bound the worst-case size of the realizing primes.

\begin{lemma}[Linear integer-score certificate]\label{lem:linear-score}
Put \(c_d=d(d!)^2 2^{d-1}\). Every \(W\in\mathcal O_d(n)\) has a
positive integer weight vector \(A\) realizing exactly the same ordered
prefix, with all included scores distinct. Its final score \(S=A\cdot x_n\)
satisfies
\[
 n-1\leq S\leq c_dn,\qquad
 \max_iA_i\leq S+1.                         \tag{15}\label{eq:linear-score}
\]
For \(n=1\), take \(A=(1,\ldots,1)\) and \(S=0\).
\end{lemma}
The proof uses a separation LP, determinant bounds and a simplex product
estimate. These are established methods \cite{OS,W02}; we include the
certificate bound as a supporting lemma, without a separate claim of a
new general weight-compression method.
\begin{proof}
Assume \(n>1\), choose a strict positive witnessing vector \(w\), and
put \(T=w\cdot x_n\). Restrict to the \(r\) active coordinates
\(w_i\leq T\), and write \(B_i=\floor{T/w_i}\geq1\).
In this face \(D=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\) is the set of all lattice
points of score at most \(T\). Its based unit cubes cover the simplex,
and hence
\[
 \prod_iB_i\leq\frac{T^r}{\prod_iw_i}\leq r!n.
\]
Let \(E\) be the minimal excluded border in this \(r\)-dimensional
face. Each \(b\in E\) has \(b_i\leq B_i+1\), and every excluded
point dominates an element of \(E\). The finite system
\[
 z_i\geq1,\quad (x_{j+1}-x_j)\cdot z\geq1\ (1\leq j<n),
 \quad (b-x_n)\cdot z\geq1\ (b\in E)
\]
is feasible after scaling \(w\). Minimize \(\sum_i z_i\);
bounded sublevel sets are compact, so the optimal face has a vertex.
At that vertex choose \(r\) independent tight rows, giving an integer
matrix \(M\) with \(Mz=\boldsymbol1\).
Every entry in column \(i\) has absolute value at most \(2B_i\).
Cramer's rule gives positive integers \(A_i=\lvert\det M\rvert z_i\)
with
\[
 A_i\leq r!2^{r-1}\prod_{j\ne i}B_j.
\]
All unit-margin constraints remain satisfied. Thus they give the
prescribed order and strictly exclude the entire complement, and
\[
 S\leq\sum_i B_iA_i
 \leq r(r!)^2 2^{r-1}n\leq c_dn.
\]
Each active unit vector belongs to \(D\), so \(A_i\leq S\).
Restore inactive coordinates with weight \(S+1\); every point using
one of them is excluded. Finally, the \(n\) distinct nonnegative
integer scores start at zero, so \(S\geq n-1\).
\end{proof}

For \(B>0\) and \(W\in\mathcal O_d(n)\), let \(h_B(W)\) be the
minimum of \(\log\max_i p_i\) over ordered tuples of distinct primes
\(p_i>B\) realizing \(W\). Let
\(H_d(n,B)=\max_{W\in\mathcal O_d(n)}h_B(W)\).
All logarithms in this section are natural. Qualitative realization
ensures these minima exist; there are finitely many words.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp worst-case prime height]\label{thm:height}
For each fixed \(d\geq2\), uniformly for \(n\geq2\) and \(B>0\),
\[
 H_d(n,B)=\Theta_d\bigl(n\log\max(B,2)\bigr).
\]
More explicitly, put \(\alpha=\max\{30,d,\log\max(B,2)+1\}\).
Then
\[
 (n-1)\log\max(B,2)\leq H_d(n,B)
 <(\alpha+\log2)(c_dn+1)+1.                 \tag{16}\label{eq:primeheight}
\]
The same bounds hold for the worst-case minimum when only an underlying
\(n\)-point corner-cut set is prescribed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take the certificate of Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-score}, and sort its
coordinates temporarily as \(m=A_1\leq A_2\leq\cdots\leq A_d\).
For distinct \(i,j\),
\[
 \operatorname{lcm}(A_i,A_j)>S.              \tag{17}\label{eq:axis-collision}
\]
Otherwise two distinct pure-axis lattice points of that common score
would both be included, contradicting their strict integer order.
Consequently \(A_j^2\geq mA_j>n-1\) for every \(j\geq2\).
Thus at most one weight can be as small as \(\sqrt{n-1}\).

Our arithmetic input is Dusart's unconditional estimate
\cite[Proposition 5.4]{Dusart}: for real \(x\geq89693\) there is a
prime \(x<p\leq x(1+1/(\log x)^3)\).
In particular, for \(x\geq e^{30}\),
\[
 x<p<x\left(1+\frac{1}{25(\log x)^2}\right).
                                                        \tag{18}\label{eq:primeinterval}
\]
Put \(\varepsilon=1/[25\alpha^2(n-1)]\).
Bertrand's postulate \cite{Erdos} gives a prime
\(e^{\alpha m}<p_1<2e^{\alpha m}\). Define
\[
 T=\frac{\log p_1}{m},\quad \alpha<T<\alpha+\log2,
 \qquad X_j=e^{TA_j}(1+\varepsilon)^{j-2}\quad(2\leq j\leq d).
\]
By \eqref{eq:axis-collision}, \(\log X_j>\alpha\sqrt{n-1}\)
and \(X_j\geq e^{30}\). Formula~\eqref{eq:primeinterval} supplies
\[
 X_j<p_j<X_j(1+\varepsilon).
\]
The intervals are disjoint since \(A_{j+1}\geq A_j\), even if some
larger weights agree; also \(X_2\geq p_1\).
Hence all chosen primes are distinct and greater than \(B\).
Writing \(\eta_i=\log p_i-TA_i\), we have
\[
 \eta_1=0,\qquad 0<\eta_j<(d-1)\varepsilon\quad(j\geq2).
\]
Every point \(x\) of an \(n\)-point downset has
\(\sum_i x_i\leq n-1\), by following a unit-step path from zero to
\(x\). It follows that
\[
 0\leq\eta\cdot x<\frac{d-1}{25\alpha^2}<\alpha<T
 \qquad(x\in D).                            \tag{19}\label{eq:height-error}
\]
For successive included \(x,y\), the integer-score gap is at least
one, so
\(\log(p^y)-\log(p^x)\geq T-\eta\cdot x>0\).
Every excluded \(y\) has \(A\cdot y\geq S+1\) and
\(\eta\cdot y\geq0\). Every included product has logarithm
less than \(T(S+1)\). This preserves the entire ordered prefix and
controls its infinite complement. Undo the coordinate permutation.
The certificate bound and \((d-1)\varepsilon<1\) give
\[
 \log\max_i p_i<T\max_iA_i+1
 <(\alpha+\log2)(c_dn+1)+1.
\]

For the lower bound, use the admissible word
\(W=(0,e_1,2e_1,\ldots,(n-1)e_1)\).
Every realization has \(p_j>p_1^{n-1}\) for \(j\geq2\), since
\(e_j\) is excluded. Thus
\(h_B(W)\geq(n-1)\log\max(B,2)\).
This same set proves the unordered lower bound, and the ordered upper
bound applies after choosing any admissible internal order.
Since \(\alpha=O_d(\log\max(B,2))\) and \(n-1\geq n/2\), the
asserted uniform orders follow.
\end{proof}

For \(n=1\), repeated Bertrand intervals give
\(\log\max_i p_i<\log\max(B,2)+d\log2\).
The prime-interval estimate is an established analytic input, not a new
prime-gap result. The bound above does not supply an efficient search
for the realizing primes or improve unranking for fixed input bases.

\begin{corollary}[A bounded prime budget realizes all prefixes]\label{cor:budget}
For \(n\geq2\) and \(B>0\), restricting the ordered prime bases to
\[
 B<p_i,\qquad
 \log\max_i p_i<(\alpha+\log2)(c_dn+1)+1
\]
leaves both the set count \(C_d(n)\) and the word count \(O_d(n)\)
unchanged. Their common asymptotic order is
\(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}\). For fixed \(d,B\), primes of
\(O_{d,B}(n)\) bits suffice for every pattern; some patterns require
\(\Omega(n)\) bits.
\end{corollary}
This counts distinct exponent patterns, not realizing prime tuples.
For one fixed tuple there is exactly one prefix of each length.

\section{Further consequences and limitations}\label{sec:consequences}
The theorem also determines the average completion multiplicity raised
in Wagner's discussion \cite[Section 3.5]{W03}. Write
\(\mathcal C_{d-1}(\leq n)\) for all nonempty corner cuts of size at
most \(n\) in dimension \(d-1\). For each such cut \(T\), let
\[
 F_n(T)=\card{\{D:\card D=n,\ D\text{ a corner cut in }
 \Nzero^d,\ D\cap\{x_d=0\}=T\times\{0\}\}}.
\]
\begin{corollary}[Average completion multiplicity]\label{cor:fibres}
For every fixed \(d\geq2\),
\[
 \frac{1}{\card{\mathcal C_{d-1}(\leq n)}}
 \sum_{T\in\mathcal C_{d-1}(\leq n)}F_n(T)
 =\Theta_d(\log n).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Restriction to \(x_d=0\) sends every nonempty corner cut to a nonempty
corner cut. Its fibres partition the \(n\)-point cuts, so the displayed
mean equals \(C_d(n)/A_{d-1}(n)\).
For \(d\geq3\), Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and its cumulative consequence
give the numerator and denominator orders
\(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}\) and
\(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-2}\), respectively.
For \(d=2\), there is one one-dimensional cut of each positive size,
so \(A_1(n)=n\), giving the same conclusion.
\end{proof}
The restriction, fibre identity and lifting argument are established
background \cite[Lemma 3.5 and Section 3.5]{W03}; the corollary records
the consequence of the new matching count. It is a mean over all slices,
not a lower bound for each slice. For example, \(\card T=n\) forces
\(F_n(T)=1\).

There is an established geometric interpretation.
For a cut \(D\), put \(\alpha_D=\sum_{x\in D}x\).
For fixed size \(n\), these vectors are distinct and each is a vertex
of their convex hull. Indeed, weights strictly cutting off \(D\)
make \(w\cdot\alpha_D\) smaller than \(w\cdot\alpha_E\) for every
other \(n\)-point set \(E\): each replacement point has greater
weight than every removed point. Thus \(\alpha_D\) is uniquely exposed.
Conversely every vertex of the finite hull is one of its generators.
The vertex count of the corner-cut polytope is consequently \(C_d(n)\).
This correspondence is known \cite{OS,Muller,BMORW}; in particular,
M\"uller \cite[Lemma 2.6]{Muller} gives the exposed-vertex argument.
Our contribution is the
quantitative lower count, not the dictionary.
For generic experimental designs the same count describes their
corner-cut models \cite[Definition 7 and Theorem 2]{BMORW}.

For fixed \(B\), let \(S_{d,B}(n)\) count distinct exponent sets of
length \(n\) obtained over ordered tuples of distinct primes greater
than \(B\). Count each set once, however many tuples realize it.
Qualitative realization and the converse observation in Section 1 give
\[
 S_{d,B}(n)=C_d(n)=\Theta_d(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}),
\]\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n S_{d,B}(j)
   =\Theta_d\!\bigl(n^d(\log n)^{d-1}\bigr).
\]
The qualitative equality is a consequence of Vietri's earlier theorem.
Corollary~\ref{cor:budget} strengthens it by imposing the explicit,
asymptotically optimal worst-case prime-size budget.

The pointwise count gives a matching asymptotic order in every fixed
dimension and for the established polytope and model dictionaries.
It supplies no asymptotic leading constant, no new enumeration algorithm,
no uniform claim as \(d\) grows with \(n\), and no fixed-prime unranking
complexity improvement. The prescribed-size step is Lemma~\ref{lem:inject};
the earlier cumulative construction alone did not give that conclusion.

\section{Related work and reproducibility}
The lower bound concerns the same cardinality count as Wagner's
\eqref{eq:baseline}. The proof uses three ingredients: a uniform finite
primitive-frame estimate, injectivity at a prescribed size, and disjoint
aspect ranges. We describe the relevant background for these steps.
M\"uller \cite[Sections 2--5]{Muller} studies the same polytopes,
including their planar vertex order and adjacency. Her enumeration
discussion \cite[Section 1, p.~324]{Muller} cites the planar result
and Wagner's bounds; her structural theorems do not supply the
bounded-cardinality count proved here.

The unrestricted density of rectangular primitive integer matrices is
known \cite{MRW,MW}. The later paper repairs a defect in the earlier
density proof. A centered-cube density alone does not assert the
uniform estimate for our moving boxes; Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive} gives
a direct finite proof. We claim no new minor criterion or unrestricted
density formula.

Chen, Feng, Liu and Wu \cite[Theorem 1]{CFLW} give finite probability
bounds for appending independent rows to a prescribed primitive matrix.
Their random entries use a common interval of length at least the
maximum prescribed entry. Our boxes have translated intervals of
different lengths, with their shortest length potentially much smaller
than the largest entry. That theorem is not a direct substitute for
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive}. Prime-by-prime rank analysis and finite
determinant cutoffs are established methods; our claimed contribution
is their use in the multiscale corner-cut count. The additional fixed-size
step is the two-patch comparison in Lemma~\ref{lem:inject}.

Here is a precise link with finite-grid threshold functions.
Put \(G_n=\{0,\ldots,n\}^d\). An \(n\)-point corner cut is exactly
an \(n\)-set of \(G_n\) containing the origin, where an \(n\)-set is
a subset strictly separable from its complement in \(G_n\).
The forward implication follows from the coordinate bound in Section 1.
For the converse, write a separator as \(w\cdot x<t\), with \(t>0\)
since the origin is included. If \(w_i\leq0\), all \(n+1\) points
\(0,e_i,\ldots,ne_i\) are included, a contradiction. Thus \(w_i>0\).
Also \(ne_i\) is excluded for every \(i\), so \(nw_i>t\);
every nonnegative lattice point outside \(G_n\) has score greater
than \(t\). This proves the converse. Wagner
\cite[Section 3.2]{W03} uses a smaller finite ground set for enumeration;
the finite-set reduction itself is not new.

The counting parameter matters even after this identification.
Haukkanen and Merikoski \cite[Equations (1) and (9)]{HM} count all
threshold dichotomies of an \(m\)-by-\(r\) grid, obtaining
\[
 t(m,r)=\frac6{\pi^2}(mr)^2+O(mr^2)\qquad(2\leq m\leq r).
\]
This total sums over every possible included cardinality. In particular,
\(t(n+1,n+1)=\Theta(n^4)\), while already
\(C_2(n)=\Theta(n\log n)\). A lower estimate for the total does not
give a lower estimate for the selected \(n\)-point class at the origin.
Lemma~\ref{lem:inject} supplies the prescribed-size control in our
construction; the known total-grid count is not being presented as
a new result.

Constraining a normal by points on parallel boundary levels also has
precedents in digital-plane recognition. Coeurjolly et al.
\cite[Proposition 6 and Theorems 8 and 11]{CSD}
describe feasible plane parameters using upper and lower leaning polygons,
under projection or connected-slice hypotheses.
Our argument requires the quantitative conclusion that bounded primitive
integer normals are distinguished at every sufficiently large prescribed
cardinality. We do not claim the general principle of boundary-point
recognition as new.

The Hilbert zonotope refines state polyhedra and supports polynomial-time
computation of universal Gr\"obner bases in fixed dimension
\cite[Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.5]{BOT}.
Its count is an upper bound for a common refinement, not the lower
bounded-cardinality count proved here.

Elizalde and Galv\'an discuss higher-dimensional pyramidal partitions
and refer to Wagner's growth bounds \cite[Section 8.2]{EGpre}.
Our comparison uses the preprint version \cite{EGpre}; the journal
article is listed separately as \cite{EGjournal}.

Kalka and Kuhnt \cite[Section 2.2, p.~6]{KK} explicitly restate
Wagner's lower and upper orders for corner cuts, while treating a
numerical statistical fan. That passage does not supply a stronger
corner-cut counting bound or a proof of our prescribed-size lemma.

Vasiliev and Pavlov \cite[Definition 5 and Section 6]{VP}
call the finite initial intervals of global monomial orders
\emph{strictly admissible} orderings. Their parameter is the number of
monomials in the interval, and Tables 7--8 enumerate ordered intervals
in two and three variables. Forgetting their internal order gives
corner-cut sets; their wider finite \emph{admissible} class need not
form an initial interval \cite[Remark 1]{VP}.
The finite-weight representation and recursive border/cone enumeration
are established background. The full paper supplies algorithms and
numerical tables, but no asymptotic corner-cut count, no prescribed-size
lower bound of the order in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and no quantitative
prime-realization bound. Our contribution concerns the uniform
cardinality count, rather than introducing this family of finite orders.

\paragraph{Scope of the comparison.}
The count in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} improves the lower bound of
\cite{W02,W03} and matches their upper bound. The restriction and lifting
viewpoint, primitive-matrix methods, boundary-point recognition, and
polytope and model correspondences are attributed above. The improvement
is the resulting uniform cardinality count. Theorem~\ref{thm:orders}
extends the count to complete words in the established finite-ordering
class. Theorem~\ref{thm:height} bounds prime height rather than asserting
a new qualitative realization result. Robbiano's finite-weight
consequence, determinant compression methods and Dusart's interval
estimate retain their original attribution. The accompanying research
audit records precisely which source versions and statements were
inspected, including the use of the author's thesis proofs for
\cite{W02} and the deferred journal-version check for \cite{EGjournal}.
It is available at \path{results/research/corner_cut_growth_audit.json}.

\paragraph{Exact checks.}
The standard-library Python checker
\path{scripts/run_corner_cut_growth_checks.py}
uses independent determinants, modular Gaussian elimination and exact
rational feasibility. It checks primitive-plane injectivity, the
conditional residue bound and a nonprimitive-projection negative control.
The prescribed-size checks construct integer Bezout
increments and affine-coset reductions, then verify reversed orders on
the two patches for 2,280 ordered pairs of distinct normals in dimensions
two through six, each at five boundary heights with different residues
relative to the witness height. All 11,400 pair--height checks pass.
The exact points and scores are saved. The 200 tie-resolution fixtures
include 150 partial boundary levels and 50 complete ones.
It exhausts stated small boxes in dimensions two through four and uses
deterministic samples in dimensions five and six.
Six prime-realization fixtures, including equal weights and unused
coordinates, are checked by integer products and an independent
heap generation of the corresponding smooth-number prefixes.
As a check of the finite-ordering identification, all 24 planar entries
of \cite[Table 7]{VP} agree with the exact crossing count. Exhaustive
border extension with exact rational feasibility reproduces the eight
entries for lengths three through ten in their Table 8. All 1,023
accepted certificates are checked independently by an additive heap;
the non-initial finite ordering in their Remark 1 is rejected.
The separate prefix checker tests 230 integer-score certificates and
90 matrix-order prefixes with exact rational vertex enumeration and an
independent scalar heap. These include unused coordinates, all active
dimensions up to four, pure-axis collision checks and the rational
error budget for Theorem~\ref{thm:height}. They do not experimentally
verify the analytic prime-interval theorem.
The explicit constants and 352 scale-rounding cases in dimensions
two through twelve are checked with rational arithmetic.
These computations can falsify steps; they do not prove the uniform
theorem or its novelty.
