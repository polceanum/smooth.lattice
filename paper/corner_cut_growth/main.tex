\begin{abstract}
Let \(C_d(n)\) count the \(n\)-point subsets of the nonnegative integer
lattice that can be strictly separated from their complements by an affine
hyperplane. For every fixed \(d\geq2\), we prove
\[
 C_d(n)=\Theta_d\!\bigl(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}\bigr).
\]
The upper bound is due to Wagner. For the lower bound, a finite
sieve produces primitive lattice simplices in separated axis boxes.
Their primitive normal directions are distinguished at a prescribed
cardinality by two integer patches on opposite sides of a boundary
level. Summing over disjoint aspect ranges supplies the logarithmic
factor. This gives a matching lower bound in every fixed dimension,
including the three-dimensional order \(n^2(\log n)^2\).
The cumulative count is consequently of order \(n^d(\log n)^{d-1}\).
\end{abstract}

\section{The counting problem and its scope}
A nonempty finite set \(D\subset\Nzero^d\) is a \emph{corner cut} if
\[
 D=\{x\in\Nzero^d:w\cdot x<t\}
 \quad\text{for some }w\in\R_{>0}^d,\ t>0,
\]
with no lattice point on the cutting hyperplane. Write \(C_d(n)\) for the
number of such sets of cardinality \(n\), and put
\[
 A_d(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_d(j).
\]
We count sets, rather than the orders of their elements or the weights
representing them. Every cut is an order ideal for the coordinate order.
In particular, every coordinate of an element of an \(n\)-point cut
is at most \(n-1\), so these counts are finite.

These objects occur as the exponent sets of initial segments of smooth
numbers. For distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_d\), comparing the products
\(\prod_i p_i^{x_i}\) is equivalent to comparing
\(\sum_i x_i\log p_i\). An exponent prefix is consequently a corner cut.
Conversely, every corner cut has such a realization, even with all
primes above any prescribed bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:prime}).
Thus the full geometric class is exactly the class of exponent-prefix
sets as the ordered prime bases vary. This does not give a
prime-distribution estimate or a faster random-access algorithm for a
fixed prime set.

Corner cuts were introduced in connection with state polyhedra and
Gr\"obner bases by Onn and Sturmfels \cite{OS}. The planar order
\(C_2(n)=\Theta(n\log n)\) is due to Corteel, R\'emond, Schaeffer and
Thomas \cite{CRST}. For fixed \(d\), Wagner \cite{W02} proved
\[
 \Omega_d(n^{d-1}\log n)\leq C_d(n)
 \leq O_d(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}).
 \tag{1}\label{eq:baseline}
\]
His thesis \cite[Chapter 3]{W03} gives the lower and upper arguments
and suggests seeking greater completion multiplicity. We obtain the
lower bound in the same dimension: we count primitive normals and show
that two of them cannot define the same sufficiently large prefix.
No monotonicity assumption or inference from an average is used.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For each fixed integer \(d\geq2\),
\[
 C_d(n)=\Theta_d\!\bigl(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}\bigr).
\]
Here is one explicit lower bound. Put
\[
 K_d=4^{d+1},\qquad T_d=[64d^2(K_d+1)]^d,\qquad
 R_0(d)=4d^2(d+1),
\]
\[
 n_*(d)=T_d4^{R_0(d)},\qquad
 \rho_d=8^{-(d-1)}(100d)^{-d^2},
\]
and
\[
 \lambda_d=\frac{\rho_d}
 {2^d(4T_d)^{d-1}[4(d+1)]^{d-1}(d-1)!}.
\]
For every integer \(n\geq n_*(d)\),
\[
 C_d(n)\geq\lambda_d n^{d-1}(\log_4 n)^{d-1}.
 \tag{2}\label{eq:explicit}
\]
\end{theorem}
Summing gives \(A_d(n)=\Theta_d(n^d(\log n)^{d-1})\).
The pointwise conclusion matches \eqref{eq:baseline} and strengthens
its lower logarithmic exponent when \(d\geq3\).
This comparison is with the inspected baseline; the priority status
is discussed at the end of the paper.

\section{Primitive simplices in axis boxes}
Fix an ambient integer \(d\geq2\) and set \(\dd=1/(100d)\).
For integer side lengths \(L_1,\ldots,L_d\), put
\[
 m=\min_i L_i,\quad M=\max_i L_i,\quad Q=\prod_{i=1}^{d}L_i.
\]
Define \(B_i\subset\Nzero^d\) by taking the \(j\)th coordinate interval to be
\[
 [L_j,L_j+\floor{\dd L_j}]\quad(j=i),\qquad
 [0,\floor{\dd L_j}]\quad(j\ne i).
\]
A \(d\)-by-\((d-1)\) integer matrix is called primitive if the greatest
common divisor of its maximal minors is one.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:primitive}
Assume \(M\leq m^2\) and \(m\geq2^{8d^2}\).
At least a fraction \(8^{-(d-1)}\) of the ordered choices
\(p_i\in B_i\) have primitive difference matrix
\[
 V=[p_2-p_1\ \cdots\ p_d-p_1].
\]
\end{lemma}
The minor criterion and its equivalence with full column rank modulo
every prime are standard \cite[Propositions 3--4]{MW}.
The point requiring proof here is uniformity for these moving,
possibly anisotropic boxes.

\begin{proof}
Keep the same \(\dd\) throughout a dimension induction.
Let \(E_r\) be the event that the first \(r\) points, projected onto the
first \(r\) coordinates, have primitive difference matrix.
The event \(E_1\) is automatic, with empty minor equal to one.
At step \(2\leq r\leq d\), fix a primitive configuration of the previous
\(r-1\) projected points. Its difference matrix \(U\) has size
\((r-1)\)-by-\((r-2)\). Its vector \(a_0\) of signed maximal minors
is primitive, and \(U\) has rank \(r-2\) modulo every prime.

Let \(u\) be the first \(r-1\) coordinates of \(p_r-p_1\), and put
\(D=\det[U\ u]\). This determinant is nonzero for every allowed choice.
Indeed, the normalized row matrix of the previous \(r-1\) projected
points differs from the identity by at most \((r-1)\dd\) in maximum
row-sum norm. The normal of their affine span, normalized to height one,
has scaled coordinates within
\((r-1)\dd/(1-(r-1)\dd)\) of one and is positive.
The new projected point has every scaled coordinate at most \(\dd\);
it is strictly on the origin side of that affine span.
Also,
\[
 |D|\leq (r-1)!\,2^{r-1}M^{r-1}
       \leq2^{d^2}m^{2(d-1)}.                    \tag{3}\label{eq:D}
\]

Every coordinate interval contains more than \(\dd m\) integers.
For a prime \(\ell\), the equation \(D=0\pmod\ell\) is a nontrivial
linear equation in the new projected point: at least one coordinate
of \(a_0\) is nonzero modulo \(\ell\).
Its probability is at most \(1/\ell+a/m\), where \(a=100d\).

Suppose \(\ell\mid D\). Since \(U\) has full column rank modulo
\(\ell\), there is a unique vector \(\lambda\) with
\(u=U\lambda\pmod\ell\). Write \(z\) for the \(r\)th row of the old
columns and \(u_r\) for the \(r\)th coordinate of the new column.
The full \(r\)-by-\((r-1)\) matrix loses rank modulo \(\ell\) precisely if
\[
 u_r=z\lambda\pmod\ell.                         \tag{4}\label{eq:residue}
\]
Conditional on everything except the last coordinate of \(p_r\),
this is one residue class, of probability at most \(1/\ell+a/m\).
Thus the probability of failure at any particular prime is at most
\((1/\ell+a/m)^2\).

Put \(s=\floor{(\log_2 m)/2}\), \(P=2^s\).
The small-prime union bound gives
\begin{align}
 \Pr(\text{failure at a prime }\ell\leq P)
 &\leq\sum_{\substack{\ell\leq P\\\ell\ {\rm prime}}}
                 (1/\ell+a/m)^2 \notag\\
 &\leq\frac34+\frac{2a(s+1)}{2^{2s}}+
                      \frac{a^2}{2^{3s}}.       \tag{5}\label{eq:small}
\end{align}
Here \(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}j^{-2}<3/4\) follows by comparison with
\(1/4+\int_2^\infty x^{-2}\,dx\).
The cross term uses \(H_P\leq1+\log P\leq1+s\), and the constant
term uses at most \(P\) primes.

For large primes, first fix the new projected coordinates.
Every bad prime \(\ell>P\) divides the same nonzero integer \(D\).
By \eqref{eq:D}, their number is at most
\[
 \frac{\log_2|D|}{s}
 \leq4(d-1)+\frac{d^2+4(d-1)}{s}
 \leq b:=4d-3
 \quad\text{if }s\geq d^2+4(d-1).
\]
For each of these finitely many primes, \eqref{eq:residue} bounds the
conditional last-coordinate failure probability by \(2^{-s}+a2^{-2s}\).
Their total contribution is therefore at most \(b(2^{-s}+a2^{-2s})\).

Define
\[
 E_d(s)=\frac{2a(s+1)}{2^{2s}}+\frac{a^2}{2^{3s}}
                  +b(2^{-s}+a2^{-2s}).          \tag{6}\label{eq:error}
\]
This decreases for \(s\geq1\).
For every integer \(d\geq2\), the choice \(S_d=4d^2\) satisfies
\(S_d\geq d^2+4(d-1)\) and \(E_d(S_d)<2^{-10}<1/8\).
For an explicit verification valid in every dimension, use
\(a\leq2^{4d}\), \(b\leq2^{2d}\) and \(4d^2+1\leq2^{3d}\).
They give
\[
 E_d(4d^2)\leq
 2^{1+7d-8d^2}+2^{8d-12d^2}
       +2^{2d-4d^2}+2^{6d-8d^2}<2^{-10}.
\]
The four exponents decrease for \(d\geq2\), and at \(d=2\)
are \(-17,-32,-12,-20\).

Consequently the conditional probability of a nonprimitive extension
is at most \(3/4+E_d(s)\leq7/8\).
To justify the conditioning across steps, the events
\(E_1,\ldots,E_{r-1}\) depend only on previous projected coordinates.
After these are fixed, the old \(r\)th coordinates and the new point
retain their product distributions. Thus
\[
 \Pr(E_r\mid E_1,\ldots,E_{r-1})\geq1/8.
\]
Multiplication proves that \(\Pr(E_1\cap\cdots\cap E_d)\geq8^{-(d-1)}\).
This intersection implies \(E_d\), as required.
All bounds on large primes were finite before any limit was taken.
\end{proof}

\section{Primitive normals at a prescribed size}
For each primitive frame from Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive}, orient its
cofactor normal \(a\) positively and put \(H_a=a\cdot p_1\).
Let \(P\) have rows \(p_i\). Its column-scaled matrix differs from the
identity by a matrix of maximum row-sum norm at most \(d\dd=1/100\).
The determinant is positive and every eigenvalue lies within \(1/100\)
of one. Consequently
\[
 H_a=\det P,\qquad 2^{-d}Q\leq H_a\leq2^dQ.
 \tag{7}\label{eq:heights}
\]
The solution of \(p_i\cdot w=1\) satisfies
\(\lvert L_iw_i-1\rvert\leq d\dd/(1-d\dd)<1/10\).
Since \(w=a/H_a\), this gives
\[
 \frac9{10}<\frac{a_iL_i}{H_a}<\frac{11}{10}.
 \tag{8}\label{eq:normalbox}
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:normals}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive}, these frames give
at least \(2^{-d}\rho_dQ^{d-1}\) distinct primitive positive normals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Primitivity gives an integer \(z\) with \(a\cdot z=1\).
The matrix \([V\ z]\) is unimodular, so the columns of \(V\) form a
basis of the entire integer kernel of \(a\).
If two points of the same \(B_i\) lie in the selected affine plane,
their difference is \(Vc\), with \(c\in\Z^{d-1}\).
The submatrix in rows \(2,\ldots,d\), after row scaling by the \(L_i\),
differs from the identity by at most \((d-1)\dd\) in maximum row-sum
norm. Thus
\[
 \norm{c}\leq\frac{\dd}{1-(d-1)\dd}<1,
\]
so the two points agree. Each plane determines its selected simplex.
Each box has at least \(\dd^dQ\) points, so the number of distinct
planes is at least \(\rho_dQ^d\).
For any fixed primitive normal, the integer height in
\eqref{eq:heights} has at most \(2^dQ\) possibilities. Division proves
the claim.
\end{proof}

When one normal occurs at several heights, fix one witnessing simplex
for it, and use that simplex to define \(H_a\).
The prefix below depends only on \(a\), not on this choice of witness.

For a positive integer normal \(a\), order lattice points by
\((a\cdot x,x_1,\ldots,x_d)\), and let \(D_n(a)\) be the first \(n\).
Let \(h\) be the least integer for which
\[
 F_a(h):=\card{\{x\in\Nzero^d:a\cdot x\leq h\}}\geq n.
\]
The set \(D_n(a)\) is a corner cut, including when the score-\(h\)
level is only partly included. Indeed
\(h\leq(n-1)\min_i a_i\), by considering a minimum-weight axis.
Every point at score at most \(h\) lies in \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^d\).
For \(n>1\), the integer vector \(u_i=n^{d-i}\) represents lexicographic
order in that box, and \(u\cdot x\leq n^d-1\) there.
Taking \(w=n^d a+u\) preserves all these levels and
strictly orders the boundary level. Positivity keeps every higher
\(a\)-level above them. A threshold between consecutive integer scores
gives a strict representation. The case \(n=1\) is immediate.
We will use the weak separation property
\[
 b\cdot x\leq b\cdot y
 \quad(x\in D_n(b),\ y\notin D_n(b)).
 \tag{9}\label{eq:weak}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-size injectivity]\label{lem:inject}
Let \(a,b\) be primitive positive normals furnished by the same aspect
box. Put \(K=4^{d+1}\). If the boundary height \(h\) of \(D_n(a)\)
satisfies
\[
 h/H_a\geq16d^2(K+1),
 \tag{10}\label{eq:deep}
\]
then \(D_n(a)=D_n(b)\) implies \(a=b\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the selected frame \(V=[v_1\ \cdots\ v_{d-1}]\) for \(a\).
Since \(V\) is a basis of its integer kernel, an integer solution
of \(a\cdot z=1\) can be reduced modulo \(V\) to give
\[
 z=p_1/H_a+Vc,\qquad |c_j|\leq1/2.
 \tag{11}\label{eq:bezout}
\]
Each entry satisfies \(\lvert(v_j)_i\rvert<2L_i\), so
\(\lvert z_i\rvert\leq(d+1)L_i\leq2dL_i\).
Let \(y_i=(h-1)/(d a_i)\). Reduce \((h-1)z\) modulo \(V\) about
\(y\), obtaining an integer \(x_0\) such that
\[
 a\cdot x_0=h-1,\qquad x_0-y=V\beta,\qquad |\beta_j|\leq1/2.
\]
Both integer patches
\[
 \{x_0+Vk:k\in\Z^{d-1},\ |k_j|\leq K\},\qquad
 \{x_0+2z+Vk:k\in\Z^{d-1},\ |k_j|\leq K\}
 \tag{12}\label{eq:patches}
\]
are nonnegative. To see this coordinatewise, the displacement from
\(y_i\) is less than \((2Kd+5d)L_i\), while
\eqref{eq:normalbox} and \eqref{eq:deep} give
\[
 y_i/L_i>\frac{h-1}{2dH_a}>2Kd+5d.
\]
Their \(a\)-scores are respectively \(h-1\) and \(h+1\).
Thus the first patch is entirely inside \(D_n(a)\), the second
entirely outside; the score-\(h\) tie is irrelevant.

Assume the two prefixes agree, and put \(S=\sum_j|b\cdot v_j|\).
By \eqref{eq:weak}, the maximum \(b\)-score on the first patch cannot
exceed the minimum on the second. Hence
\[
 KS\leq b\cdot z
 \leq b\cdot p_1/H_a+S/2.
 \tag{13}\label{eq:patchineq}
\]
For two normals in the same aspect family,
\eqref{eq:heights} gives \(H_b/H_a\leq4^d\), and
\eqref{eq:normalbox} gives
\[
 \frac{b\cdot p_1}{H_a}
 <4^d\frac{11}{10}(1+d\dd)<2\cdot4^d.
\]
It follows that \((K-1/2)S<2\cdot4^d<K-1/2\).
But \(S\) is an integer, so \(S=0\). Thus \(b\) annihilates every
column of \(V\) and is parallel to \(a\). Primitivity and positivity
force \(a=b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:extents}
If \(n/Q\geq T_d\) and \(m\geq2^{8d^2}\), then
\eqref{eq:deep} holds. With \(G=(d!n/Q)^{1/d}\), the maximum
\(i\)th axis coordinate of \(D_n(a)\) lies strictly between
\(GL_i/2\) and \(2GL_i\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The disjoint half-open unit cubes based at the points counted by
\(F_a(t)\) contain the positive simplex of height \(t\), and are
contained in the simplex of height \(t+\sum_i a_i\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{t^d}{d!\prod_i a_i}\leq F_a(t)
 \leq\frac{(t+\sum_i a_i)^d}{d!\prod_i a_i}\qquad(t\geq0).
\]
Use \(F_a(h-1)<n\leq F_a(h)\) and \eqref{eq:normalbox} to get
\[
 \frac9{10}G-\frac{11d}{10m}
 <\frac h{H_a}<\frac{11}{10}G+\frac1{H_a}.
\]
Since \(m\geq10d\) and \(G\geq64d^2(K_d+1)\), this implies
\(4G/5<h/H_a<6G/5\), and also \eqref{eq:deep}.
If \(M_i\) is the axis maximum, all integer axis points below score
\(h\) are included and none above it are included. Thus
\(h/a_i-2<M_i\leq h/a_i\).
Combining these inequalities with \eqref{eq:normalbox} gives
\(GL_i/2<M_i<2GL_i\).
\end{proof}

\section{Summing aspect ranges}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The upper bound is \eqref{eq:baseline}. For the lower bound, take
\(R\) maximal with \(Q=4^R\leq n/T_d\). For every tuple
\[
 r_1+\cdots+r_d=R,\qquad r_i\geq\ceil{R/(d+1)},
\]
put \(L_i=4^{r_i}\). When \(n\geq n_*(d)\), these tuples satisfy
\(R\geq R_0(d)\), \(\min_iL_i\geq2^{8d^2}\), and
\(\max_iL_i\leq(\min_iL_i)^2\).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:normals}--\ref{lem:extents} therefore give
at least \(2^{-d}\rho_dQ^{d-1}\) different \(n\)-point cuts per tuple.

The value \(G\) in Lemma~\ref{lem:extents} is common to all tuples.
Two distinct tuples differ by a factor of at least four in one \(L_i\).
The open observable ranges \((GL_i/2,2GL_i)\) are disjoint for that
coordinate, so no cut is counted twice across tuples.

Put \(r=R-d\ceil{R/(d+1)}\). The number of tuples is
\[
 \binom{r+d-1}{d-1}
 \geq\frac{R^{d-1}}{[2(d+1)]^{d-1}(d-1)!},
\]
since \(r\geq R/(d+1)-d\geq R/[2(d+1)]\).
Also \(Q>n/(4T_d)\) and \(R\geq(\log_4n)/2\).
For the latter explicit bound, \(d^2\leq4^{d-1}\) gives
\(T_d\leq4^{2d^2+4d}\), and
\(R_0(d)\geq2d^2+4d+2\).
Multiplying the counts yields \eqref{eq:explicit}.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences and limitations}\label{sec:consequences}
There is an established geometric interpretation.
For a cut \(D\), put \(\alpha_D=\sum_{x\in D}x\).
For fixed size \(n\), these vectors are distinct and each is a vertex
of their convex hull. Indeed, weights strictly cutting off \(D\)
make \(w\cdot\alpha_D\) smaller than \(w\cdot\alpha_E\) for every
other \(n\)-point set \(E\): each replacement point has greater
weight than every removed point. Thus \(\alpha_D\) is uniquely exposed.
Conversely every vertex of the finite hull is one of its generators.
The vertex count of the corner-cut polytope is consequently \(C_d(n)\).
This correspondence is known \cite{OS,Muller,BMORW}; in particular,
M\"uller \cite[Lemma 2.6]{Muller} gives the exposed-vertex argument.
Our contribution is the
quantitative lower count, not the dictionary.
For generic experimental designs the same count describes their
corner-cut models \cite[Definition 7 and Theorem 2]{BMORW}.

\begin{proposition}[Realization by prime bases]\label{prop:prime}
For every nonempty finite corner cut \(D\subset\Nzero^d\) and every
\(B>0\), there are distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_d>B\) such that \(D\)
is the exponent set of the first \(\card{D}\) smooth numbers on these
ordered bases.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(D=\{x:w\cdot x<1\}\) strictly, and set
\[
 g=1-\max_{x\in D}w\cdot x>0,
 \qquad H=\max_{x\in D}\sum_i x_i.
\]
Relabel the coordinates temporarily so that \(w_1\leq\cdots\leq w_d\).
Choose \(T>0\) with \(e^{Tw_1}>\max(B,2)\) and
\(dH\log 2/T<g\).
Bertrand's postulate \cite{Erdos} supplies a prime in each interval
\[
 2^{i-1}e^{Tw_i}<p_i<2^i e^{Tw_i}.
\]
These intervals are disjoint, so the primes are distinct, including
when some weights agree. Put \(v_i=\log p_i/T\). Then
\(0<v_i-w_i<d\log 2/T\).
For \(x\in D\),
\(v\cdot x<w\cdot x+g\leq1\), whereas for every \(x\notin D\),
\(v\cdot x\geq w\cdot x>1\).
Consequently the products for \(x\in D\) are precisely those below
\(e^T\). Unique factorization makes their order strict. Undoing the
coordinate relabeling proves the statement.
\end{proof}

For fixed \(B\), let \(S_{d,B}(n)\) count distinct exponent sets of
length \(n\) obtained over ordered tuples of distinct primes greater
than \(B\). Count each set once, however many tuples realize it.
The proposition and the converse observation in Section 1 give
\[
 S_{d,B}(n)=C_d(n)=\Theta_d(n^{d-1}(\log n)^{d-1}),
\]\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n S_{d,B}(j)
   =\Theta_d\!\bigl(n^d(\log n)^{d-1}\bigr).
\]
The realization argument is an elementary use of a classical theorem;
it is not a separate novelty claim. Its role is to specify exactly
which smooth-number shapes the counting result describes. A single
fixed prime tuple gives one prefix of each length.

The pointwise count gives a matching asymptotic order in every fixed
dimension and for the established polytope and model dictionaries.
It supplies no asymptotic leading constant, no new enumeration algorithm,
no uniform claim as \(d\) grows with \(n\), and no fixed-prime unranking
complexity improvement. The prescribed-size step is Lemma~\ref{lem:inject};
the earlier cumulative construction alone did not give that conclusion.

\section{Prior results and reproducibility}
The following distinctions delimit the contribution being assessed.
M\"uller \cite[Sections 2--5]{Muller} studies the same polytopes,
including their planar vertex order and adjacency. Her enumeration
discussion \cite[Section 1, p.~324]{Muller} cites the planar result
and Wagner's bounds; her structural theorems do not supply the
bounded-cardinality count proved here.

The unrestricted density of rectangular primitive integer matrices is
known \cite{MRW,MW}. The later paper repairs a defect in the earlier
density proof. A centered-cube density alone does not assert the
uniform estimate for our moving boxes; Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive} gives
a direct finite proof. We claim no new minor criterion or unrestricted
density formula.

Chen, Feng, Liu and Wu \cite[Theorem 1]{CFLW} give finite probability
bounds for appending independent rows to a prescribed primitive matrix.
Their random entries use a common interval of length at least the
maximum prescribed entry. Our boxes have translated intervals of
different lengths, with their shortest length potentially much smaller
than the largest entry. That theorem is not a direct substitute for
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive}. Prime-by-prime rank analysis and finite
determinant cutoffs are established methods; our claimed contribution
is their use in the multiscale corner-cut count. The additional fixed-size
step is the two-patch comparison in Lemma~\ref{lem:inject}.

Finite-grid threshold encodings and planar counts are related
background \cite{GPZ,ZZ}. Their parameters are the ambient grid and,
in the latter case, intersections of two halfplanes. The argument here
additionally controls the number of included points, restricts the
positive intercepts, and sums disjoint aspect ranges.
A difference in definitions alone is not evidence of novelty.

The Hilbert zonotope refines state polyhedra and supports polynomial-time
computation of universal Gr\"obner bases in fixed dimension
\cite[Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.5]{BOT}.
Its count is an upper bound for a common refinement, not the lower
bounded-cardinality count proved here.
Likewise the lattice-subspace covering bounds of
Balko, Cibulka and Valtr \cite[Theorems 3--5]{BCV} concern another
counting parameter. Neither result is being recast as a new theorem.

Elizalde and Galv\'an \cite[Section 8.2]{EG} discuss higher-dimensional
pyramidal partitions and refer to the corner-cut growth literature.
For this draft, the relevant results in their 2025 journal article are
provisionally assumed unchanged from arXiv:2312.16353v1, as authorized
for the project. The final-version comparison is recorded separately
for later checking. This assumption does not establish priority.

Kalka and Kuhnt \cite[Section 2.2, p.~6]{KK} explicitly restate
Wagner's lower and upper orders for corner cuts, while treating a
numerical statistical fan. That passage does not supply a stronger
corner-cut counting bound or a proof of our prescribed-size lemma.

\paragraph{Current priority status.}
The proof establishes the stated inequalities and their strict
improvement over the inspected lower bound in \eqref{eq:baseline}.
The literature audit has not established an unrestricted claim of
priority or submission readiness. In particular, the complete 2002
journal text has not been inspected; the proofs were compared with
the author's full thesis chapter. The 2007 grid-encoding paper was
compared at the abstract level only. The detailed audit accompanies
the source at \path{results/research/corner_cut_growth_audit.json}.
This paragraph must be resolved before the draft is described as
submission-ready.

\paragraph{Exact checks.}
The standard-library Python checker
\path{scripts/run_corner_cut_growth_checks.py}
uses independent determinants, modular Gaussian elimination and exact
rational feasibility. It checks primitive-plane injectivity, the
conditional residue bound, a nonprimitive-projection negative control,
and a retained degenerate-arrangement fixture with 18 interior vertices
and 48 chambers. The prescribed-size checks construct integer Bezout
increments and affine-coset reductions, then verify reversed orders on
the two patches for 2,280 ordered pairs of distinct normals in dimensions
two through six, each at five boundary heights with different residues
relative to the witness height. All 11,400 pair--height checks pass.
The exact points and scores are saved. The 200 tie-resolution fixtures
include 150 partial boundary levels and 50 complete ones.
It exhausts stated small boxes in dimensions two through four and uses
deterministic samples in dimensions five and six.
Six prime-realization fixtures, including equal weights and unused
coordinates, are checked by integer products and an independent
heap generation of the corresponding smooth-number prefixes.
The explicit constants and 352 scale-rounding cases in dimensions
two through twelve are checked with rational arithmetic.
These computations can falsify steps; they do not prove the uniform
theorem or its novelty.

Run, from the repository root,
\begin{verbatim}
python3 -B scripts/run_corner_cut_growth_checks.py
\end{verbatim}
The report records fixtures, witnesses, the exact command and SHA-256
hashes of its sources in
\path{results/research/corner_cut_growth_checks.json}.
The proof above is independent of the computation.
